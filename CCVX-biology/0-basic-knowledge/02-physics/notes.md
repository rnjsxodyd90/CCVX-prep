# Basic Knowledge of Physics: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX syllabus, the Dutch CvTE syllabus and standard VWO biology, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question.

- **In Dutch VWO:** assumed knowledge (*nevenkennis*), listed in CvTE appendix 6 (*Kennis van natuurkunde en scheikunde*) and A8 spec 3. CCVX never tests it on its own, only inside a biology question.
- **Dutch source:** [CvTE syllabus biologie vwo 2027](https://www.examenblad.nl/2027/vwo/documenten/syllabus-biologie-vwo-2027) (version 2, July 2025), appendix 6 and A8 spec 3. The CvTE also prints these terms in italics next to the biology specifications that use them; a citation like *(CvTE M2.3 spec 3)* means specification 3 of M2.3, and its numbers don't match the CCVX numbers.

Dutch terms are in brackets. Binas tables are named, not numbered, because the numbers differ between editions.

## Mass, density and weight

- Mass (*massa*, m) is the amount of matter, in kilograms, and is the same everywhere. Weight (*gewicht*) is a force in newtons: the pull of gravity on a mass, F = m·g with g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹ on Earth, so a 70 kg person weighs about 690 N. *(CvTE A8 spec 3)*
- In daily language and in many biology texts "weight" means mass, as in body weight in kg or the dry weight (*drooggewicht*) of biomass: its mass after drying, because the water content varies ([B08](../../B-self-regulation/B08-regulation-of-ecosystems/README.md)).
- Density (*dichtheid*) ρ = m / V, in kg m⁻³, and 1 g cm⁻³ = 1 g mL⁻¹ = 1000 kg m⁻³. Water is about 1000 kg m⁻³ and densest at about 4 °C; ice is lighter (about 920 kg m⁻³), so it floats, a lake freezes from the top down, and fish survive in the water underneath.
- **Trap:** check whether "weight" means a mass in kg or a force in N; an astronaut on the Moon keeps the same mass but weighs about one-sixth as much.

## Solid, liquid and gas phase

- In a solid (*vaste stof*) the particles sit in fixed places, in a liquid (*vloeistof*) they are close together but move past each other, and in a gas (*gas*) they are far apart and move fast. Melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, sublimation and deposition: [chemistry 0-basic-knowledge/01](../../../CCVX-chemistry/0-basic-knowledge/01-pure-substances-and-mixtures/README.md).
- Evaporation (*verdamping*) takes heat from the surface: about 2.4 MJ per kg of water at skin temperature, so evaporating 100 g of sweat removes about 240 kJ from the body ([B04](../../B-self-regulation/B04-self-regulation-of-organisms/README.md)).
- Evaporation from leaves pulls water up through the xylem; CCVX calls this the "evaporation flow", usually the transpiration stream ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)). *(CvTE O1.6 spec 7)*
- O₂ and CO₂ dissolve in water and blood plasma, and a gas has to dissolve before it can diffuse through cells, which is why gas exchange surfaces are moist ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)).
- **Trap:** evaporation is not boiling: water evaporates from its surface at any temperature, which is why sweat cools the skin at well below 100 °C.

## Rates and frequencies

- A rate (*snelheid*) is a change per unit of time: speed in m s⁻¹, a reaction rate in mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹, a photosynthesis rate in mL O₂ per hour, and a diffusion rate (*diffusiesnelheid*) as an amount per area per time, in mol m⁻² s⁻¹ or g m⁻² s⁻¹. *(CvTE A8 spec 3)*
- Fick's law (*wet van Fick*), which CCVX calls the Fick principle: the diffusion rate is proportional to the exchange area and the concentration difference, and inversely proportional to the distance. Alveoli combine a very large area, a very thin wall and a steep gradient ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)). *(CvTE O1.3 spec 4)*
- Nerve impulses travel up to about 100 m s⁻¹ along thick myelinated fibres and about 1 m s⁻¹ along thin unmyelinated ones: over 1 m that is 10 ms against 1 s ([B04](../../B-self-regulation/B04-self-regulation-of-organisms/README.md)).
- A frequency (*frequentie*) f is the number of events per second, in hertz (Hz = s⁻¹), and the period is T = 1/f. A heart rate (*hartslagfrequentie*) of 70 beats per minute is 1.17 Hz, so one beat lasts 0.86 s; with a stroke volume of 70 mL the cardiac output is 70 × 70 mL = 4.9 L per minute ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)). *(CvTE O1.6 specs 1–2)*
- Humans hear about 20 Hz to 20 kHz, and a stronger stimulus raises the frequency of action potentials, not their size ([B07](../../B-self-regulation/B07-perception-by-organisms/README.md)).
- **Trap:** an allele frequency (*allelfrequentie*) is a proportion between 0 and 1, not a number per second ([F02](../../F-evolution/F02-speciation/README.md)).

## Types of energy

- Energy (*energie*) is measured in joules (J); food labels also use kcal, and 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ, so 2000 kcal is about 8.4 MJ. *(CvTE A8 spec 3)*
- Biology works with chemical energy (*chemische energie*) in glucose, fat and ATP, light energy (*lichtenergie*), kinetic energy (*kinetische energie*, the energy of movement) and heat (*warmte*). Electrical energy appears as the membrane potential of a nerve cell, about −70 mV at rest. *(CvTE M2.3 specs 3–4)*
- These forms are converted into each other: photosynthesis turns light energy into chemical energy, dissimilation turns the chemical energy of glucose into ATP and heat, and muscles turn ATP into kinetic energy and heat ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md), [B06](../../B-self-regulation/B06-movement-of-the-organism/README.md)).
- The total amount of energy stays the same, but every conversion releases part of it as heat. Along a food chain most energy leaves as heat from dissimilation or stays in uneaten parts and faeces, so only about 10% (roughly 5–20%) reaches the next trophic level ([B08](../../B-self-regulation/B08-regulation-of-ecosystems/README.md)).
- **Trap:** energy is converted, not used up; and ATP is an energy carrier, not an energy source, because it is made with energy from food or light.

## Radioactive isotopes, ionising radiation and half-life

- Isotopes (*isotopen*) are atoms of one element (same number of protons) with different numbers of neutrons: ¹²C, ¹³C and ¹⁴C all have 6 protons. Radioactive isotopes (*radioactieve isotopen*) such as ¹⁴C, ³²P and ³H have unstable nuclei that decay and give off radiation ([chemistry 0-basic-knowledge/03](../../../CCVX-chemistry/0-basic-knowledge/03-atomic-structure/README.md)). *(CvTE appendix 6; CvTE P4.4 spec 2)*
- Isotopes work as tracers: ¹⁴C in CO₂ showed the route of carbon through the Calvin cycle ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)), and the heavy but stable ¹⁵N showed that DNA replication is semi-conservative ([E01](../../E-reproduction/E01-dna-replication/README.md)).
- Ionising radiation (*ioniserende straling*), such as X-rays, gamma rays and the particles from radioactive decay, has enough energy to knock electrons out of molecules; it can break DNA and cause mutations and cancer. UV light is not ionising but also damages DNA, so both count as mutagenic radiation (*mutagene straling*, [F01](../../F-evolution/F01-selection/README.md)). *(CvTE M8.2 spec 2)*
- The half-life (*halveringstijd*) t½ is the time in which half of the radioactive nuclei decay, so after n half-lives a fraction (½)ⁿ is left. ¹⁴C has a half-life of about 5730 years: a bone with 25% of its original ¹⁴C is 2 half-lives, about 11 460 years, old. After 50 000 years less than 0.3% is left, so older rocks and fossils are dated with longer-lived isotopes such as potassium-40 (half-life about 1.25 billion years).
- Such dates calibrate the geological timescale (the Binas table of the geological timescale) and the branching points in cladograms ([F02](../../F-evolution/F02-speciation/README.md), [F04](../../F-evolution/F04-genesis-of-life/README.md)).
- **Trap:** decay is exponential, not linear: after two half-lives a quarter is left, not nothing.

## Electromagnetic spectrum

- The electromagnetic spectrum (*elektromagnetisch spectrum*) orders radiation by wavelength: gamma rays, X-rays, UV, visible light (*licht*), infrared, microwaves and radio waves. A shorter wavelength means a higher frequency (c = λ·f) and more energy per photon; values are in the Binas table of the electromagnetic spectrum. *(CvTE M2.3 spec 5)*
- Visible light runs from about 400 nm (violet) to about 700 nm (red); light of 500 nm has f = 3.00·10⁸ / 500·10⁻⁹ = 6.0·10¹⁴ Hz.
- Chlorophyll absorbs mainly blue and red light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green and why photosynthesis runs mainly on blue and red light ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md), [B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)).
- In the eye, rods and three kinds of cones respond to visible light only ([B07](../../B-self-regulation/B07-perception-by-organisms/README.md)); bees also see UV, which humans can't. UV in sunlight causes sunburn and DNA damage that can lead to skin cancer ([E01](../../E-reproduction/E01-dna-replication/README.md)).
- **Trap:** leaves look green because chlorophyll absorbs green light least, not because green light is the most useful for photosynthesis.

## Specific heat, heat capacity and heat of combustion

- Specific heat (*soortelijke warmte*) c is the energy needed to warm 1 kg of a substance by 1 K, so Q = m·c·ΔT. Water has a high value, 4.18·10³ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹: warming 1.0 kg of water from 20 °C to 37 °C takes 1.0 × 4.18·10³ × 17 = 71 kJ. Because cells, bodies and lakes are mostly water, their temperature changes slowly.
- Heat capacity (*warmtecapaciteit*) C = m·c is the energy needed to warm a whole object by 1 K: a 70 kg person, with c about 3.5 kJ kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, has C ≈ 245 kJ K⁻¹. A large animal also has less surface per volume, so it cools down more slowly than a small one ([A08](../../A-skills/A08-scientific-instruments/README.md)).
- Heat of combustion (*verbrandingswarmte*) is the energy released when 1 g, 1 kg or 1 mol of a substance burns completely. Glucose gives about 2.8·10³ kJ per mol, about 16 kJ per gram. Food labels count 17 kJ g⁻¹ (4 kcal) for carbohydrate and protein and 37 kJ g⁻¹ (9 kcal) for fat: more than twice as much, one reason why animals store energy as fat ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)).
- **Trap:** the "calories" on a food label are kilocalories; multiply by 4.184 to get kJ.

## Units the Dutch syllabus adds

- Pressure (*druk*) is force per area, in Pa (N m⁻²) or mmHg, with 1 mmHg ≈ 133.3 Pa: a blood pressure (*bloeddruk*) of 120/80 mmHg is 16.0/10.7 kPa. Blood pressure, filtration pressure and root pressure are in [B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md), osmotic pressure in [B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md). *(CvTE A8 spec 3; CvTE O1.6 specs 1–2)*
- Voltage (*spanning*) is in volts: a resting potential of about −70 mV is −0.070 V ([B04](../../B-self-regulation/B04-self-regulation-of-organisms/README.md)).
- Temperature (*temperatuur*) is in °C or K, with T in K = T in °C + 273 (more precisely 273.15): 37 °C is 310 K.
- Length, area and volume are in m, m², m³ and L, with 1 L = 1 dm³ = 10⁻³ m³. Prefixes run from nano (n, 10⁻⁹) via micro (µ, 10⁻⁶), milli (m, 10⁻³) and kilo (k, 10³) to mega (M, 10⁶) and giga (G, 10⁹): a red blood cell of about 7.5 µm is 7.5·10⁻⁶ m across.

## CCVX versus the Dutch syllabus

- **In both:** mass, density, speed and diffusion rate, energy in J and kcal (its forms are in M2.3 spec 3), radioactive isotopes, light and the electromagnetic spectrum. *(CvTE appendix 6; CvTE A8 spec 3)*
- **Dutch syllabus only:** length, area and volume (m, m², m³, L), pressure (Pa, N m⁻², mmHg), voltage (V), temperature (°C, K) and the prefixes from nano to giga, covered in the section above.
- **CCVX only:** weight, the three phases, frequency, ionising radiation, half-life, specific heat, heat capacity and heat of combustion are not in appendix 6. A few show up in Dutch biology specifications anyway: heart rate (*hartslagfrequentie*, O1.6 specs 1–2), evaporation (*verdamping*, O1.6 spec 7) and mutagenic radiation (*mutagene straling*, M8.2 spec 2).
