### bio-02-q01 | points 2 | outcome mass-weight | difficulty easy

#### Question

A biology textbook writes: "A male lion has a body weight of 190 kg."

Explain what is loose about this wording, in terms of the physical quantities mass and weight.

#### Answer

"Weight" here is used to mean mass.

#### Solution

"Weight" here is used to mean mass. Mass is the amount of matter in kilograms; weight is a force in newtons — the pull of gravity on that mass. The lion has a mass of 190 kg.
*Marking:* • sentence points out the mix-up (1) • mass in kg, weight in N as a force (1)

### bio-02-q03 | points 2 | outcome density | difficulty medium

#### Question

The density of ice is about 920 kg m⁻³, that of liquid water about 1000 kg m⁻³. Explain why a lake freezes from the surface down, and why that matters for the fish in it.

#### Answer

Ice is less dense than liquid water (920 < 1000 kg m⁻³), so ice floats and forms an insulating layer on top; the water underneath stays liquid, so fish survive the winter.

#### Solution

Ice is less dense than liquid water (920 < 1000 kg m⁻³), so ice floats and forms an insulating layer on top; the water underneath stays liquid, so fish survive the winter.

### bio-02-q06 | points 2 | outcome evaporation | difficulty medium

#### Question

During a marathon in warm weather, a runner loses 350 g of sweat per hour by evaporation. The evaporation of water takes about 2.4 MJ per kg.

Explain why evaporation cools the skin even though the skin is much cooler than 100 °C (why "evaporation is not boiling").

#### Answer

Evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature: the fastest water molecules escape, so the average kinetic energy (temperature) of the water left behind drops, drawing heat from the skin.

#### Solution

Evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature: the fastest water molecules escape, so the average kinetic energy (temperature) of the water left behind drops, drawing heat from the skin. Boiling is the formation of vapour bubbles throughout the liquid at the boiling point.
*Marking:* • evaporation at any temperature at the surface (1) • fastest molecules escape → cooling (1)

### bio-02-q14 | points 2 | outcome half-life | difficulty medium

#### Question

The radioactive isotope ¹⁴C has a half-life of 5730 years. In a human bone, the ¹⁴C content has fallen to 12.5% of the amount present when the person died.

Explain why ¹⁴C dating is of little use for a fossil that is about 200 000 years old.

#### Answer

200 000 years is about 35 half-lives; the remaining fraction is (½)³⁵ ≈ 3 × 10⁻¹¹, far too little ¹⁴C to measure reliably.

#### Solution

200 000 years is about 35 half-lives; the remaining fraction is (½)³⁵ ≈ 3 × 10⁻¹¹, far too little ¹⁴C to measure reliably. (Such old material is dated with longer-lived isotopes, for example potassium-40.)

### bio-02-q17 | points 2 | outcome light | difficulty medium

#### Question

Leaves look green. Explain what this shows about the wavelengths that chlorophyll absorbs, and what consequence that has for the light that drives photosynthesis.

#### Answer

Leaves look green because chlorophyll reflects (does not absorb) green light; it absorbs mainly blue and red light.

#### Solution

Leaves look green because chlorophyll reflects (does not absorb) green light; it absorbs mainly blue and red light. Photosynthesis therefore runs mainly on blue and red light, and barely on green.
*Marking:* • green reflected, not absorbed (1) • blue and red absorbed → drive photosynthesis (1)
