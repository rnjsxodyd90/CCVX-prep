# Basic Knowledge of Physics: self-check questions

Eight original self-check questions on the assumed physics for biology. The CCVX exam never tests this knowledge on its own, only inside a biological context — these questions check that the basics are reliable. Worked answers with marking points are at the end.

## Questions

### Question 1 — Mass and weight

A biology textbook writes: "A male lion has a body weight of 190 kg."

**1a.** Explain what is loose about this wording, in terms of the physical quantities mass and weight. (2 points)

**1b.** Calculate the weight (the force of gravity) of this lion on Earth. Use g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹. (2 points)

### Question 2 — Floating and sinking

**2a.** The density of ice is about 920 kg m⁻³, that of liquid water about 1000 kg m⁻³. Explain why a lake freezes from the surface down, and why that matters for the fish in it. (2 points)

**2b.** A small water organism has a density of 1010 kg m⁻³. Seawater has a density of 1025 kg m⁻³. Does this organism float or sink? Explain using the given values. (2 points)

### Question 3 — Cooling by sweat

During a marathon in warm weather, a runner loses 350 g of sweat per hour by evaporation. The evaporation of water takes about 2.4 MJ per kg.

**3a.** Calculate the amount of heat that evaporation of this sweat removes from the runner per hour. Give your answer in kJ. (2 points)

**3b.** Explain why evaporation cools the skin even though the skin is much cooler than 100 °C (why "evaporation is not boiling"). (2 points)

### Question 4 — Heart rate and cardiac output

At rest, an athlete has a heart rate of 48 beats per minute and a stroke volume of 105 mL.

**4a.** Give the heart rate in Hz and the duration of one heartbeat. (2 points)

**4b.** Calculate the cardiac output (the volume pumped per minute) at rest, in L per minute. (2 points)

**4c.** During exercise the cardiac output rises to 22 L per minute, while the heart rate is 176 beats per minute. Calculate the stroke volume now, in mL. (2 points)

### Question 5 — Energy in food

A muesli portion contains 45 g of carbohydrate. The heat of combustion of carbohydrate is 17 kJ per gram.

**5a.** Calculate the chemical energy of the carbohydrate in this portion, in kJ. (1 point)

**5b.** Convert your answer to kilocalories (1 kcal = 4.184 kJ), the "calories" used on food labels. (1 point)

**5c.** A student says: "ATP is the energy source of the cell." Correct this statement. (2 points)

### Question 6 — Carbon dating

The radioactive isotope ¹⁴C has a half-life of 5730 years. In a human bone, the ¹⁴C content has fallen to 12.5% of the amount present when the person died.

**6a.** Calculate the age of this bone. (3 points)

**6b.** Explain why ¹⁴C dating is of little use for a fossil that is about 200 000 years old. (2 points)

### Question 7 — Light and photosynthesis

**7a.** Calculate the frequency of light with a wavelength of 600 nm. The speed of light is c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. Give your answer in Hz, in the form 5.0 × 10¹⁴. (2 points)

**7b.** For light, a shorter wavelength means more energy per photon. Explain which light, blue (about 450 nm) or red (about 650 nm), delivers more energy per photon. (1 point)

**7c.** Leaves look green. Explain what this shows about the wavelengths that chlorophyll absorbs, and what consequence that has for the light that drives photosynthesis. (2 points)

### Question 8 — Warming a body

**8a.** A person has a mass of 60 kg. The average specific heat of the body is about 3.5 kJ kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. Calculate how much energy is needed to raise this person's body temperature by 1.0 K. (2 points)

**8b.** Water has a much higher specific heat (4.18 · 10³ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹) than, for example, sand (about 0.8 · 10³ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹). Explain, with this difference, why the sea warms up more slowly in spring than the beach. (2 points)

**8c.** A trap in terminology: give the energy that corresponds to the label value "250 kcal" in kJ. (1 point)

## Answers

**1a.** "Weight" here is used to mean mass. Mass is the amount of matter in kilograms; weight is a force in newtons — the pull of gravity on that mass. The lion has a mass of 190 kg.
*Marking:* • sentence points out the mix-up (1) • mass in kg, weight in N as a force (1)

**1b.** F = m·g = 190 × 9.81 = 1864 N ≈ 1.86 kN.
*Marking:* • m × g (1) • 1.9 × 10³ N (1)

**2a.** Ice is less dense than liquid water (920 < 1000 kg m⁻³), so ice floats and forms an insulating layer on top; the water underneath stays liquid, so fish survive the winter.
*Marking:* • lower density → floats (1) • water below stays liquid; insulation (1)

**2b.** The organism (1010 kg m⁻³) is less dense than seawater (1025 kg m⁻³), so it floats.
*Marking:* • comparison of the two values (1) • conclusion: floats (1)

**3a.** 350 g = 0.350 kg; Q = 0.350 × 2.4 MJ = 0.84 MJ = **840 kJ** per hour.
*Marking:* • kg conversion (1) • 840 kJ (1)

**3b.** Evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature: the fastest water molecules escape, so the average kinetic energy (temperature) of the water left behind drops, drawing heat from the skin. Boiling is the formation of vapour bubbles throughout the liquid at the boiling point.
*Marking:* • evaporation at any temperature at the surface (1) • fastest molecules escape → cooling (1)

**4a.** 48 min⁻¹ = 48/60 = 0.80 Hz; one beat takes T = 1/f = 1/0.80 = 1.25 s.
*Marking:* • 0.80 Hz (1) • 1.25 s (1)

**4b.** Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume = 48 × 105 mL = 5040 mL ≈ 5.0 L per minute.
*Marking:* • rate × stroke volume (1) • 5.0 L min⁻¹ (1)

**4c.** Stroke volume = 22 000 mL ÷ 176 = **125 mL**.
*Marking:* • output ÷ rate (1) • 125 mL (1)

**5a.** 45 g × 17 kJ g⁻¹ = **765 kJ**.
*Marking:* • 765 kJ (1)

**5b.** 765 ÷ 4.184 ≈ **183 kcal**.
*Marking:* • 183 kcal (1)

**5c.** ATP is not a source but an energy *carrier*: it is made from ADP and phosphate using the chemical energy of food (or light in photosynthesis) and delivers that energy where needed; the real sources are glucose, fat and the like.
*Marking:* • carrier, not source (1) • made from ADP using energy of food/light (1)

**6a.** 12.5% = 0.125 = (½)³, so 3 half-lives have passed: 3 × 5730 = **17 190 years** (about 17 000 years).
*Marking:* • 0.125 = (½)³ (1) • 3 half-lives (1) • 3 × 5730 = 17 190 years (1)

**6b.** 200 000 years is about 35 half-lives; the remaining fraction is (½)³⁵ ≈ 3 × 10⁻¹¹, far too little ¹⁴C to measure reliably. (Such old material is dated with longer-lived isotopes, for example potassium-40.)
*Marking:* • far more half-lives than measurable / almost no ¹⁴C left (1) • conclusion with reasoning (1)

**7a.** f = c / λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ (600 × 10⁻⁹) = **5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz**.
*Marking:* • c/λ (1) • 5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz (1)

**7b.** Blue light (450 nm) has the shorter wavelength, so more energy per photon.
*Marking:* • blue, with the shorter-wavelength argument (1)

**7c.** Leaves look green because chlorophyll reflects (does not absorb) green light; it absorbs mainly blue and red light. Photosynthesis therefore runs mainly on blue and red light, and barely on green.
*Marking:* • green reflected, not absorbed (1) • blue and red absorbed → drive photosynthesis (1)

**8a.** Q = m·c·ΔT = 60 × 3.5 × 1.0 = **210 kJ**.
*Marking:* • m·c·ΔT (1) • 210 kJ (1)

**8b.** With a higher specific heat, more energy is needed per kilogram for the same temperature rise: for the same sunlight input, the sea (mostly water) warms up more slowly than the (mostly sandy) beach.
*Marking:* • higher c → more energy needed per degree (1) • correct link to slower warming of the sea (1)

**8c.** 250 kcal × 4.184 = **1046 kJ ≈ 1.0 × 10³ kJ**.
*Marking:* • 1046 kJ (1)
