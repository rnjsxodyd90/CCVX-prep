# Basic Knowledge of Chemistry: self-check questions

Eight original self-check questions on the assumed chemistry for biology. The CCVX exam never tests this knowledge on its own, only inside a biological context — these questions check that the basics are reliable. Worked answers with marking points are at the end. You may use the BINAS table of atomic masses.

## Questions

### Question 1 — Blood glucose

A fasting blood glucose value is about 0.90 g per litre.

**1a.** Calculate the molar mass of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆. Use C = 12.01, H = 1.008, O = 16.00 u. (2 points)

**1b.** Calculate the same value in mmol per litre, to one decimal place. (2 points)

**1c.** A glucose molecule is a single molecule. Give the total number of atoms in one molecule of glucose and in one molecule of oxygen gas, O₂. (1 point)

### Question 2 — From amino acids to protein and back

Keratin in hair is a protein. One keratin chain contains 250 amino acids.

**2a.** Calculate the number of peptide bonds in this chain. (1 point)

**2b.** Give what happens chemically when a peptide bond forms (name of the reaction type and the small molecule that is released), and what happens when pepsin breaks the bond again (name of that reaction type). (2 points)

**2c.** The keratin chain is folded and held in shape by bridges. Name the weak bridges between backbone groups that a permanent wave lotion first breaks, and the strong covalent bridges between cysteine side groups that give keratin its toughness. (2 points)

### Question 3 — Stomach acid and blood

Gastric juice has a pH of about 1.5; blood plasma has a pH of 7.4.

**3a.** Calculate the ratio between [H⁺] in gastric juice and [H⁺] in blood plasma. Give your answer in the form 8 × 10⁵ (one digit × 10 to a power). (3 points)

**3b.** A student says: "An acid gives off OH⁻ in water; that is why its pH is low." Assess this claim. (2 points)

**3c.** Blood is buffered. Complete the equation in which the blood buffer takes up excess H⁺, and explain what the lungs then do with the product: HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ → … (2 points)

### Question 4 — Physiological saline

Infusion fluid for hospitals is physiological saline: a solution of 9.0 g NaCl per litre.

**4a.** Calculate the concentration of this solution in mol per litre. Use M(NaCl) = 58.44 g mol⁻¹. (2 points)

**4b.** In solution, every NaCl is present as two separate ions. Calculate the total concentration of dissolved particles, and explain why this particle concentration, rather than the mass concentration, matters for osmosis. (3 points)

### Question 5 — Identifying substances

A student tests four samples from a digestion experiment.

**5a.** Sample P contains starch. Give the reagent and the colour change that show this. (1 point)

**5b.** Sample Q contains glucose. Name a reagent and the colour change it shows with glucose when heated. (2 points)

**5c.** Sample R contains protein. Give the reagent and the colour change. (1 point)

**5d.** Sample S is bubbled through limewater to test whether yeast in the sample produced CO₂. Describe what is seen if CO₂ is present. (1 point)

### Question 6 — A glass of wine

A 250 mL glass of wine contains 12.0% alcohol (ethanol, C₂H₅OH) by volume. The density of ethanol is 0.79 g mL⁻¹.

**6a.** Calculate the volume of ethanol in the glass. (1 point)

**6b.** Calculate the mass of ethanol in the glass. (1 point)

**6c.** Calculate the chemical quantity (amount of substance) of ethanol in the glass. Use M(C₂H₅OH) = 46.07 g mol⁻¹. Give your answer in mol to two decimal places. (2 points)

### Question 7 — Oxidising and reducing agents

The net equation of aerobic dissimilation is: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O.

**7a.** Give which substance is the reducing agent and which is the oxidising agent in this reaction. (2 points)

**7b.** Explain, in terms of electrons, what happens to the oxidising agent. (2 points)

**7c.** In the light reaction of photosynthesis, water is split: electrons leave H₂O and O₂ is formed. Explain whether water acts here as oxidising or as reducing agent. (2 points)

### Question 8 — Mercury in fish

A predatory fish contains 0.40 ppm mercury (by mass).

**8a.** Explain what 0.40 ppm means, in mg per kg. (1 point)

**8b.** Calculate the mass of mercury in a 150 g portion of this fish, in µg. (2 points)

**8c.** Explain why the mercury concentration is much higher in this predatory fish than in the water plants at the base of the food chain, and give the name of this effect. (2 points)

## Answers

**1a.** M = 6 × 12.01 + 12 × 1.008 + 6 × 16.00 = 72.06 + 12.10 + 96.00 = 180.16 u, so 180.16 g mol⁻¹.
*Marking:* • correct use of atomic masses × atom counts (1) • answer 180.16 g mol⁻¹ (1)

**1b.** 0.90 g L⁻¹ ÷ 180.16 g mol⁻¹ = 5.0 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ = **5.0 mmol L⁻¹**.
*Marking:* • division by the molar mass (1) • answer 5.0 mmol L⁻¹, one decimal (1)

**1c.** Glucose: 6 + 12 + 6 = 24 atoms. O₂: 2 atoms.
*Marking:* • both correct (1)

**2a.** 250 − 1 = 249 peptide bonds.
*Marking:* • 249 (1)

**2b.** Formation: condensation, releasing water (H₂O). Breakdown by pepsin: hydrolysis, taking up water.
*Marking:* • condensation with release of H₂O (1) • hydrolysis (1)

**2c.** Weak bridges: hydrogen bonds (hydrogen bridges). Strong covalent bridges: disulfide bridges (sulfur bridges, S–S).
*Marking:* • hydrogen bonds (1) • disulfide bridges (1)

**3a.** Difference in pH: 7.4 − 1.5 = 5.9 units; each unit is a factor 10 in [H⁺], and the lower pH has the higher [H⁺]. Ratio = 10⁵·⁹ ≈ **8 × 10⁵** (gastric juice has about eight hundred thousand times more H⁺).
*Marking:* • factor 10 per pH unit (1) • exponent 5.9 (1) • correct direction of the ratio (1)

**3b.** The claim is wrong. An acid gives off H⁺ (protons) in water; it is the high [H⁺] that makes the pH low. OH⁻ is given off by (or: is in excess with) bases, whose pH is high.
*Marking:* • claim rejected (1) • acids give off H⁺; OH⁻ concerns bases (1)

**3c.** HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂ (via carbonic acid). The CO₂ diffuses into the alveoli and is breathed out, so excess acid leaves the body.
*Marking:* • products H₂O and CO₂ (1) • CO₂ exhaled by the lungs (1)

**4a.** c = 9.0 g L⁻¹ ÷ 58.44 g mol⁻¹ = 0.154 mol L⁻¹ (0.15 mol L⁻¹).
*Marking:* • division by 58.44 (1) • answer 0.154 mol L⁻¹ (1)

**4b.** Particle concentration = 2 × 0.154 = 0.308 mol L⁻¹. Osmosis depends on the number of dissolved particles (ions and molecules), not on their mass: water flows towards the side with the higher particle concentration, so it is the ion/particle concentration that must match that of blood plasma.
*Marking:* • ×2 for two ions (1) • 0.308 mol L⁻¹ (1) • osmosis depends on number of particles, not mass (1)

**5a.** Iodine solution: yellow-brown turns blue-black.
*Marking:* • iodine + blue-black (1)

**5b.** Benedict's reagent (or Fehling's): blue turns orange-red (brick-red) when heated.
*Marking:* • Benedict's/Fehling's (1) • blue to orange-red (1)

**5c.** Biuret reagent: blue turns violet (purple).
*Marking:* • biuret + violet (1)

**5d.** The limewater turns milky (cloudy, white precipitate).
*Marking:* • turns milky/cloudy (1)

**6a.** V = 12.0% × 250 mL = 30 mL ethanol.
*Marking:* • 30 mL (1)

**6b.** m = 30 mL × 0.79 g mL⁻¹ = 23.7 g.
*Marking:* • 23.7 g (1)

**6c.** n = 23.7 g ÷ 46.07 g mol⁻¹ = **0.51 mol**.
*Marking:* • division by the molar mass (1) • 0.51 mol, two decimals (1)

**7a.** Reducing agent: glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆). Oxidising agent: oxygen (O₂).
*Marking:* • glucose (1) • oxygen (1)

**7b.** The oxidising agent takes up electrons and is itself reduced: O₂ takes up electrons (and H⁺) and becomes H₂O.
*Marking:* • takes up electrons / is reduced (1) • becomes H₂O (1)

**7c.** Water gives off electrons (it is oxidised to O₂), so water is the reducing agent.
*Marking:* • electrons leave water / water is oxidised (1) • conclusion: reducing agent (1)

**8a.** 0.40 ppm = 0.40 mg per kg (a mass fraction of 0.4 × 10⁻⁶).
*Marking:* • 0.40 mg per kg (1)

**8b.** 0.150 kg × 0.40 mg kg⁻¹ = 0.060 mg = **60 µg**.
*Marking:* • 0.06 mg (1) • conversion to 60 µg (1)

**8c.** Mercury is not (or barely) broken down or excreted: each organism keeps what it eats, so the concentration rises at every step of the food chain and is highest in the top predator. This is called (bio)accumulation (or biomagnification).
*Marking:* • not broken down, retained (1) • rises per trophic level; name (bio)accumulation (1)

*Total: 40 points. Self-test pace: about 2 points per minute.*
