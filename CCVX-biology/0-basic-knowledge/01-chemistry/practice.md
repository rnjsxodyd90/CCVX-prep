### bio-01-q01 | points 2 | outcome mol-mass | difficulty easy

#### Question

A fasting blood glucose value is about 0.90 g per litre.

Calculate the molar mass of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆. Use C = 12.01, H = 1.008, O = 16.00 u.

#### Answer

M = 6 × 12.01 + 12 × 1.008 + 6 × 16.00 = 72.06 + 12.10 + 96.00 = 180.16 u, so 180.16 g mol⁻¹.

#### Solution

M = 6 × 12.01 + 12 × 1.008 + 6 × 16.00 = 72.06 + 12.10 + 96.00 = 180.16 u, so 180.16 g mol⁻¹.
*Marking:* • correct use of atomic masses × atom counts (1) • answer 180.16 g mol⁻¹ (1)

### bio-01-q07 | points 3 | outcome ph | difficulty medium

#### Question

Gastric juice has a pH of about 1.5; blood plasma has a pH of 7.4.

Calculate the ratio between [H⁺] in gastric juice and [H⁺] in blood plasma. Give your answer in the form 8 × 10⁵ (one digit × 10 to a power).

#### Answer

Difference in pH: 7.4 − 1.5 = 5.9 units; each unit is a factor 10 in [H⁺], and the lower pH has the higher [H⁺]. Ratio = 10⁵·⁹ ≈ **8 × 10⁵** (gastric juice has about eight hundred thousand times more H⁺).

#### Solution

Difference in pH: 7.4 − 1.5 = 5.9 units; each unit is a factor 10 in [H⁺], and the lower pH has the higher [H⁺]. Ratio = 10⁵·⁹ ≈ **8 × 10⁵** (gastric juice has about eight hundred thousand times more H⁺).
*Marking:* • factor 10 per pH unit (1) • exponent 5.9 (1) • correct direction of the ratio (1)

### bio-01-q08 | points 2 | outcome acids-bases | difficulty easy

#### Question

Gastric juice has a pH of about 1.5; blood plasma has a pH of 7.4.

A student says: "An acid gives off OH⁻ in water; that is why its pH is low." Assess this claim.

#### Answer

The claim is wrong.

#### Solution

The claim is wrong. An acid gives off H⁺ (protons) in water; it is the high [H⁺] that makes the pH low. OH⁻ is given off by (or: is in excess with) bases, whose pH is high.
*Marking:* • claim rejected (1) • acids give off H⁺; OH⁻ concerns bases (1)

### bio-01-q11 | points 3 | outcome osmotic-particles | difficulty medium

#### Question

Infusion fluid for hospitals is physiological saline: a solution of 9.0 g NaCl per litre.

In solution, every NaCl is present as two separate ions. Calculate the total concentration of dissolved particles, and explain why this particle concentration, rather than the mass concentration, matters for osmosis.

#### Answer

Particle concentration = 2 × 0.154 = 0.308 mol L⁻¹.

#### Solution

Particle concentration = 2 × 0.154 = 0.308 mol L⁻¹. Osmosis depends on the number of dissolved particles (ions and molecules), not on their mass: water flows towards the side with the higher particle concentration, so it is the ion/particle concentration that must match that of blood plasma.
*Marking:* • ×2 for two ions (1) • 0.308 mol L⁻¹ (1) • osmosis depends on number of particles, not mass (1)

### bio-01-q24 | points 2 | outcome ppm | difficulty medium

#### Question

A predatory fish contains 0.40 ppm mercury (by mass).

Explain why the mercury concentration is much higher in this predatory fish than in the water plants at the base of the food chain, and give the name of this effect.

#### Answer

Mercury is not (or barely) broken down or excreted: each organism keeps what it eats, so the concentration rises at every step of the food chain and is highest in the top predator. This is called (bio)accumulation (or biomagnification).

#### Solution

Mercury is not (or barely) broken down or excreted: each organism keeps what it eats, so the concentration rises at every step of the food chain and is highest in the top predator. This is called (bio)accumulation (or biomagnification).
*Marking:* • not broken down, retained (1) • rises per trophic level; name (bio)accumulation (1)
