# Basic Knowledge of Chemistry: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX syllabus, the Dutch CvTE syllabus and standard VWO biology, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question.

- **In Dutch VWO:** assumed knowledge (*nevenkennis*), listed in CvTE appendix 6 (*Kennis van natuurkunde en scheikunde*) and A8 spec 3. CCVX never tests it on its own, only inside a biology question.
- **Dutch source:** [CvTE syllabus biologie vwo 2027](https://www.examenblad.nl/2027/vwo/documenten/syllabus-biologie-vwo-2027) (version 2, July 2025), appendix 6 and A8 spec 3. The CvTE also prints these terms in italics next to the biology specifications that use them; a citation like *(CvTE M2.3 spec 10)* means specification 10 of M2.3, and its numbers don't match the CCVX numbers.

Dutch terms are in brackets. Binas tables are named, not numbered, because the numbers differ between editions.

## Particles, atomic mass and formulas

- An atom (*atoom*) is the smallest particle of an element; a molecule (*molecuul*) is a group of atoms held together by covalent bonds, such as O₂, H₂O and C₆H₁₂O₆. *(CvTE appendix 6; CvTE M1.1 specs 1–2)*
- An ion (*ion*) is an atom or group of atoms with extra or missing electrons, so it is charged: Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻. Differences in ion concentration across membranes drive nerve impulses ([B04](../../B-self-regulation/B04-self-regulation-of-organisms/README.md)).
- A proton (*proton*) is the positive particle in the nucleus; in biology "proton" usually means H⁺. Mitochondria and chloroplasts pump H⁺ across a membrane, and the H⁺ flowing back drives ATP synthase ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 3)*
- Atomic mass (*atoommassa*) is the mass of one atom in u; a molecular mass is the sum of its atomic masses. Glucose is 6 × 12.011 + 12 × 1.008 + 6 × 15.999 = 180.16 u, so 1 mol glucose has a mass of 180.16 g ([chemistry C02](../../../CCVX-chemistry/C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C02-chemical-calculations/README.md)).
- A molecular formula (*molecuulformule*) counts the atoms; a structural formula (*structuurformule*) shows how they are bonded. More in [chemistry 0-basic-knowledge/02](../../../CCVX-chemistry/0-basic-knowledge/02-elements-and-compounds/README.md) and [chemistry 0-basic-knowledge/03](../../../CCVX-chemistry/0-basic-knowledge/03-atomic-structure/README.md). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 10)*
- **Trap:** a molecular formula doesn't identify a substance: glucose, fructose and galactose are all C₆H₁₂O₆ and differ only in their structural formulas.

## Reagents, reaction equations, equilibrium and catalysts

- A reagent (*reagens*) shows that a substance is present by a visible change: limewater turns milky with CO₂, iodine solution turns blue-black with starch, Benedict's or Fehling's reagent turns from blue to orange-red with glucose when heated, and biuret reagent turns violet with protein. More in [chemistry 0-basic-knowledge/04](../../../CCVX-chemistry/0-basic-knowledge/04-reactions/README.md).
- A reaction equation (*reactievergelijking*) has the same number of each kind of atom on both sides. Photosynthesis: 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂; aerobic dissimilation is the reverse overall: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 10)*
- Equilibrium reactions (*evenwichtsreacties*) run both ways at once (⇌); at equilibrium both directions go equally fast, so the concentrations stay constant. In blood, CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ (via carbonic acid); in the lungs CO₂ diffuses out, the equilibrium shifts to the left, and HCO₃⁻ turns back into CO₂ ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 2)*
- A catalyst (*katalysator*) speeds up a reaction by lowering the activation energy, isn't used up, and doesn't shift an equilibrium. Enzymes are biological catalysts: catalase speeds up 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂, and carbonic anhydrase the CO₂ reaction above ([chemistry C04](../../../CCVX-chemistry/C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C04-reaction-kinetics/README.md), [chemistry C10](../../../CCVX-chemistry/C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C10-activation-energy/README.md)). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 2)*
- **Trap:** at equilibrium the reactions don't stop, and the concentrations on both sides are constant, not equal.

## Water-soluble or fat-soluble, saturation

- Hydrophilic (*hydrofiel*) substances are polar or charged and form hydrogen bonds or ion–dipole bonds with water, so they are water-soluble (*wateroplosbaar*): glucose, amino acids, urea, Na⁺, Cl⁻. Hydrophobic (*hydrofoob*) substances are apolar and fat-soluble (*vetoplosbaar*): fats, cholesterol, steroid hormones, vitamins A, D, E and K. The CvTE adds polar (*polair*) and apolar (*apolair*). *(CvTE M2.2 spec 2)*
- A phospholipid has a hydrophilic head and two hydrophobic tails, so phospholipids form a bilayer: O₂, CO₂ and steroid hormones diffuse straight through it, while ions and glucose need transport proteins ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)). Bile salts emulsify fat so lipase can reach it ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)). Bonds and solubility: [chemistry B03](../../../CCVX-chemistry/B-substances-and-materials/B03-bonds-and-properties/README.md).
- Saturation (*verzadiging*): CCVX doesn't say which meaning it intends, but next to solubility it most likely means a saturated solution, which holds the maximum amount that can dissolve at that temperature. Gases dissolve less in warm water, so warm water holds less O₂ and fish can run short of oxygen in a hot summer.
- Biology uses the word in two more ways: the oxygen saturation of haemoglobin, shown in its saturation curve (*verzadigingscurve*, [B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)), and saturated fatty acids (below). *(CvTE O1.3 spec 4)*
- **Trap:** size alone doesn't decide what crosses the lipid bilayer: apolar O₂ and CO₂ pass it freely, but a small ion such as Na⁺ can't, because its charge makes it hydrophilic.

## Acids, bases, pH and indicators

- An acid (*zuur*) gives off H⁺ in water and a base (*base*) takes up H⁺: gastric juice contains hydrochloric acid, and ammonia is a base, NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ ([chemistry 0-basic-knowledge/05](../../../CCVX-chemistry/0-basic-knowledge/05-acids-and-bases/README.md)).
- pH = −log [H⁺]: below 7 is acidic, 7 neutral (at 25 °C), above 7 basic, and each pH unit is a factor 10. Gastric juice of about pH 2 has 10⁵ times more H⁺ than a solution of pH 7; blood of pH 7.4 has [H⁺] = 10<sup>−7.4</sup> = 4.0·10⁻⁸ mol L⁻¹ ([chemistry C02](../../../CCVX-chemistry/C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C02-chemical-calculations/README.md)). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 12)*
- A buffer keeps the pH nearly constant: in blood HCO₃⁻ takes up extra H⁺ (HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂), and the lungs breathe out the CO₂ ([B04](../../B-self-regulation/B04-self-regulation-of-organisms/README.md), [chemistry C01](../../../CCVX-chemistry/C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/README.md)).
- An indicator (*indicator*) is a dye whose colour depends on pH. Bromothymol blue is yellow in acid and blue in basic solution: CO₂ makes water acidic, so a water plant that takes up CO₂ in the light turns it from yellow back to blue. Phenolphthalein, pink in basic solution, turns colourless when lipase releases fatty acids from the fat in milk. *(CvTE M2.3 spec 11)*
- **Trap:** pH is logarithmic and runs the other way: pH 4 has ten times more H⁺ than pH 5, not a little more.

## Oxidising and reducing agents

- An oxidising agent (*oxidator*) takes up electrons and is reduced; a reducing agent (*reductor*) gives off electrons and is oxidised ([chemistry C01](../../../CCVX-chemistry/C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/README.md)). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 9)*
- In aerobic dissimilation glucose is the reducing agent: its electrons pass via NAD⁺ (which becomes NADH) to O₂, the final oxidising agent, which becomes H₂O. In photosynthesis light energy takes electrons from H₂O (oxidised to O₂), and NADPH uses them to reduce CO₂ ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)).
- **Trap:** the oxidising agent is itself reduced: O₂ oxidises glucose and is reduced to water.

## Amino acids, proteins, and hydrogen and sulfur bridges

- An amino acid (*aminozuur*) has an amino group (–NH₂), a carboxyl group (–COOH) and a side chain that differs between the 20 amino acids in proteins; the Binas table of amino acids gives their structural formulas ([chemistry G01](../../../CCVX-chemistry/G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G01-chemistry-of-life/README.md)).
- CCVX's "peptide binding" is the peptide bond (*peptidebinding*): the –COOH of one amino acid and the –NH₂ of the next form –CO–NH– and release one H₂O (condensation). Two glycines: 2 C₂H₅NO₂ → C₄H₈N₂O₃ + H₂O. A chain of 100 amino acids has 99 peptide bonds; pepsin and trypsin break them again by hydrolysis ([chemistry C01](../../../CCVX-chemistry/C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/README.md)). *(CvTE M1.2 spec 3)*
- A protein (*eiwit*) is one or more of these chains, folded into a specific shape that the amino acid sequence decides ([B01](../../B-self-regulation/B01-protein-synthesis/README.md)).
- Hydrogen bridges, usually called hydrogen bonds (*waterstofbruggen*), are weak attractions between an H on O or N and another O or N. They hold the DNA strands together (two per A–T pair, three per G–C pair), keep a protein's helices and sheets in shape, and make water cohesive in the xylem ([chemistry B03](../../../CCVX-chemistry/B-substances-and-materials/B03-bonds-and-properties/README.md)). *(CvTE M1.1 specs 1–2)*
- Sulfur bridges, usually disulfide bridges (*zwavelbruggen*), are covalent S–S bonds between the side chains of two cysteines. They are much stronger and fix the tertiary and quaternary structure, as between the two chains of insulin. *(CvTE M1.2 spec 3)*
- **Trap:** heat or an extreme pH denatures a protein by breaking hydrogen bonds, but it leaves the peptide bonds, and so the primary structure, intact.

## Fats and carbohydrates

- A fat (*vet*) is one glycerol (*glycerol*, C₃H₈O₃) joined to three fatty acids (*vetzuren*) by condensation: C₃H₈O₃ + 3 C₁₇H₃₅COOH → C₅₇H₁₁₀O₆ + 3 H₂O; lipase hydrolyses it back. *(CvTE M2.3 specs 6–7)*
- A saturated fatty acid (*verzadigd vetzuur*) has only C–C single bonds, such as stearic acid C₁₇H₃₅COOH; an unsaturated one (*onverzadigd vetzuur*) has one or more C=C double bonds, such as oleic acid C₁₇H₃₃COOH. A cis double bond kinks the chain, so the molecules pack less tightly: oils rich in unsaturated fatty acids are liquid, butter is solid.
- Monosaccharides (*monosachariden*) are single sugar units: glucose, fructose and galactose (C₆H₁₂O₆), ribose and deoxyribose (five C atoms). Disaccharides (*disachariden*) are two units joined by condensation: maltose (glucose + glucose), sucrose (glucose + fructose), lactose (glucose + galactose), as in 2 C₆H₁₂O₆ → C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O. *(CvTE M2.3 specs 6–7)*
- Polysaccharides (*polysachariden*) are long chains of glucose: starch stores energy in plants, glycogen in liver and muscle, and cellulose builds plant cell walls. Amylase hydrolyses starch to maltose ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md), [chemistry G01](../../../CCVX-chemistry/G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G01-chemistry-of-life/README.md)).
- **Trap:** starch, glycogen and cellulose are all glucose, linked in different ways; humans have no enzyme that digests cellulose, so it passes through as dietary fibre.

## Methane, ethanol and the named substances

- Methane (*methaan*, CH₄) is made by archaea (methanogens) in anaerobic places such as marshes, rice fields, the rumen of cattle and biogas installations. It is a greenhouse gas, and it burns as CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md), [B08](../../B-self-regulation/B08-regulation-of-ecosystems/README.md)). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 8; CvTE P1.2 spec 3)*
- Alcohol here is ethanol (*alcohol*, C₂H₅OH), made by yeast in alcoholic fermentation (*alcoholische gisting*): C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2 C₂H₅OH + 2 CO₂, which yields only 2 ATP per glucose; the CO₂ makes bread dough rise. *(CvTE M2.3 spec 8)*

| Substance | Formula | Where it matters in biology |
|---|---|---|
| ammonia (*ammoniak*) | NH₃ | made when amino acids are broken down; the liver turns it into urea |
| calcium | Ca, in the body as Ca²⁺ | bones and teeth; muscle contraction and blood clotting |
| iron (*ijzer*) | Fe, as Fe²⁺ in haem | binds O₂ in haemoglobin |
| potassium (*kalium*) | K, as K⁺ | high inside cells; Na⁺/K⁺ pump and resting potential |
| carbon dioxide (*koolstofdioxide*) | CO₂ | product of dissimilation, raw material of photosynthesis |
| magnesium | Mg, as Mg²⁺ | at the centre of the chlorophyll molecule |
| sodium chloride (*natriumchloride*) | NaCl (Na⁺ and Cl⁻) | table salt; 9 g per litre in physiological saline |
| nitrogen (*stikstof*) | N₂ as a gas | about 78% of air; fixed by bacteria, for example in root nodules |
| water | H₂O | solvent; raw material of photosynthesis, product of dissimilation |

- The CvTE list also names phosphate (PO₄³⁻: in DNA, ATP and fertiliser), nitrate (NO₃⁻) and nitrite (NO₂⁻) from the nitrogen cycle, glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) and oxygen gas (O₂) ([B08](../../B-self-regulation/B08-regulation-of-ecosystems/README.md)). *(CvTE M2.3 spec 10)*
- **Trap:** "nitrogen" in air means N₂ gas, which plants can't use; they take up nitrogen as nitrate or ammonium.

## Quantities and units

- Concentration (*concentratie*) is an amount per volume of solution, in mol L⁻¹ (n/V) or in g L⁻¹ (m/V); convert with the molar mass: g L⁻¹ = mol L⁻¹ × M ([chemistry C02](../../../CCVX-chemistry/C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C02-chemical-calculations/README.md)). *(CvTE A8 spec 3)*
- Physiological saline, 9.0 g NaCl per litre (often written as 0.9%), is 9.0 / 58.44 = 0.154 mol L⁻¹ NaCl; each NaCl gives two ions, so 0.308 mol L⁻¹ of particles, which is what counts for osmosis ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)). A normal fasting blood glucose of about 5 mmol L⁻¹ is 5.0·10⁻³ × 180.16 = 0.90 g L⁻¹ ([B04](../../B-self-regulation/B04-self-regulation-of-organisms/README.md)).
- Mass percentage (*massapercentage*) = m(part) / m(total) × 100%, and volume percentage (*volumepercentage*) = V(part) / V(total) × 100%. Dry air is about 21% O₂ by volume; a 330 mL bottle of beer with 5.0% alcohol by volume holds 16.5 mL ethanol, which is 13 g at a density of 0.789 g mL⁻¹.
- ppm (parts per million) is a fraction × 10⁶: by mass 1 mg per kg, by volume 1 mL per m³. CO₂ in air is now over 400 ppm, more than 0.04%. Fish with 0.5 ppm mercury contain 0.5 mg per kg, so a 200 g portion holds 0.10 mg; such substances accumulate along food chains ([D05](../../D-interaction/D05-interaction-in-ecosystems/README.md)).
- **Trap:** a percentage refers to the whole solution: 5 g glucose in 95 g water is a 5.0% solution (5/100), not 5.26% (5/95).

## CCVX versus the Dutch syllabus

- **In both:** atom, molecule, ion and proton; molecular and structural formulas; reaction equations, equilibrium reactions and catalysts; oxidising and reducing agents; hydrophilic and hydrophobic, water- and fat-soluble; pH and indicators; peptide bonds, sulfur bridges and hydrogen bridges; methane, ammonia, carbon dioxide, sodium chloride, nitrogen gas and water; calcium, iron and potassium; concentration in mol L⁻¹ and g L⁻¹, mass and volume percentage, ppm. Amino acids, fats, glycerol, fatty acids, carbohydrates and alcohol are Dutch biology content in M2.3 (specs 6–8) rather than appendix 6. *(CvTE appendix 6; CvTE A8 spec 3)*
- **Dutch syllabus only:** the electron (covered under ions and redox); light, the electromagnetic spectrum and isotopes (covered in [02-physics](../02-physics/README.md)); polar and apolar; the elements chlorine, phosphorus, carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and sodium; phosphate, glucose, nitrate, nitrite and oxygen gas (named briefly above).
- **CCVX only:** reagents, atomic mass, acids and bases (the CvTE lists only pH and indicator), saturation and saturated or unsaturated fatty acids (the CvTE only has the haemoglobin saturation curve, O1.3 spec 4), magnesium, and the name ethanol (the CvTE says *alcohol*).
