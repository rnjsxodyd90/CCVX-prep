### bio-d04-q01 | points 2 | outcome D4.1.1 | difficulty easy

#### Question

A biology textbook states: "In humans, sexuality has two functions." Explain this statement by describing both functions.

#### Answer

Reproduction: sexual intercourse brings sperm cells to the egg in the oviduct. Maintaining relationships: intimacy, touch and sex strengthen the bond between partners (partly via bonding hormones such as oxytocin).

#### Solution

Reproduction: sexual intercourse brings sperm cells into the vagina; they swim to the oviduct where fertilization can take place. Maintaining relationships: intimacy, touch and sex give pleasure and strengthen the pair bond; bonding hormones such as oxytocin are released, and a stable bond helps raise children who need care for many years.
*Marking:* • reproductive function (1) • relationship/bonding function (1)

### bio-d04-q02 | points 2 | outcome D4.1.3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

In most mammals a female mates only in the fertile period; human couples have sex throughout the menstrual cycle, during pregnancy and while using contraception. Women also show no outward sign of ovulation.

Argue on the basis of these facts that in humans sexual behaviour is largely uncoupled from reproductive behaviour.

#### Answer

Human sex occurs almost entirely at moments when fertilization is impossible or unwanted — outside the fertile window, during pregnancy, with contraception — so it cannot primarily serve producing offspring.

#### Solution

Human sex occurs almost entirely at moments when fertilization is impossible or unwanted — outside the fertile window, during pregnancy, with contraception, between same-sex partners — so it cannot primarily serve producing offspring. Unlike most mammals, human mating is not hormone-switched to the fertile period, and hidden ovulation means partners cannot know the fertile moment; sexual behaviour in humans therefore serves pleasure and bonding far more than reproduction.
*Marking:* • sex not tied to the fertile period/ovulation (1) • most sexual acts cannot lead to pregnancy → mainly other functions, unlike mammals in heat (1)

### bio-d04-q03 | points 2 | outcome D4.1.3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A male silk moth flies hundreds of metres upwind as soon as he detects one molecule of the female's pheromone. Human sexual arousal, by contrast, depends strongly on the situation, the relationship, experience and culture.

Both the pheromone and a human sexually exciting image act as stimuli that trigger sexual behaviour. Describe the main difference in how fixed this stimulus–response link is in the two species.

#### Answer

In the silk moth the link is fixed and innate: one specific sign stimulus (the pheromone) reliably releases the behaviour in every male. In humans the link is flexible: the effect of a sexual stimulus depends on emotions, the relationship, experience and culture, and is much less directly hormone-driven.

#### Solution

In the silk moth the link is fixed and innate: one specific sign stimulus (the pheromone) reliably releases the behaviour in every male — a typical reproductive sign stimulus. In humans the link is flexible: the effect of a sexual stimulus depends on emotions, the relationship, experience and culture, and human sexual behaviour is much less directly switched on by one chemical signal or hormone.
*Marking:* • moth: fixed, innate, species-typical sign stimulus (1) • humans: context-dependent, modifiable link (1)

### bio-d04-q09 | points 3 | outcome D4.1.9 | difficulty medium

#### Question

The combined contraceptive pill contains a synthetic oestrogen and a progestogen. Explain, using negative feedback, why a woman taking the pill does not ovulate, and name one further way in which the pill prevents pregnancy.

#### Answer

The pill's hormones raise the blood levels of oestrogen- and progesterone-like hormones; these inhibit the hypothalamus and pituitary (negative feedback), so little FSH and LH are released.

#### Solution

The pill's hormones raise the blood levels of oestrogen- and progesterone-like hormones; these inhibit the hypothalamus and pituitary (negative feedback), so little FSH and LH are released. Without FSH no follicle matures, and without an LH peak no ovulation takes place. Additionally the pill thickens the mucus of the cervix, which stops sperm cells (and thins the uterine lining).
*Marking:* • negative feedback on hypothalamus/pituitary (1) • less FSH/LH → no follicle maturation and no ovulation (1) • extra mechanism: thickened cervical mucus (1)

### bio-d04-q11 | points 2 | outcome D4.1.4 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A couple avoids sex on the "fertile days" but still becomes pregnant after six months. The woman has a regular cycle of 32 days. Ovulation occurs about 14 days before the next menstruation; sperm cells survive up to 5 days in the female body, the egg cell about 24 hours.

Calculate on which cycle days fertilization is possible, and use this to argue why the natural method is less reliable than a hormonal IUD.

### bio-d04-q16 | points 3 | outcome D4.1.7 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Epidemiologists trial-offer a chlamydia test to 100 000 people aged 16–29 in one region. True prevalence is 3.0%. The test's sensitivity is 90% (detects 90% of the infected), its specificity is 99% (labels 99% of the non-infected correctly negative).

### bio-d04-q17 | points 3 | outcome D4.1.7 | difficulty hard

#### Question

Same screening as in q16 (100 000 people, prevalence 3.0%, sensitivity 90%, specificity 99%).

Calculate the number of false positives, and the percentage of all positive test results that is a false alarm. Explain why this percentage is so high even though the test is 99% specific.

#### Answer

Non-infected: 97 000. False positives: 1% × 97 000 = **970**.

#### Solution

Non-infected: 100 000 − 3 000 = 97 000. False positives: (100% − 99%) × 97 000 = **970**. Total positives: 2 700 + 970 = 3 670; false-alarm share: 970/3 670 × 100% = **26.4%**. Because the infection is rare, the small error rate applies to a very large non-infected group, so 1% of 97 000 still yields many false positives — and people with a false-positive result get unnecessary treatment and worry.
*Marking:* • 97 000 non-infected and 970 false positives (1) • 26.4% computed (1) • explanation: low prevalence makes 1% errors numerous (1)

### bio-d04-q19 | points 3 | outcome D4.1.7 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Explain, starting from the cell HIV infects, how an untreated HIV infection develops into AIDS over about ten years. In your answer also make clear why both antibody production and the killing of infected cells fail, and why antibiotics cannot help.

#### Answer

HIV infects T helper cells (which carry the CD4 receptor) and multiplies in them; over the years the number of T helper cells falls.

#### Solution

HIV infects T helper cells (which carry the CD4 receptor) and multiplies in them; over the years the number of T helper cells falls. T helper cells activate B cells (so antibody production fails) and activate cytotoxic T cells (so the killing of infected cells fails); with both arms of the specific defence down, opportunistic infections and tumours take over — this final stage is AIDS. Antibiotics only work against bacteria; HIV is a virus, so they cannot help (antiretroviral drugs are used instead).
*Marking:* • HIV targets T helper cells and their number declines (1) • both humoral (B cells) and cellular (cytotoxic T) arms fail via lost activation (1) • virus, not bacterium → antibiotics useless (1)
