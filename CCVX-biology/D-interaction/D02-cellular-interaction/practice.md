### bio-d02-q01 | points 3 | outcome D2.1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

During a sudden fright, the adrenal medulla releases adrenaline into the blood. Liver cells respond within seconds: they break down glycogen and release glucose into the blood. Adrenaline is a small water-soluble signal substance.

Describe how a liver cell receives this signal and processes it into a response. Name the receptor, the second messenger and the cell's response.

#### Answer

Adrenaline (the signal substance) binds to a matching receptor in the cell membrane of the liver cell.

#### Solution

Adrenaline (the signal substance) binds to a matching receptor in the cell membrane of the liver cell. The activated receptor switches on a relay inside the cell (a G protein and adenylate cyclase), which forms the second messenger cAMP. cAMP activates a cascade of protein kinases, and the final response is that glycogen is broken down and glucose is released into the blood.
*Marking:* • adrenaline binds a receptor in the cell membrane (1) • cAMP formed as second messenger, relayed by (G protein / adenylate cyclase and) kinases (1) • response: glycogen broken down, glucose released (1)

### bio-d02-q03 | points 2 | outcome D2.1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

During a sudden fright, the adrenal medulla releases adrenaline into the blood. Liver cells respond within seconds: they break down glycogen and release glucose into the blood. Adrenaline is a small water-soluble signal substance.

Adrenaline itself does not enter the liver cell, yet the cell reacts. Explain how the second messenger makes this possible.

The receptor, activated by adrenaline on the outside, causes adenylate cyclase on the inside of the membrane to form cAMP.

#### Solution

The receptor, activated by adrenaline on the outside, causes adenylate cyclase on the inside of the membrane to form cAMP. *Marking:* • cAMP is formed inside the cell when the receptor is activated (1) • cAMP passes the signal on through the cytoplasm (to the cascade) (1)

The blood glucose level stays high because uptake is poor, and a high glucose level is the stimulus for the β-cells of the pancreas to release insulin, so the pancreas keeps producing extra insulin (negative feedback can no longer switch it off).
