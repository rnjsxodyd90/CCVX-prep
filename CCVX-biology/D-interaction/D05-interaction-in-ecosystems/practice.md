### bio-d05-q03 | points 2 | outcome D5.1.3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

The researchers' notes from the first question list twelve "is eaten by" relations in the meadow food web.

Explain why the starling cannot be assigned to a single trophic level in this web, and name the trophic levels at which the starling can feed. (Producers count as level 1.)

#### Answer

The starling eats grasshoppers, which are herbivores (level 2), so the starling then feeds at level 3; it also eats ladybirds (level 3), and then it feeds at level 4.

#### Solution

The trophic level of an animal depends on the level of its prey. The grasshopper eats producers, so it is at level 2; the starling eating a grasshopper is at level 3. The ladybird eats aphids, so the ladybird is at level 3; the starling eating a ladybird is at level 4. Because the starling uses prey from two levels, it has no single trophic level.
*Marking:* • insight: the level depends on the prey's level, and the starling has prey from different levels (1) • level 3 via the grasshopper and level 4 via the ladybird (1)

### bio-d05-q04 | points 3 | outcome D5.1.3 | difficulty hard

#### Question

The researchers' notes from the first question list twelve "is eaten by" relations in the meadow food web.

A viral disease kills nearly all field voles in the meadow. Predict the effect on the barn owl population and on the standing crop of grass and clover. Explain both predictions with the food web.

#### Answer

The barn owl population declines, but less sharply than the vole population, because the barn owl has an alternative prey (the common shrew). The standing crop of grass and clover increases, because an important grazer has disappeared, so less of the plants is eaten.

#### Solution

In the web the barn owl eats the field vole but also the common shrew. *Marking:* • prediction: barn owl declines (1) • explanation: barn owl still has the shrew as an alternative prey, so the decline is damped (1) • less grazing, so the plant crop increases (1)

### bio-d05-q06 | points 3 | outcome D5.1.4 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A persistent insecticide (invented name: Veltox) leaks into a lake. Veltox is fat-soluble and is not broken down in the environment or in organisms. The mean concentrations measured are:

| Food chain link | Organism | Veltox (mg per kg fresh mass) |
|---|---|---|

| zooplankton | water fleas | 0.04 |
| plankton-eating fish | stickleback | 0.8 |

Calculate the concentration factor of Veltox from the algae to the kingfisher.

#### Answer

16 / 0.002 = **8 000 times**.

Concentration factor = concentration in the kingfisher / concentration in the algae = 16 / 0.002 = 16 000 / 2 = **8 000**. So the concentration in the kingfisher is eight thousand times that in the algae.

### bio-d05-q07 | points 3 | outcome D5.1.4 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Using the same lake data (Veltox in the stickleback: 0.8 mg per kg), calculate the mean concentration factor per trophic step, and calculate how many μg of Veltox a kingfisher takes in per day if it eats 40 g of stickleback per day. ### bio-d05-q08 | points 3 | outcome D5.2.3 | difficulty hard

#### Question

Using the same lake data on Veltox, explain step by step why the concentration of Veltox (mg per kg) becomes higher at each trophic level, while only about 10% of the biomass of the level below is converted into new biomass of the level above.

#### Answer

Veltox is persistent and fat-soluble, so organisms break it down hardly at all and cannot excrete it; it stays in their tissues. A water flea keeps nearly all the Veltox of the algae it eats, but builds only about 10% of that algae biomass into its own biomass. The same amount of toxin is thus stored in ten times less biomass, so the concentration (amount per kg) rises about tenfold per step; this repeats at every step up to the kingfisher.

#### Solution

Explanation has three parts: (1) persistence and fat-solubility: the substance is not degraded and not excreted with urine, so it accumulates in the body of each organism; (2) biomass transfer: only ~10% of eaten biomass becomes the consumer's own biomass, the rest is respired or lost as faeces, but most of the toxin is kept; (3) consequence: the same mass of toxin divided by a much smaller biomass gives a higher concentration, and this repeats at each level, giving accumulation along the chain.
*Marking:* • persistent and fat-soluble, so stored, not broken down or excreted (1) • ~90% of the biomass is lost per step while most of the toxin is kept (1) • same amount of toxin in less biomass → higher concentration, repeated per level (1)

### bio-d05-q09 | points 2 | outcome D5.2.3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A second substance, Selparon, is water-soluble and biodegradable: soil bacteria break it down to harmless inorganic substances within a few weeks. Explain why Selparon does not accumulate in a food chain, unlike Veltox. Name the two properties that decide whether a substance accumulates.

#### Answer

Selparon does not accumulate because it is biodegradable — decomposers break it down before organisms can pass large amounts up the chain — and water-soluble, so animals excrete it in their urine instead of storing it in fat. The two decisive properties are persistence (broken down or not) and solubility (fat-soluble or water-soluble).

#### Solution

Accumulation requires that a substance is persistent (not broken down in the environment or in organisms) and fat-soluble (stored in fat tissue, not excreted). Selparon fails on both counts: decomposers degrade it within weeks, and being water-soluble it leaves the body with the watery urine. So it is neither kept in a body nor passed on in rising amounts.
*Marking:* • biodegradable → broken down by decomposers (1) • water-soluble → excreted, not stored in fat; accumulation needs persistence and fat-solubility (1)

### bio-d05-q10 | points 2 | outcome D5.1.4 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Veltox was applied at a ditch some kilometres from the lake, and the kingfisher nests in trees along the lake. Explain why it is precisely the kingfisher — a top predator that never touches the ditch — that runs the highest risk of being poisoned.

#### Answer

The Veltox runs off via the ditch into the lake's food chain and accumulates: the concentration becomes higher at each trophic level. The kingfisher is at the end of a long chain (algae → water flea → stickleback → kingfisher), so despite the distance it receives the highest dose in its food.

#### Solution

Run-off carries the persistent substance from the ditch into surface water. From there it passes along the food chain, and at each step the concentration rises (see the accumulation data). Because the kingfisher is a top predator at the end of a long chain, the substance it gets through its fish is concentrated thousands of times; top predators therefore run the highest risk even far from the source. (Compare the DDT egg-shell thinning in birds of prey and the Minamata mercury poisoning.)
*Marking:* • the substance reaches the food chain via run-off/water (1) • accumulation along the chain gives the top predator the highest concentration (1)

### bio-d05-q12 | points 2 | outcome D5.1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

When spider mites feed on the leaves of a lima bean plant, the leaves release volatile substances into the air. Predatory mites, which hunt spider mites, are attracted by these substances and move onto the plant.

Give the term for such volatile substances, and explain how their release benefits the lima bean plant.

#### Answer

Term: signal substances. The plant benefits because the attracted predatory mites eat the spider mites, so the number of herbivores on the plant falls and the plant suffers less feeding damage.

#### Solution

Substances that carry information between organisms are signal substances. Here the signal attracts a natural enemy of the herbivore (the predatory mite); the predatory mites reduce the spider-mite population, so the plant is less damaged. This is a form of indirect defence: the plant "calls up" the enemy of its attacker.
*Marking:* • term: signal substances (1) • benefit: enemies of the herbivore are attracted, so feeding damage decreases (1)

### bio-d05-q13 | points 2 | outcome D5.2.5 | difficulty medium

#### Question

In a sweet-pepper greenhouse, the grower fights the whitefly pest not with insecticide but by regularly releasing parasitic wasps (Encarsia formosa), whose larvae develop at the expense of the whiteflies.

Give the name of this pest-control method, and explain one advantage of it for the sustainability of the food production compared with spraying insecticide.

#### Answer

Method: biological control. Advantage (example): no insecticide enters the environment, so there is no toxic pollution, no accumulation of the substance in food chains and no selection for resistance; also harmless insects (such as pollinators) are spared.

#### Solution

Using a natural enemy of a pest is biological control. Unlike an insecticide, a released wasp population regulates itself with the whitefly population, leaves no persistent toxic residues that could accumulate in a food chain, does not select for resistance genes in the pest, and does not kill beneficial insects such as the bumblebees used for pollination. A condition is that the whitefly population is never fully wiped out, otherwise the wasps die out too.
*Marking:* • term: biological control (1) • one correct advantage for sustainability (no residues/accumulation, no resistance selection, no harm to other species) (1)

### bio-d05-q18 | points 3 | outcome D5.2.4 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Two duckweed species grow in identical aquaria with the same water, light and nutrients. After four weeks the water surface is covered as follows (% of the surface covered by each species):

| Aquarium | Lemna minor (%) | Spirodela polyrhiza (%) |
|---|---|---|

Argue with the data that Spirodela suffers from the presence of Lemna much more than the other way round, and explain this with the concept of the niche.

#### Answer

Grown together, Lemna declines only slightly (85 → 80%) but Spirodela collapses (70 → 12%). ### bio-d05-q19 | points 2 | outcome D5.2.4 | difficulty easy

#### Question

On a wheat field many organisms need the same scarce resources: light, water, nitrate and space. Distinguish between competition within a population (intraspecific) and competition between populations (interspecific), and give one example of each from this wheat field.

Intraspecific: wheat plants competing with each other for light (or water, nitrate, space). Interspecific: wheat plants competing with wild oats (or another weed) for nitrate and light, or two aphid species competing for the phloem sap of wheat.

### bio-d05-q25 | points 2 | outcome D5.2.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A brook is sampled at two stations. Station A is upstream; station B is just below the discharge pipe of a small factory.

| Organism group | Station A | Station B |
|---|---|---|
| stonefly larvae | many | absent |
| mayfly larvae | many | few |
| bloodworms (red midge larvae) | absent | many |
| sludge worms (Tubifex) | absent | many |

Which station has the lower oxygen concentration? Name one indicator species from the table and state what it indicates.

#### Answer

Station B has the lower oxygen concentration. Indicator species (example): stonefly larvae indicate clean, oxygen-rich water — their disappearance at B shows that the oxygen concentration there is low (alternatively: sludge worms/bloodworms indicate oxygen-poor, polluted water, and they appear at B).

#### Solution

Indicator species have a narrow tolerance for one abiotic factor, so their presence or absence shows its value. Stonefly and mayfly larvae need oxygen-rich water; they disappear below the discharge. Bloodworms and sludge worms tolerate oxygen-poor, polluted water; they appear exactly there. So station B is oxygen-poor.
*Marking:* • conclusion: station B has the lower oxygen concentration (1) • one indicator species named with the condition it indicates (1)

### bio-d05-q26 | points 4 | outcome D5.2.6 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A pupil calculates his ecological footprint: 6.0 hectares of productive land and sea per year, divided as: transport 2.1 ha, food 1.8 ha, home energy 1.4 ha, purchases 0.7 ha. The Earth's productive area can sustainably provide about 1.7 hectares per person (the world's mean biocapacity per person).

Calculate how many times larger this pupil's footprint is than the mean biocapacity per person. Then name his two largest footprint categories and give for each one concrete measure that reduces it, with the mechanism by which it works.

#### Answer

6.0 / 1.7 ≈ **3.5 times larger**. Largest categories: transport (2.1 ha) and food (1.8 ha). Measures (example): travel by bike, train or electric instead of by petrol car or plane — that cuts the fossil-fuel emissions the footprint charges; eat less meat and dairy — meat needs much more land per kJ of food than crops, because an extra trophic level loses most of the energy.

#### Solution

Ratio: 6.0 / 1.7 = 3.53 ≈ 3.5. The two largest items are transport (2.1 ha) and food (1.8 ha). Transport: replacing car/plane kilometres by biking, train or electric driving reduces the burning of fossil fuel, so less of the footprint is needed to absorb CO₂. Food: less meat avoids a trophic level — crops can feed people directly, so far less farmland per kJ of food is required. (Answers can vary; each measure must match the chosen category and give its mechanism.)
*Marking:* • ratio 6.0 / 1.7 ≈ 3.5 (1) • the two largest categories named: transport and food (1) • fitting transport measure with its mechanism (1) • fitting food measure with its mechanism (1)
