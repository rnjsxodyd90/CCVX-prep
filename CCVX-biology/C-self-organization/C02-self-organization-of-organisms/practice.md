### bio-c02-q04 | points 2 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty easy

Then it forms a chrysalis, hanging from a leaf. After about two weeks an adult butterfly comes out, which feeds on nectar.

#### Answer

The statement is wrong.

#### Solution

The statement is wrong. Inside the chrysalis most of the larval tissue is broken down and the adult body (wings, legs, reproductive organs) is built from groups of cells that were set aside in the larva. Far from resting, the pupa is a stage of intensive rebuilding.

### bio-c02-q19 | points 2 | outcome C2.3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A greenhouse horticulturist grows tomatoes and wants the highest possible production during the dark winter months. She measures the growth of the plants under four conditions.

| Light in the greenhouse | CO₂ in the greenhouse air | Growth (g per plant per week) |
|---|---|---|
| low (natural winter light) | 400 ppm (normal outside air) | 250 |
| low | 900 ppm (extra CO₂ added) | 285 |
| high (extra LED lighting) | 400 ppm | 610 |
| high | 900 ppm | 780 |

Calculate by what percentage the growth increases when CO₂ rises from 400 to 900 ppm, at low light and at high light.

#### Answer

At low light: (285 − 250) / 250 × 100% = 14% increase.

#### Solution

At low light: (285 − 250) / 250 × 100% = 14% increase. At high light: (780 − 610) / 610 × 100% ≈ 28% increase.
*Marking:* • 14% at low light (1) • about 28% at high light (1)

#### Question

A greenhouse horticulturist grows tomatoes and wants the highest possible production during the dark winter months. She measures the growth of the plants under four conditions.

| Light in the greenhouse | CO₂ in the greenhouse air | Growth (g per plant per week) |
|---|---|---|
| low (natural winter light) | 400 ppm (normal outside air) | 250 |
| low | 900 ppm (extra CO₂ added) | 285 |
| high (extra LED lighting) | 400 ppm | 610 |
| high | 900 ppm | 780 |

Explain, using the results from 10a, which factor limits growth at low light.

#### Answer

If CO₂ were the limiting factor at low light, adding CO₂ would raise growth strongly.

#### Solution

If CO₂ were the limiting factor at low light, adding CO₂ would raise growth strongly. In reality extra CO₂ gives only 14% more growth at low light, while extra light raises the growth from 250 to 610 g per plant per week — an increase of 144% at the same CO₂ level. So at low light it is light, not CO₂, that limits growth; extra CO₂ only pays off when light is abundant (+28%).
*Marking:* • extra CO₂ at low light gives little extra growth (+14%) (1) • extra light raises growth much more (250 → 610, +144%) (1) • conclusion: light is the limiting factor at low light (1)

The bumblebees intervene at the flowering stage: they pollinate the flowers (carry pollen to the stigma), which is needed for fertilization and therefore for the formation of fruits and seeds.
