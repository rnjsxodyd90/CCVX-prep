### bio-c01-q01 | points 2 | outcome C1.1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Biologists map a human gene, GEN-S, that codes for an enzyme produced in salivary gland cells. The gene is 2100 base pairs long and contains three exons and two introns. Exon 1 is 300 base pairs, exon 2 is 240 base pairs and exon 3 is 120 base pairs. Intron 1, between exons 1 and 2, is 900 base pairs long.

Calculate the length of intron 2, and show with a calculation that this gene consists largely of introns.

#### Answer

Exons total 300 + 240 + 120 = 660 base pairs, so the two introns together are 2100 − 660 = 1440 base pairs.

#### Solution

Exons total 300 + 240 + 120 = 660 base pairs, so the two introns together are 2100 − 660 = 1440 base pairs. Intron 2 = 1440 − 900 = **540 base pairs**. Share of introns: 1440 / 2100 × 100% = 68.6%, well over half, so the gene consists largely of introns.
*Marking:* • intron 2 = 540 bp (via exon total 660 and intron total 1440) (1) • 68.6% with the conclusion "largely introns" (1)

Inside the macrophage the lysosomes fuse with the packages and their digestive enzymes, which work at a low pH, break down the remains.
*Marking:* • the cell dismantles itself into membrane-wrapped packages (1) • macrophages engulf the packages (1) • lysosomes fuse with them and digest the contents (1)

### bio-c01-q30 | points 2 | outcome C1.2.5 | difficulty easy

#### Question

During the metamorphosis of a tadpole, the tail disappears within a few days. The tail cells do not burst: they shrink and are taken up in small packages by macrophages. The disappearance is controlled by thyroid hormone: tadpoles with a high level of this hormone lose their tail earlier.

Injured cells can die by necrosis. Explain the difference between apoptosis and necrosis, and explain why apoptosis in the tail causes no inflammation.

#### Answer

In apoptosis the cell contents stay wrapped in membrane and nothing leaks out, so no inflammation arises; in necrosis the injured cell swells and bursts, its contents leak into the tissue and trigger inflammation.

#### Solution

In apoptosis the cell contents stay wrapped in membrane and nothing leaks out, so no inflammation arises; in necrosis the injured cell swells and bursts, its contents leak into the tissue and trigger inflammation. In the tail everything is packaged and taken up, which is why no inflammation occurs.
*Marking:* • difference: controlled with membrane-wrapped contents versus uncontrolled bursting/leaking (1) • no leaking contents → no inflammation in apoptosis (1)
