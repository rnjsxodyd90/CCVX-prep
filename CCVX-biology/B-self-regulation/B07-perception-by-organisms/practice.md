### bio-b07-q01 | points 2 | outcome B7.3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

During a football match a defender heads a heavy ball. For a few seconds he "sees stars": short flashes of light, although the light on the pitch has not changed at all.

Give the adequate stimulus of the rods and cones in the retina, and explain what is meant by the term "adequate stimulus".

#### Answer

The adequate stimulus of rods and cones is light.

#### Solution

The adequate stimulus of rods and cones is light. The adequate stimulus of a receptor is the kind of stimulus to which that receptor is most sensitive: the stimulus with the lowest threshold, for which the receptor is specialised.
*Marking:* • adequate stimulus of rods/cones is light (1) • an adequate stimulus is the stimulus type the receptor is most sensitive to / has the lowest threshold for (1)

### bio-b07-q02 | points 2 | outcome B7.3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

During a football match a defender heads a heavy ball. For a few seconds he "sees stars": short flashes of light, although the light on the pitch has not changed at all.

The header is a mechanical stimulus, not light. Yet the player perceives flashes of light. Explain how this is possible.

#### Answer

All sensory impulses are action potentials of the same kind; what you perceive depends only on which brain centre they reach.

#### Solution

All sensory impulses are action potentials of the same kind; what you perceive depends only on which brain centre they reach. The blow deforms the receptors of the retina (a mechanical stimulus), so the rods and cones fire anyway, as they have no other "language". Those impulses travel along the optic nerve to the visual cortex, and the brain interprets everything arriving there as light: hence flashes of light without any real light.
*Marking:* • the blow mechanically excites the retinal receptors (1) • impulses reach the visual cortex, so the brain perceives them as light (1)

### bio-b07-q05 | points 2 | outcome B7.2 | difficulty medium

#### Question

An optician shows a trainee a model of the human eye and follows the path of the light that falls on the eye.

The cornea performs about two-thirds of the refraction, the lens only a smaller part. Yet it is the lens, not the cornea, that is used for accommodation. Explain why.

#### Answer

Accommodation requires an adjustable refraction.

#### Solution

Accommodation requires an adjustable refraction. The cornea is a fixed, rigid, transparent window, so its refractive power cannot change. The lens is elastic: the ciliary muscle can change its shape (rounder for near, flatter for far), so its refractive power — and with it the focus distance — is adjustable. A structure with constant strength cannot be used for focusing at different distances.
*Marking:* • the cornea is rigid, so its refraction is fixed (1) • the lens is elastic and its shape/refractive power is adjustable via the ciliary muscle (1)

### bio-b07-q18 | points 3 | outcome B7.2 | difficulty medium

#### Question

An amateur astronomer gives a tip: "If you want to see a very faint star, do not look straight at it, but slightly next to it — then it suddenly appears." She also notices that faint stars have no colour, while a bright planet clearly shows colour.

Explain why the faint star is seen better when you look slightly next to it. Use the distribution of rods and cones over the retina in your answer.

#### Answer

If you look straight at the star, its image falls on the yellow spot (macula/fovea), which contains only cones — and cones need much more light than rods, so a faint star gives no response there.

#### Solution

If you look straight at the star, its image falls on the yellow spot (macula/fovea), which contains only cones — and cones need much more light than rods, so a faint star gives no response there. *Marking:* • looking straight: image falls on the macula/fovea with only cones (1) • cones are less light-sensitive than rods, so the faint star fails there (1) • looking beside it: image falls on rods in the outer retina, which are very light-sensitive (1)

#### Question

Hikers who suddenly see a snake on the path get an immediate stress response: heart beating fast, wide pupils, ready to jump back. Describe how the senses, the nervous system and the hormonal system work together here, and explain the use of the wider pupils.

#### Answer

The eyes pass the image of the snake via sensory neurons to the visual cortex; the brain interprets it as danger and activates the sympathetic part of the autonomic nervous system.

#### Solution

The eyes pass the image of the snake via sensory neurons to the visual cortex; the brain interprets it as danger and activates the sympathetic part of the autonomic nervous system. This makes the adrenal medulla release the hormone adrenaline into the blood, which reinforces and prolongs the sympathetic effects: faster heartbeat, glucose release for the muscles, and widening of the pupils (radial muscle of the iris). The wider pupils admit more light and improve the overview of the surroundings, which helps the quick evaluation of the danger and the escape (and the muscles stand ready to jump back).
*Marking:* • visual perception → sympathetic activation (nervous system) (1) • adrenaline from the adrenal medulla reinforces the stress response (hormonal system) (1) • wider pupils let in more light → better overview during escape readiness (1)

A rabbit is a prey animal: with eyes at the sides of the head the two visual fields overlap little, so together they cover almost the whole horizon — the rabbit can spot approaching predators in nearly every direction.
