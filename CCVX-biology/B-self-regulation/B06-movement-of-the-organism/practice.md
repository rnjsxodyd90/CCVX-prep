### bio-b06-q01 | points 3 | outcome B6.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

About a day after death, the muscles of a body become stiff (rigor mortis). A pathologist explains to students that this stiffness follows directly from the way a normal muscle contraction works at the level of the sarcomere.

Describe, in the right order, how an action potential in a muscle fibre leads to the formation of cross-bridges between actin and myosin. Mention the roles of the sarcoplasmic reticulum and Ca²⁺.

#### Answer

The action potential spreads over the membrane of the muscle fibre and into the fibre through the T-tubules, and makes the sarcoplasmic reticulum release its stored Ca²⁺ into the cytoplasm.

#### Solution

The action potential spreads over the membrane of the muscle fibre and into the fibre through the T-tubules, and makes the sarcoplasmic reticulum release its stored Ca²⁺ into the cytoplasm. Ca²⁺ binds to troponin on the actin filament, which shifts tropomyosin, so the binding sites on actin are uncovered. The cocked myosin heads can now bind to actin and form cross-bridges.
*Marking:* • action potential reaches the sarcoplasmic reticulum via the (T-tubules of the) membrane (1) • the reticulum releases Ca²⁺ (1) • Ca²⁺ uncovers the binding sites on actin (via troponin/tropomyosin), so cross-bridges can form (1)

### bio-b06-q02 | points 2 | outcome B6.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

About a day after death, the muscles of a body become stiff (rigor mortis). A pathologist explains to students that this stiffness follows directly from the way a normal muscle contraction works at the level of the sarcomere.

Explain why the myosin heads stay bound to the actin when the muscle cell runs out of ATP, so that the muscle becomes stiff.

#### Answer

A myosin head can only let go of actin when a new ATP molecule binds to the head; splitting that ATP then cocks the head again.

#### Solution

A myosin head can only let go of actin when a new ATP molecule binds to the head; splitting that ATP then cocks the head again. After death no new ATP is produced, the head cannot detach, and the cross-bridges stay locked: the muscle becomes stiff.
*Marking:* • ATP is needed to release the myosin head from actin (1) • without new ATP the cross-bridges remain → stiffness (1)

### bio-b06-q04 | points 2 | outcome B6.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A physiotherapist uses dumbbell exercises with a client who is recovering from an elbow injury. During a biceps curl the client bends the elbow to lift the dumbbell, then slowly straightens the arm again.

Explain why the muscles around the elbow must work as an antagonist pair: why can one and the same muscle not both bend and straighten the elbow?

#### Answer

A muscle can only exert force by contracting, so it can only pull; it cannot push to move the bones back.

#### Solution

A muscle can only exert force by contracting, so it can only pull; it cannot push to move the bones back. Bending the elbow needs a pulling muscle on one side (the flexor, biceps); straightening it again needs a second muscle pulling on the other side (the extensor, triceps).
*Marking:* • a muscle can only pull (shorten), not push (1) • so an opposite second muscle is needed to move the joint back (1)

### bio-b06-q08 | points 3 | outcome B6.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

The tap stretches the quadriceps for a split second. Describe the reflex arc of the knee-jerk response, naming the receptor, the neuron(s) in order, and the effector.

#### Answer

The receptor is the muscle spindle in the quadriceps, which measures stretch.

#### Solution

The receptor is the muscle spindle in the quadriceps, which measures stretch. Monosynaptic means there is only one synapse in the whole reflex arc, that between the sensory neuron and the motor neuron in the spinal cord.

#### Solution

### bio-b06-q11 | points 3 | outcome B6.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

|---|---|---|

#### Answer

Athlete P is the marathon runner.

*Marking:* • correct choice: P (1) • two rows of the table correctly linked to endurance (1) • third row correctly linked (1)
