#### Question

#### Solution

The pyramid of biomass shows the standing biomass at one moment, not the production over time. #### Question

On one day in April, researchers in the North Sea find a standing biomass of 3 g dry mass per m² of phytoplankton and 5 g dry mass per m² of zooplankton. A pyramid of biomass drawn for that day is therefore upside down.

A pyramid of energy production (production per trophic level in kJ m⁻² year⁻¹) can never be upside down. Explain why.

#### Answer

Energy is lost at every transfer: part is not eaten, part leaves as faeces, and most of the absorbed energy is converted to heat by dissimilation.

#### Solution

Energy is lost at every transfer: part is not eaten, part leaves as faeces, and most of the absorbed energy is converted to heat by dissimilation. Therefore the production of a trophic level can never exceed the production of the level below it (energy conservation), so each higher bar of the pyramid is always smaller.
*Marking:* • energy losses between levels (faeces, heat) (1) • production of the next level can never be larger than that of the level below, so the pyramid always narrows upward (1)

#### Solution

The closed mat blocks nearly all light; submerged water plants and algae get too little light, photosynthesis stops and they die; decomposers break down the dead material and consume large amounts of oxygen (and the dying plants no longer produce O₂); the oxygen concentration falls, so fish and other animals suffocate or leave; fewer species remain and the food web is impoverished.
*Marking:* • mat blocks light → submerged plants/algae die (1) • decomposition of the remains consumes O₂ / less photosynthesis, so O₂ falls (1) • fish and other organisms die off / biodiversity declines (1)

### bio-b08-q29 | points 4 | outcome B8.3.4 | difficulty hard

#### Question

A fast-growing South American floating pond plant is sold for garden ponds. After pond clean-outs, fragments are dumped in Dutch ditches, where the plant — arriving without its natural enemies — spreads and forms a dense mat that doubles its area every 10 days.

A water board considers two measures: (1) frequent mechanical removal of the plants, (2) releasing an introduced South American beetle that eats only this plant. Give one advantage and one risk or disadvantage per measure for the ditch ecosystem.

#### Answer

Mechanical removal: advantage — it directly reduces the plant mass and exports nutrients from the ditch, without introducing new organisms; disadvantage — fragments regrow easily and are spread by machines, and small animals are removed or disturbed along with the plants, so it must be repeated often.

#### Solution

Mechanical removal: advantage — it directly reduces the plant mass and exports nutrients from the ditch, without introducing new organisms; disadvantage — fragments regrow easily and are spread by machines, and small animals are removed or disturbed along with the plants, so it must be repeated often. Beetle release: advantage — once established it suppresses the plant continuously and works everywhere, no repeated machine work; disadvantage/risk — the beetle may turn out to damage native species too or have unforeseen effects, and once released it is very hard to reconsider (a new introduced species).
*Marking:* • advantage of mechanical removal (1) • disadvantage/risk of mechanical removal (1) • advantage of the beetle (1) • risk of releasing a new introduced species (1)
