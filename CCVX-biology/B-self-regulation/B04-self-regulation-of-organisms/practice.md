### bio-b04-q01 | points 2 | outcome B4.1.3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

### bio-b04-q10 | points 2 | outcome B4.1.3 | difficulty medium

#### Answer

In the peripheral nervous system: Schwann cells.

### bio-b04-q33 | points 2 | outcome B4.3.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

In multiple sclerosis (MS), the immune system damages the myelin in the central nervous system. A healthy myelinated axon conducts impulses at about 100 m s⁻¹; an axon that has lost its myelin conducts at about 1 m s⁻¹. The distance from the lower spinal cord to a foot muscle is about 0.90 m.

Explain how impulses travel along a myelinated axon and why this is much faster.

#### Answer

Myelin insulates the axon, so ions can only cross the membrane at the nodes of Ranvier.

#### Solution

Myelin insulates the axon, so ions can only cross the membrane at the nodes of Ranvier. The action potential is therefore regenerated only at the nodes and in effect jumps from node to node (saltatory conduction), which covers the distance much faster than rebuilding the action potential at every point of an unmyelinated membrane.
*Marking:* • ions can only pass at the nodes of Ranvier (1) • jumping from node to node is faster (1)

Calculate the extra conduction time over 0.90 m after the myelin is lost.

#### Answer

With myelin: t = 0.90 / 100 = 0.009 s (9 ms).

#### Solution

With myelin: t = 0.90 / 100 = 0.009 s (9 ms). Without myelin: t = 0.90 / 1 = 0.9 s. Extra time = 0.9 − 0.009 = **0.891 s ≈ 0.9 s**.
*Marking:* • both conduction times correct (1) • difference ≈ 0.9 s (1)

### bio-b04-q36 | points 3 | outcome B4.3.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

ADHD is linked to the signalling of the neurotransmitters dopamine and noradrenaline in the brain. The medicine methylphenidate blocks the reuptake of these neurotransmitters by the presynaptic cell.

Describe in the right order the steps of signal transfer at a synapse, from the arrival of an action potential at the axon terminal to the response in the postsynaptic cell.

#### Answer

The action potential opens voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in the axon terminal; Ca²⁺ flows in.

#### Solution

The action potential opens voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in the axon terminal; Ca²⁺ flows in. Vesicles with neurotransmitter then fuse with the presynaptic membrane (exocytosis), and the neurotransmitter is released into the synaptic cleft. It diffuses across the cleft and binds to receptor proteins in the postsynaptic membrane, which open ion channels there and change the postsynaptic potential.
*Marking:* • Ca²⁺ influx triggers vesicle fusion / release (1) • diffusion across the cleft (1) • binding to receptors opens ion channels in the postsynaptic membrane (1)

### bio-b04-q37 | points 2 | outcome B4.3.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

ADHD is linked to the signalling of the neurotransmitters dopamine and noradrenaline in the brain. The medicine methylphenidate blocks the reuptake of these neurotransmitters by the presynaptic cell.

Explain the difference between an excitatory and an inhibitory synapse, in terms of ion movements and the effect on the postsynaptic membrane.

#### Answer

At an excitatory synapse Na⁺ channels (or channels that let positive charge in) open and the postsynaptic membrane depolarises, moving it closer to the threshold.

#### Solution

At an excitatory synapse Na⁺ channels (or channels that let positive charge in) open and the postsynaptic membrane depolarises, moving it closer to the threshold. At an inhibitory synapse Cl⁻ channels (or K⁺ channels) open and the membrane hyperpolarises, taking it further from the threshold, so an action potential becomes less likely.
*Marking:* • excitatory: Na⁺ in, depolarisation, closer to threshold (1) • inhibitory: Cl⁻ in (or K⁺ out), hyperpolarisation, further from threshold (1)

ADHD is linked to the signalling of the neurotransmitters dopamine and noradrenaline in the brain. The medicine methylphenidate blocks the reuptake of these neurotransmitters by the presynaptic cell.

Explain how methylphenidate changes the dopamine concentration in the synaptic cleft and how this changes the signalling.

#### Answer

Blocking the reuptake means that dopamine released into the cleft is not taken back up (quickly) by the presynaptic cell, so the dopamine concentration in the cleft stays higher for longer.

#### Solution

Blocking the reuptake means that dopamine released into the cleft is not taken back up (quickly) by the presynaptic cell, so the dopamine concentration in the cleft stays higher for longer. The postsynaptic receptors are therefore stimulated longer/more strongly: the dopamine signal is amplified and prolonged.
*Marking:* • dopamine remains longer in the cleft / higher concentration (1) • longer stimulation of the postsynaptic membrane → strengthened signal (1)

### bio-b04-q43 | points 1 | outcome B4.3.2 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A cook touches a hot pan and pulls her hand back before she feels pain. The impulse of the withdrawal reflex travels 1.6 m along nerve fibres (finger → spinal cord → muscle) at 80 m s⁻¹ and crosses two synapses, each with a delay of 1.5 ms.

The pain is felt only after the hand is already moving. Explain why.

#### Answer

To feel pain the impulses must travel on from the spinal cord through the white-matter tracts to the cerebral cortex — a longer path with extra synapses.

#### Solution

To feel pain the impulses must travel on from the spinal cord through the white-matter tracts to the cerebral cortex — a longer path with extra synapses. The withdrawal reflex uses the short circuit through the spinal cord, so the muscle contractions start before the conscious sensation is formed.
*Marking:* • pain needs the longer path to the brain (more synapses), the reflex uses the shorter spinal circuit (1)
