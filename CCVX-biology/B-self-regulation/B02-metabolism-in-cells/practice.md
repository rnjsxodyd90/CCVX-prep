### bio-b02-q01 | points 3 | outcome B2.1.1 | difficulty easy

#### Question

At 20:00 a cook wipes a worktop with a damp cloth. Tests show that the cloth leaves 200 *Salmonella* cells behind. The worktop stays damp at room temperature, conditions in which Salmonella can divide every 30 minutes.

Give three characteristics of a bacterial cell that distinguish it from a eukaryotic cell.

#### Answer

Bacterial cells differ from eukaryotic cells for example in their DNA organisation, their organelles and their wall.

#### Solution

Bacterial cells differ from eukaryotic cells for example in their DNA organisation, their organelles and their wall. Accept any three of: no nucleus (one circular chromosome lies free in the cytoplasm, in the nucleoid); no membrane-bound organelles (no mitochondria, ER, and so on); a cell wall of peptidoglycan instead of cellulose; smaller ribosomes than in eukaryotic cytoplasm; often plasmids, small extra rings of DNA.
*Marking:* • a first correct characteristic, with a correct contrast to a eukaryotic cell (1) • a second, different correct characteristic (1) • a third, different correct characteristic (1)

### bio-b02-q07 | points 3 | outcome B2.1.4 | difficulty hard

#### Question

The Na⁺/K⁺ pump in the membrane of a heart muscle cell moves 3 Na⁺ out of the cell and 2 K⁺ into the cell for every ATP it splits. Ions also leak back slowly through ion channels. The plant poison ouabain blocks the Na⁺/K⁺ pump specifically.

The Na⁺ and K⁺ concentrations inside a healthy heart muscle cell stay roughly constant for years. Explain how the pump and the leak together cause this, and name the state the ion concentrations are in.

#### Answer

The pump continuously transports Na⁺ out and K⁺ in, at a rate that exactly balances the leakage in the opposite direction; the ATP costs are met by dissimilation.

#### Solution

The pump continuously transports Na⁺ out and K⁺ in, at a rate that exactly balances the leakage in the opposite direction; the ATP costs are met by dissimilation. Inflow thus equals outflow at a constant level: a dynamic equilibrium (a steady state of an open system).
*Marking:* • leak balanced by active pumping against the gradients (1) • it costs ATP (1) • dynamic equilibrium (1)

### bio-b02-q29 | points 3 | outcome B2.3.5 | difficulty hard

#### Question

During a 400 m sprint the leg muscles cannot get enough O₂ at the rate ATP is used. Toward the end of the race the runner feels a burning sensation in her legs; lactic acid concentration in the muscle cells has risen. Explain why fermentation yields so much less ATP per glucose than aerobic dissimilation, and name the role of O₂ in this.

#### Answer

Without O₂ only glycolysis runs (2 ATP net): O₂ is the final electron acceptor of the electron transport chain, so without it oxidative phosphorylation and the citric acid cycle stop, and fermentation only regenerates NAD⁺ without yielding ATP.

#### Solution

Without O₂ only glycolysis runs, netting 2 ATP per glucose.
