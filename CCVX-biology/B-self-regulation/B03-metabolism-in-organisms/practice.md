### bio-b03-q09 | points 3 | outcome B3.3.3 | difficulty hard

#### Question

A freediver hyperventilates for two minutes before diving under, hoping to stay down longer. At a depth of ten metres he suddenly loses consciousness and has to be rescued.

Describe how a rise in the CO₂ content of the blood normally leads to faster and deeper breathing: name the receptors, the control centre and the executive organs.

#### Answer

When the CO₂ content of the blood rises, CO₂ forms carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), so the pH falls.

#### Solution

When the CO₂ content of the blood rises, CO₂ forms carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), so the pH falls. Chemoreceptors — in the brainstem and in bodies on the aortic arch and the carotid arteries — detect this and fire more impulses. The respiratory centre in the brainstem (medulla) then sends more impulses along motor nerves to the respiratory muscles, the diaphragm and the intercostal muscles, which work faster and deeper; the extra ventilation blows the surplus CO₂ off again.
*Marking:* • CO₂ lowers the pH, detected by chemoreceptors (aortic arch/carotid or brainstem) (1) • respiratory centre in the brainstem coordinates (1) • more impulses to diaphragm and intercostal muscles → faster, deeper breathing (1)
