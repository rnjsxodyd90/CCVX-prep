### bio-e01-q01 | points 1 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty easy

#### Question

Bacteria are grown for many generations on a medium with the heavy nitrogen isotope ¹⁵N, so all their DNA is "heavy". They are then moved to a medium with normal ¹⁴N. After each cell division, DNA is extracted and centrifuged in a CsCl gradient, where heavy, intermediate and light DNA collect in separate bands.

State what is meant by semi-conservative replication.

#### Answer

Each new DNA molecule consists of one old (template) strand and one newly made strand.

#### Solution

Each new DNA molecule consists of one old (template) strand and one newly made strand: the two strands of the parent molecule separate and each acts as the template for a new complementary strand.
*Marking:* • one old strand plus one new strand in each daughter molecule (1)

### bio-e01-q02 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty easy

#### Question

Bacteria are grown for many generations on a medium with the heavy nitrogen isotope ¹⁵N, so all their DNA is "heavy". They are then moved to a medium with normal ¹⁴N. In which band(s) does the DNA collect after exactly one division on ¹⁴N? Explain your answer.

All DNA collects in the intermediate band.

#### Solution

All DNA collects in the intermediate band. After one division every DNA molecule contains one original ¹⁵N strand and one new ¹⁴N strand (semi-conservative replication), so every molecule has intermediate density; no heavy and no light DNA is present.
*Marking:* • only the intermediate band (1) • every molecule has one ¹⁵N and one ¹⁴N strand (1)

### bio-e01-q03 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Bacteria are grown for many generations on a medium with the heavy nitrogen isotope ¹⁵N, so all their DNA is "heavy". They are then moved to a medium with normal ¹⁴N. After each cell division, DNA is extracted and centrifuged in a CsCl gradient, where heavy, intermediate and light DNA collect in separate bands.

One original DNA molecule has now been replicated four times, giving 16 DNA molecules. Calculate how many of these 16 molecules still contain an original ¹⁵N strand, and calculate what percentage of the 16 molecules that is.

#### Answer

2 molecules; 2/16 × 100% = 12.5%.

#### Solution

2 molecules; 2/16 × 100% = 12.5%. The original molecule contributed exactly 2 (¹⁵N) strands. ### bio-e01-q04 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Bacteria are grown for many generations on a medium with the heavy nitrogen isotope ¹⁵N, so all their DNA is "heavy". They are then moved to a medium with normal ¹⁴N. After each cell division, DNA is extracted and centrifuged in a CsCl gradient, where heavy, intermediate and light DNA collect in separate bands.

After three divisions, only 2 of the 8 DNA molecules contain a ¹⁵N strand. A student concludes: "The old DNA is gradually broken down during replication." Assess this claim.

#### Answer

The claim is wrong.

#### Solution

The claim is wrong. The original strands are not broken down: they are conserved. Each of the two ¹⁵N strands remains intact inside one DNA molecule; they are only divided ("diluted") over an ever larger number of molecules as new strands are made around them.
*Marking:* • claim rejected (1) • original strands stay intact, they are only distributed over more molecules (1)

### bio-e01-q05 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty hard

#### Question

Bacteria are grown for many generations on a medium with the heavy nitrogen isotope ¹⁵N, so all their DNA is "heavy". They are then moved to a medium with normal ¹⁴N. After each cell division, DNA is extracted and centrifuged in a CsCl gradient, where heavy, intermediate and light DNA collect in separate bands.

Before semi-conservative replication was proven, some biologists proposed conservative replication: the whole old molecule is kept together and a completely new molecule is made next to it. Describe what this model predicts for the band pattern after one division on ¹⁴N, and explain how the actual result rules the model out.

#### Answer

Conservative replication predicts 50% heavy and 50% light DNA, with no intermediate band.

#### Solution

Conservative replication predicts 50% heavy and 50% light DNA, with no intermediate band: the unchanged all-¹⁵N parent molecule would remain heavy, and the newly made all-¹⁴N molecule would be light. The actual result — all DNA in one intermediate band after one division — does not match this, so it rules out the conservative model (and supports semi-conservative replication).
*Marking:* • prediction: heavy + light bands, no intermediate (1) • observed all-intermediate result contradicts it / rules the model out (1)

### bio-e01-q06 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

The drawing below shows a replication fork that is opening towards the right. A and B are the two parental strands.

```
                       fork →
 strand A    3' ───────────────┐
                                \
                                 )
                                /
 strand B    5' ───────────────┘
```

A new strand is built on each of the two strands. State for strand A and for strand B whether the new strand is a leading or a lagging strand, and explain how you can tell.

#### Answer

On strand A the leading strand is built; on strand B the lagging strand.

#### Solution

On strand A the leading strand is built; on strand B the lagging strand. On strand A (which runs 3' → 5' towards the fork) the new strand can grow 5' → 3' towards the fork, so it is made continuously. On strand B the new strand must also grow 5' → 3', which here is away from the fork, so it can only be made in pieces (Okazaki fragments).
*Marking:* • A → leading, B → lagging (1) • explanation: new strand is built 5' → 3', towards the fork on A but away from the fork on B (1)

### bio-e01-q07 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Five events at a replication fork are given in a random order:

a. DNA polymerase adds free DNA nucleotides to the 3' end of the primer.
b. DNA ligase joins the fragments of DNA into one continuous strand.
c. Helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds between the base pairs.
d. Primase builds a short RNA primer on the template strand.
e. The RNA primers are removed and replaced by DNA.

Give the events in the correct order.

#### Answer

c → d → a → e → b.

#### Solution

c → d → a → e → b. First the strands are separated (c), then a primer is laid (d), only then can the polymerase add nucleotides (a); when the fragments are finished their primers are replaced by DNA (e) and the remaining gaps are sealed by ligase (b).
*Marking:* • c first, and d before a (1) • correct closing order a → e → b (1)

### bio-e01-q08 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

In a cell, DNA polymerase cannot start a new DNA strand on its own; primase must first make a short RNA primer.

Explain why DNA polymerase needs this primer, and why it has to be the 3' end of the primer that is free.

#### Answer

DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides to a free 3' end of an existing strand; it cannot start a strand from nothing.

#### Solution

DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides to a free 3' end of an existing strand; it cannot start a strand from nothing. The enzyme connects each new nucleotide to the 3'-OH group of the last nucleotide, so synthesis can only proceed 5' → 3' and the primer must expose a free 3' end.
*Marking:* • polymerase extends an existing strand only / cannot start de novo (1) • it attaches new nucleotides to the free 3' end, building 5' → 3' (1)

#### Question

A student explains the difference between the leading and the lagging strand as follows: "The leading strand is built in the 5' → 3' direction, but on the lagging strand the DNA polymerase works in the 3' → 5' direction, so it has to work in pieces."

Is this explanation correct? Assess it.

The explanation is wrong.

### bio-e01-q10 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

### bio-e01-q11 | points 3 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty hard

#### Question

A human cell replicates its diploid genome of 6.4 × 10⁹ base pairs during an S phase of 8 hours. At one replication fork, new DNA is made at a rate of 50 base pairs per second (the fork advances 50 bp per second). Assume that every fork works during the whole S phase.

Show with a calculation that at least about 4400 replication forks must be active at the same time.

#### Answer

One fork copies 50 × 28,800 = 1.44 × 10⁶ bp in 8 hours; 6.4 × 10⁹ / 1.44 × 10⁶ ≈ 4444 forks.

#### Solution

One fork copies 50 × 28,800 = 1.44 × 10⁶ bp in 8 hours; 6.4 × 10⁹ / 1.44 × 10⁶ ≈ 4444 forks. Working: 8 hours = 8 × 3600 = 28,800 s. Per fork: 50 bp/s × 28,800 s = 1.44 × 10⁶ bp. Required number of forks: 6.4 × 10⁹ / 1.44 × 10⁶ ≈ 4444, so at least about 4400 forks must work simultaneously (about 2200 origins, two forks per origin).
*Marking:* • 8 h converted to 28,800 s (1) • 1.44 × 10⁶ bp per fork (1) • 6.4 × 10⁹ / 1.44 × 10⁶ ≈ 4444, matching the claim (1)

### bio-e01-q12 | points 2 | outcome E1.1 | difficulty easy

#### Question

The DNA of chromosome 1 would take about a month to copy if replication started from a single origin of replication, yet the complete S phase of a human cell takes only a few hours.

### bio-e01-q15 | points 2 | outcome E1.2 | difficulty medium

#### Question

In a laboratory, cultured human skin cells are exposed to controlled doses of UV-B radiation, after which the mutation frequency in a chosen gene (the HPRT gene) is measured.

Explain why a thymine dimer that is replicated before it is repaired becomes a permanent mutation, while the same dimer repaired in time leaves no trace.

#### Answer

During replication the polymerase inserts bases opposite the dimer that are not complementary; after replication the "wrong" bases are normally paired, so repair enzymes no longer recognise an error.

#### Solution

During replication the polymerase inserts bases opposite the dimer that are not complementary (it cannot read the damaged bases); after replication the "wrong" bases are normally paired in the daughter molecules, so repair enzymes no longer recognise an error.
