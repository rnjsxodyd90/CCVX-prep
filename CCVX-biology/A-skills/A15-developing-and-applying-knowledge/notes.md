## What the skill means

- Analysing how scientific and technological knowledge is developed and applied. *(CvTE A15)*
- Knowledge grows in a cycle: an observation raises a question, a hypothesis is tested by experiment or observation, and the conclusion leads to new questions. A result counts once others can check and repeat it, which is why research is published and reviewed by other scientists ([A05](../A05-research/README.md)).

## How knowledge was developed

- Darwin and Wallace arrived at natural selection independently; their papers were presented together in 1858, and Darwin's On the Origin of Species followed in 1859 ([F02](../../F-evolution/F02-speciation/README.md)).
- Hershey and Chase (1952) labelled the DNA of bacterial viruses with ³²P and their protein with ³⁵S: the ³²P went into the bacteria while most of the ³⁵S stayed outside, so DNA carries the genetic information ([F01](../../F-evolution/F01-selection/README.md)). Watson and Crick's DNA model of 1953 rested partly on Rosalind Franklin's X-ray photographs ([B01](../../B-self-regulation/B01-protein-synthesis/README.md)).
- Meselson and Stahl (1958) grew bacteria on heavy ¹⁵N and then on ¹⁴N: after one generation all DNA had an intermediate density, after two generations half was intermediate and half light, so replication is semi-conservative ([E01](../../E-reproduction/E01-dna-replication/README.md)).
- Miller and Urey (1953) made amino acids from simple gases and electric sparks, testing an idea about how the first organic molecules formed. The endosymbiotic theory, developed by Lynn Margulis from 1967, explains mitochondria and chloroplasts as former bacteria: they have their own circular DNA, a double membrane and bacterial-type ribosomes, and they divide by fission ([F04](../../F-evolution/F04-genesis-of-life/README.md)).

## How knowledge is applied

- Technology and knowledge drive each other: better microscopes revealed cells and organelles, and PCR and DNA sequencing made it possible to read genomes and study mutations, as the doctors in the F01 context do for radiation ([B01](../../B-self-regulation/B01-protein-synthesis/README.md), [F01](../../F-evolution/F01-selection/README.md)).
- Applications include human insulin made by genetically modified bacteria since the early 1980s ([D01](../../D-interaction/D01-molecular-interaction/README.md)), vaccines ([B05](../../B-self-regulation/B05-defence-in-organisms/README.md)), new crop varieties ([F01](../../F-evolution/F01-selection/README.md)), IVF ([E03](../../E-reproduction/E03-reproduction-of-organisms/README.md)) and stem cells to replace damaged tissue ([C01](../../C-self-organization/C01-self-organization-of-cells/README.md)).
- Applications raise questions that science alone can't answer: the Bureau GGO in the B01 context checks whether a GM crop is safe, but whether to allow it also calls for the reasoned judgment of [A09](../A09-evaluation-and-judgment/README.md).

## How it shows up in a written exam

- A context describes a historical or recent study, and the questions ask what the researchers wanted to know, which result supports which conclusion, or why a control was needed.
- **Trap:** "evolution is just a theory" confuses the everyday meaning of theory, a guess, with a scientific theory: a well-tested explanation supported by many independent lines of evidence.
