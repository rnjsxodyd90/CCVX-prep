# A6 — Design: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX syllabus, the Dutch CvTE syllabus and standard VWO biology, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question.

- **In Dutch VWO:** central exam and school exam (CvTE §1.4), always inside a content question.
- **Dutch source:** [CvTE syllabus biologie vwo 2027](https://www.examenblad.nl/2027/vwo/documenten/syllabus-biologie-vwo-2027) (version 2, July 2025), domain A *Vaardigheden*, A6 *Ontwerpen*. A citation like *(CvTE A6 spec 4)* means specification 4 of A6; the CvTE has 10 specifications against 9 CCVX items, so they are matched by content.

Dutch terms are in brackets. Binas tables are named, not numbered, because the numbers differ between editions.

## 1. Analysing and describing a design problem

- A design problem (*ontwerpprobleem*) asks for a product or method that does a job, not for new knowledge; describing it means saying what the design must achieve, for whom and under which conditions. *(CvTE A6 spec 1)*
- Running example: patients whose kidneys have failed need a machine that removes urea and other waste from their blood, haemodialysis or an artificial kidney ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)).
- Design contexts in the CCVX specimens: the clap skate, whose hinge at the front lets the blade stay on the ice while the heel lifts, so the calf muscles add to the push-off ([B06](../../B-self-regulation/B06-movement-of-the-organism/README.md)); building with nature to protect the coast ([C03](../../C-self-organization/C03-self-organization-of-ecosystems/README.md)); a greenhouse that uses less energy ([D05](../../D-interaction/D05-interaction-in-ecosystems/README.md)).

## 2. Schedule of requirements and preferences

- CCVX's "schedule of requirements" is usually called a list of requirements or design specification (*programma van eisen*): requirements (*eisen*) the design must meet and preferences (*wensen*) that are welcome but not essential. *(CvTE A6 spec 3)*
- For the dialysis machine, requirements: urea and excess K⁺ leave the blood, blood cells and proteins stay in, glucose and salts stay at their normal levels, the blood doesn't clot, and everything that touches the blood is sterile. Preferences: small, quiet, cheap, short sessions.
- **Trap:** a design that fails a requirement is rejected, one that misses a preference is only less attractive; and a requirement must be testable, so "removes urea" should say how much in what time.

## 3. Scientific knowledge behind functions and properties

- Each property follows from biology or physics. The membrane's pores let small molecules such as urea through but not blood cells and proteins; hollow fibres give a large area; dialysis fluid without urea keeps the concentration gradient, and with glucose and salts at normal blood levels it stops them from diffusing out (Fick's law, [B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)). *(CvTE A6 spec 4)*
- The dialysis fluid flows opposite to the blood: the countercurrent principle (*tegenstroomprincipe*), which CCVX calls the counterflow principle, keeps a gradient along the whole membrane.
- This is one of the three design items tested in the Dutch central exam.

## 4. Several versions

- Before choosing, think of several solutions (*meerdere uitwerkingen of oplossingen*) for each function: flat sheets or hollow fibres as membrane, the fluid flowing the same way as the blood or against it. *(CvTE A6 spec 5)*

## 5. A reasoned design proposal

- A design proposal (*ontwerpvoorstel*) picks one combination and argues for it with the list of requirements and their priorities: countercurrent hollow fibres remove urea fastest in a small machine. *(CvTE A6 spec 6)*

## 6. Building a prototype

- A prototype (*prototype*) is a first working version, built to test the design. *(CvTE A6 spec 7)*
- A written exam can't ask you to build one, and CCVX leaves out what can't be tested in writing *(CCVX syllabus §1)*.

## 7. Testing and evaluating

- Testing checks the design against each requirement, for instance by measuring the urea concentration in the blood before and after a session; evaluating also looks back at how the design process went. *(CvTE A6 spec 8)*

## 8. Proposals for improvement

- An improvement tackles a weakness found in testing and explains with biology why it would help: if urea leaves too slowly, a larger or thinner membrane, or a faster flow of fresh dialysis fluid, speeds up diffusion. *(CvTE A6 spec 9)*
- This is one of the three design items tested in the Dutch central exam.
- **Trap:** "use a better membrane" is not an improvement; say what changes and why that solves the weakness.

## 9. Presenting a design

- Presenting the design process and product (*ontwerpproces en -product presenteren*), for example in a report or a demonstration. *(CvTE A6 spec 10)*

## CCVX versus the Dutch syllabus

- **In both:** all nine CCVX items, matched by content to CvTE specs 1 and 3–10, and priorities (*prioriteiten*). The Dutch central exam tests only CCVX items 1, 3 and 8 (CvTE specs 1, 4 and 9); the CvTE prints the others grey. *(CvTE A6)*
- **Dutch syllabus only:** splitting the problem into sub-problems (*deelproblemen*, spec 2) and boundary conditions (*randvoorwaarden*, spec 3), conditions set from outside such as the budget or the law; both printed grey.
- **CCVX only:** nothing.
