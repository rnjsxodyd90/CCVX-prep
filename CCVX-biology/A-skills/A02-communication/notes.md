# A2 — Communication: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX syllabus, the Dutch CvTE syllabus and standard VWO biology, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question.

- **In Dutch VWO:** central exam and school exam (CvTE §1.4), always inside a content question.
- **Dutch source:** [CvTE syllabus biologie vwo 2027](https://www.examenblad.nl/2027/vwo/documenten/syllabus-biologie-vwo-2027) (version 2, July 2025), domain A, A2 *Communiceren*, and appendix 5 *Examen(werk)woorden*. A1–A4 have only an attainment target (*eindterm*), which the CvTE doesn't specify further; a citation like *(CvTE A2)* means that attainment target.

Dutch terms are in brackets. Binas tables are named, not numbered, because the numbers differ between editions.

## What the skill means

- Communicating (*communiceren*) effectively about biology in writing, in speech and digitally, in public (*in het publieke domein*). *(CvTE A2)*
- A written exam tests only the written part *(CCVX syllabus §1)*: a clear answer in correct biological terms (*vaktaal*) that does exactly what the question's verb asks. CCVX says its questions are of the same type as the Dutch CvTE ones, so the CvTE meanings of the exam verbs are the ones to learn.

## Exam verbs

- The CvTE fixes the meaning of the exam verbs (*examenwerkwoorden*) in appendix 5; the list is not complete. *(CvTE appendix 5)*

| Verb | What the answer must contain |
|---|---|
| give, name, list (*(aan)geven*, *(be)noemen*, *noteren*, *aanvullen*) | a short answer: a word or phrase, without explanation unless asked |
| describe (*beschrijven*) | full sentences that give the parts asked for |
| argue, reason (*beargumenteren*, *beredeneren*) | data and biological knowledge combined logically to support a choice or statement |
| justify briefly (*toelichten*) | one or a few sentences supporting a choice or statement |
| explain (*uitleggen*, *verklaren*, *aangeven waardoor*) | a cause–effect relationship: *uitleggen* for several steps, *verklaren* or *aangeven waardoor* for one |
| indicate why (*aangeven waarom*) | a reason or a purpose |
| determine (*bepalen*) | the result found or worked out from the data, showing the measurements or values used and the steps, with unit unless the question gives it, rounded as asked |
| calculate (*berekenen*) | the values used and the steps, with unit unless the question gives it, rounded as asked |
| draw (*tekenen*) | an accurate enough drawing; a graph with scaled axes, the independent variable on the x-axis, quantities and units where possible; a food web with the organisms apart as far as possible and arrows in the direction of the energy flow |

- The verbs in the specifications are a different set: the CvTE sorts them into three levels of mastery (*beheersingsniveaus*), naming (*benoemen*), explaining (*verklaren*) and arguing (*beargumenteren*). An exam verb doesn't have to match that level, and a level-1 verb in a complex context can still be a hard question. *(CvTE §2.4)*

## How it shows up in a written exam

- "Explain why a red blood cell bursts in distilled water" asks for several steps: the cell contents have a higher osmotic value than the water, so water enters by osmosis, the cell swells, and without a cell wall the membrane bursts ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)).
- Use the terms of the syllabus and of the context, write complete chains rather than keywords, and give exactly as many answers as asked.
- **Trap:** answering "explain" (*uitleggen*) with only the last step ("because of osmosis") leaves out the cause–effect chain the verb asks for.
