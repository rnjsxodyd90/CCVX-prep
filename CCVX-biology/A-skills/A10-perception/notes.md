## What the skill means

- Putting into words the feelings and meanings that nature, or objects from nature, evoke, and paying attention to the feelings and meanings of other people. *(CvTE A10)*
- CCVX's title "Perception" translates *Beleven*, experiencing; it is about how people experience nature, not about the senses, which are [B07](../../B-self-regulation/B07-perception-by-organisms/README.md).
- CCVX leaves out what can't be tested in writing *(CCVX syllabus §1)*, and a feeling can't be marked right or wrong. What a written question can use is the link with [A09](../A09-evaluation-and-judgment/README.md) item 2: recognising which statements express a feeling or meaning, and keeping them apart from scientific arguments.

## Meanings people give to nature

- People value nature for its use (food, clean water, protection against flooding), for its beauty and the rest it gives, and for its own sake, apart from any use to humans (intrinsic value, *intrinsieke waarde*).
- The C03 world-view context, municipal biologists researching public attitudes to nature for land-use plans ([C03](../../C-self-organization/C03-self-organization-of-ecosystems/README.md)), is in Dutch research into *natuurbeleving van burgers*, how citizens experience nature. *(CvTE appendix 4)*
- The F03 world-view context, a conservation group discussing the claim in Bas Haring's book Plastic Panda's that species extinction is not a problem ([F03](../../F-evolution/F03-biodiversity/README.md)), is a discussion where scientific arguments, values and feelings about nature meet.
- **Trap:** "a forest makes people calm" describes an experience; it becomes a scientific claim only once it is measured, for example in a study of stress levels.
