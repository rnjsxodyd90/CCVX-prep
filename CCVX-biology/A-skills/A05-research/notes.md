# A5 — Research: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX syllabus, the Dutch CvTE syllabus and standard VWO biology, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question.

- **In Dutch VWO:** central exam and school exam (CvTE §1.4), always inside a content question.
- **Dutch source:** [CvTE syllabus biologie vwo 2027](https://www.examenblad.nl/2027/vwo/documenten/syllabus-biologie-vwo-2027) (version 2, July 2025), domain A *Vaardigheden*, A5 *Onderzoeken*. A citation like *(CvTE A5 spec 9)* means specification 9 of A5; here the numbers match the CCVX numbers.

Dutch terms are in brackets. Binas tables are named, not numbered, because the numbers differ between editions.

## 1. Recognising and specifying a scientific problem

- A scientific problem (*natuurwetenschappelijk probleem*) can be answered with observations or measurements; specifying it means saying exactly what is unknown, in which organism and under which conditions. *(CvTE A5 spec 1)*
- Example: a tomato grower wants a higher winter yield ([D05](../../D-interaction/D05-interaction-in-ecosystems/README.md)). Specified: which factor, light, CO₂ or temperature, limits the photosynthesis of these plants in winter ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md))?
- **Trap:** "should growers heat their greenhouses?" is not a scientific problem, because the answer also depends on costs and values ([A09](../A09-evaluation-and-judgment/README.md)).

## 2. From problem to research question

- A research question (*onderzoeksvraag*) asks for the effect of an independent variable (*onafhankelijke variabele*), which the researcher changes, on a dependent variable (*afhankelijke variabele*), which is measured. *(CvTE A5 spec 2)*
- Example: "what is the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis of pondweed?", measured as O₂ bubbles per minute, with temperature, CO₂ and the plant kept the same.
- **Trap:** changing two variables at once, such as light and temperature, makes it impossible to tell which one caused the effect.

## 3. Linking the question to scientific knowledge

- Knowledge says what to measure and what to expect: O₂ comes from the light reaction, so the O₂ released measures net photosynthesis, which is gross photosynthesis minus the plant's own dissimilation ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)). *(CvTE A5 spec 3)*
- Knowledge of limiting factors predicts that the rate rises with light intensity until another factor, such as CO₂, becomes limiting ([B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md)).

## 4. Hypothesis and expectations

- A hypothesis (*hypothese*) is a testable answer to the research question, based on knowledge: "more light gives a higher rate of photosynthesis, up to a maximum". An expectation (*verwachting*) says what the measurements will show if the hypothesis is right: "the number of bubbles per minute rises as the lamp moves closer, then levels off". *(CvTE A5 spec 4)*
- A hypothesis must be falsifiable: some possible result would show it to be wrong.
- **Trap:** a hypothesis is a statement, not a question; and an expectation describes measurements, not an explanation.

## 5. Work plan

- A work plan (*werkplan*) gives the set-up, the values of the independent variable, what is measured, how and how often, which variables are kept constant, the number of repeats and the control. *(CvTE A5 spec 5)*
- Example: catalase activity at 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 °C, with the same volumes of liver extract and H₂O₂ solution each time, the O₂ collected in a gas syringe for 2 minutes, three repeats per temperature, and boiled liver extract as a control to show that the active enzyme releases the O₂ ([B02](../../B-self-regulation/B02-metabolism-in-cells/README.md)).
- In a trial with people, such as the trial of an appetite-inhibiting substance in [B03](../../B-self-regulation/B03-metabolism-in-organisms/README.md), the control group (*controlegroep*) gets a placebo, and in a double-blind trial neither the subjects nor the researchers know who gets which.
- **Trap:** a control is not a repeat: a control shows that the factor itself causes the effect, a repeat shows whether the result can be reproduced.

## 6. Observations and measuring data

- Observations (*waarnemingen*) and measuring data (*meetgegevens*) are recorded as they are, with units, in a table prepared beforehand; qualitative observations, such as an indicator changing colour, count as data too. *(CvTE A5 spec 6)*
- **Trap:** an observation ("the solution turned yellow") is not an interpretation ("CO₂ was produced"); keep the two apart.

## 7. Processing and presenting data

- Processing means calculating what answers the question, such as the mean of the repeats or a rate per minute; presenting means a table or graph that shows the relationship ([A08](../A08-scientific-instruments/README.md) items 2 and 5). *(CvTE A5 spec 7)*
- A line graph suits a continuous independent variable such as temperature, a bar chart (*staafdiagram*) suits categories such as plant species; the independent variable goes on the x-axis.
- **Trap:** a line drawn between the values for different species suggests in-between values that don't exist.

## 8. Drawing conclusions

- A conclusion (*conclusie*) answers the research question with the data of this study, within the range tested, for example: "in this experiment O₂ production rose from 10 to 40 °C and was lower at 50 °C". *(CvTE A5 spec 8)*
- **Trap:** "the enzyme is denatured at 50 °C" is an explanation from knowledge, not a conclusion from the data; and data for 10–50 °C say nothing about 70 °C.

## 9. Evaluating: validity and reliability

- Validity (*validiteit*): does the study measure what it is meant to? If the lamp also warms the water, temperature changes along with light; a heat filter or water bath restores validity. *(CvTE A5 spec 9)*
- Reliability (*betrouwbaarheid*): would a repeat give the same result? More repeats and larger samples make a result more reliable.
- The Dutch syllabus adds accuracy (*nauwkeurigheid*): collecting O₂ in a graduated syringe is more accurate than counting bubbles of different sizes.
- **Trap:** repeating a flawed experiment makes the result more reliable, but not more valid.

## 10. Presenting a study

- A report (*verslag*) or poster follows the steps of the study, from research question and hypothesis via method and results to conclusion and discussion, so that others can check and repeat it. *(CvTE A5 spec 10)*
- This item is printed grey in the Dutch syllabus: it is not tested in the central exam.

## 11. Approaches other than experiments

- Some questions can't be answered with an experiment. Other approaches are field observations and counts, comparing DNA or anatomy between species ([F02](../../F-evolution/F02-speciation/README.md)), twin studies ([D03](../../D-interaction/D03-behaviour-and-interaction/README.md)), large population studies such as a Chlamydia screen ([D04](../../D-interaction/D04-sexuality/README.md)), and models ([A07](../A07-modelling/README.md)). *(CvTE A5 spec 11)*
- In these studies the researcher doesn't control the variables, so a link such as the one between childhood sunburn and later skin cancer ([E01](../../E-reproduction/E01-dna-replication/README.md)) needs extra arguments, such as a known mechanism, before it counts as cause and effect.
- **Trap:** a correlation alone doesn't prove a cause: a third factor may cause both.

## 12. Uncertainty and probability

- Research results always carry uncertainty (*onzekerheid*): measurements have errors and samples are limited, so a conclusion is stated in terms of probability (*waarschijnlijkheid*): "the data support the hypothesis". *(CvTE A5 spec 12)*
- One reliable result against a hypothesis can refute it, but no number of results for it proves it for good.
- **Trap:** "this proves that …" overstates a result; write "this supports" or "this makes it likely that".

## CCVX versus the Dutch syllabus

- **In both:** all twelve items, with the same numbers, but the CvTE prints item 10 grey, so it isn't tested in the Dutch central exam. *(CvTE A5)*
- **Dutch syllabus only:** accuracy (*nauwkeurigheid*) in item 9.
- **CCVX only:** nothing.
