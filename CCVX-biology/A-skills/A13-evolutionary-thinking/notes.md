## What the skill means

- Explaining biological phenomena at any level of organisation with the theory of evolutionary mechanisms: how variation arises, how selection acts on it, and how new species form. *(CvTE A13)*

## How genetic variation arises

- Variation in a population comes from mutation (new alleles), recombination in meiosis and fertilisation (new combinations of alleles) and gene flow (alleles arriving from other populations). *(CvTE A13 spec 1)*
- Genetic drift changes allele frequencies by chance, most strongly in small populations, as after a founder or bottleneck effect ([F02](../../F-evolution/F02-speciation/README.md)).
- Mutations are random with respect to their use: a resistance mutation arises by chance, not because an antibiotic is present, and the antibiotic only selects the resistant bacteria ([F01](../../F-evolution/F01-selection/README.md)).

## Selection and adaptation

- Selection pressure (*selectiedruk*) acts on heritable variation: more offspring are born than can survive, and individuals whose traits suit the environment better leave more offspring, so over generations the favourable alleles and the adaptations they cause become more common. *(CvTE A13 spec 2)*
- Example: in a hospital, antibiotics kill the sensitive bacteria while resistant ones survive and multiply, so the share of resistant bacteria rises (F02 context, [F02](../../F-evolution/F02-speciation/README.md)).
- What is favoured depends on the environment: the sickle-cell allele causes anaemia in homozygotes, but heterozygotes are better protected against malaria, so the allele stays common where malaria occurs.

## Reproductive isolation and speciation

- A new species forms when populations no longer exchange genes (reproductive isolation) and change separately by mutation, selection and drift, until they can no longer produce fertile offspring together. *(CvTE A13 spec 3)*
- Allopatric speciation follows geographic separation, as in Darwin's finches on different Galápagos islands; sympatric speciation happens in the same area, for example by polyploidy in plants ([F02](../../F-evolution/F02-speciation/README.md)).
- Common descent shows in homologous structures and in the universal genetic code, which CCVX names as a scientific argument for the kinship of all life ([F-evolution](../../F-evolution/README.md)).
- **Trap:** "the bacteria became resistant in order to survive" or "giraffes stretched their necks, so their young had longer necks" is goal-directed or Lamarckian reasoning; individuals don't adapt genetically, a population changes because individuals with certain alleles leave more offspring.
