# A9 — Evaluation and Judgment: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX syllabus, the Dutch CvTE syllabus and standard VWO biology, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question.

- **In Dutch VWO:** central exam and school exam (CvTE §1.4), always inside a content question.
- **Dutch source:** [CvTE syllabus biologie vwo 2027](https://www.examenblad.nl/2027/vwo/documenten/syllabus-biologie-vwo-2027) (version 2, July 2025), domain A *Vaardigheden*, A9 *Waarderen en oordelen*. A citation like *(CvTE A9 spec 2)* means specification 2 of A9; here the numbers match the CCVX numbers.

Dutch terms are in brackets. Binas tables are named, not numbered, because the numbers differ between editions.

## 1. A reasoned judgment or choice

- A reasoned judgment (*beargumenteerd oordeel*) weighs arguments from scientific knowledge and data before reaching a conclusion or choosing between alternatives. *(CvTE A9 spec 1)*
- Example: a grower chooses between a chemical pesticide (*bestrijdingsmiddel*) and biological control with natural enemies of the pest. A persistent, fat-soluble pesticide accumulates in food chains ([D05](../../D-interaction/D05-interaction-in-ecosystems/README.md)), and spraying selects for resistant pests ([F02](../../F-evolution/F02-speciation/README.md)); biological control leaves no residues, but works more slowly and only against specific pests. *(CvTE P3.2 specs 3–4)*
- **Trap:** "pesticides are bad" is an opinion, not a reasoned judgment; name the mechanism and the evidence.

## 2. Scientific arguments, social considerations and personal views

- A scientific argument (*wetenschappelijk argument*) rests on facts and testable knowledge; a normative social consideration (*normatieve maatschappelijke overweging*) rests on what a society values, such as safety, fairness or cost; a personal view (*persoonlijke opvatting*) is what one person believes or prefers. *(CvTE A9 spec 2)*
- For interventions in heredity and reproduction, such as IVF with embryo selection or cloning, CCVX and the CvTE use the pair biological argument (*biologisch argument*) and ethical argument (*ethisch argument*, [E03](../../E-reproduction/E03-reproduction-of-organisms/README.md)). *(CvTE M7.1 spec 6; CvTE O9.1 spec 8)*
- Example: "embryo selection can prevent a child being born with a serious hereditary disease" is a biological argument; "parents should not choose which embryo is implanted" is an ethical one.
- **Trap:** words such as "should", "acceptable" or "unnatural" mark a normative or personal statement, not a scientific one.

## 3. Citing sources for facts

- Supporting facts with their source (*feiten met bronnen verantwoorden*): in an exam, refer to the figure, table or text that shows the fact ("figure 3 shows that …"); in a report, give a reference list. *(CvTE A9 spec 3)*
- **Trap:** "research shows" without saying which research can't be checked.

## 4. Reliability and value of information

- Judging the reliability (*betrouwbaarheid*) of information: who wrote it and with what interest, whether it rests on research, how large and well controlled that research was, and whether it really answers the question. *(CvTE A9 spec 4)*
- A result found in rats or in the laboratory doesn't automatically hold for people or in the field, and a small study without a control group carries little weight ([A05](../A05-research/README.md)).
- CCVX specimen contexts where this fits: newspaper reports on antibiotics in livestock farming ([B05](../../B-self-regulation/B05-defence-in-organisms/README.md)), Greenpeace literature on possible disadvantages of GMOs ([E03](../../E-reproduction/E03-reproduction-of-organisms/README.md)), and the claim in Bas Haring's book Plastic Panda's that species extinction is not a problem ([F03](../../F-evolution/F03-biodiversity/README.md)).
- **Trap:** a source with an interest, such as a campaign group or a company, isn't automatically wrong; judge its evidence.
