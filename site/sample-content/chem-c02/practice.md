# C2 — Chemical Calculations — practice

Eight original questions on the two C02 outcomes. Molar masses follow Binas table 99; gas volumes use V<sub>m</sub> = 24.5 L mol⁻¹ at 298 K and p₀ (Binas table 7).

### chem-c02-q01 | points 3 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A student weighs out 25.0 g of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). Calculate the amount of CaCO₃ in moles. Use M(CaCO₃) = 100.09 g mol⁻¹.
#### Answer
0.250 mol CaCO₃
#### Solution
1. The amount of substance follows from n = m / M.
2. Substitute the values: n(CaCO₃) = 25.0 g / 100.09 g mol⁻¹ = 0.2498 mol.
3. The mass is given in 3 significant figures, so the answer is 0.250 mol.

### chem-c02-q02 | points 3 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
You dissolve 5.844 g of sodium chloride (NaCl) in water and make the solution up to exactly 250.0 mL. Calculate the concentration of NaCl in mol L⁻¹. Use M(NaCl) = 58.44 g mol⁻¹.
#### Answer
0.4000 mol L⁻¹
#### Solution
1. First convert the mass to moles: n(NaCl) = 5.844 g / 58.44 g mol⁻¹ = 0.1000 mol.
2. Concentration follows from c = n / V with V = 250.0 mL = 0.2500 L.
3. c(NaCl) = 0.1000 mol / 0.2500 L = 0.4000 mol L⁻¹.

### chem-c02-q03 | points 2 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
You pipette 10.0 mL of the 0.4000 mol L⁻¹ NaCl solution from question chem-c02-q02 into a 100.0 mL volumetric flask and fill it with water to the mark. Calculate the concentration of the diluted solution.
#### Answer
0.0400 mol L⁻¹
#### Solution
1. The amount of NaCl in the pipette: n = c × V = 0.4000 mol L⁻¹ × 0.0100 L = 0.00400 mol.
2. This amount ends up in 100.0 mL = 0.1000 L.
3. c = 0.00400 mol / 0.1000 L = 0.0400 mol L⁻¹. The shortcut c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ gives the same result.

### chem-c02-q04 | points 2 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
A solution has [H₃O⁺] = 2.5·10⁻³ mol L⁻¹. Calculate the pH. Give the right number of decimals.
#### Answer
pH = 2.60
#### Solution
1. pH = −log [H₃O⁺].
2. pH = −log(2.5·10⁻³) = 2.6021.
3. A pH gets as many decimals as the concentration has significant figures (two here), so pH = 2.60.

### chem-c02-q05 | points 4 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Ammonia is made with the Haber-Bosch reaction: N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) → 2 NH₃(g). You let 14.0 g of nitrogen react with 4.00 g of hydrogen. Show which starting material is in deficiency, and calculate the maximum mass of ammonia that can form. Use M(N₂) = 28.02, M(H₂) = 2.016 and M(NH₃) = 17.03 g mol⁻¹.
#### Answer
Nitrogen is in deficiency; maximum 17.0 g NH₃.
#### Solution
1. Convert both masses to moles: n(N₂) = 14.0 / 28.02 = 0.4996 mol; n(H₂) = 4.00 / 2.016 = 1.984 mol.
2. The equation needs 3 mol H₂ per 1 mol N₂, so 0.4996 mol N₂ needs 1.499 mol H₂. You have 1.984 mol, which is more than enough: H₂ is in excess and N₂ is in deficiency.
3. Calculate the product from the substance in deficiency: n(NH₃) = 2 × 0.4996 = 0.9993 mol.
4. Convert to mass: m(NH₃) = 0.9993 mol × 17.03 g mol⁻¹ = 17.0 g.

### chem-c02-q06 | points 2 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
The experiment from question chem-c02-q05 actually produces 13.5 g of ammonia instead of the theoretical amount. Calculate the yield as a percentage.
#### Answer
79.4%
#### Solution
1. Yield = actual output / theoretical output.
2. Yield = 13.5 g / 17.0 g = 0.794, so 79.4%.

### chem-c02-q07 | points 3 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Calcium carbonate reacts with an excess of hydrochloric acid: CaCO₃(s) + 2 H⁺(aq) → Ca²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g). Starting from 25.0 g of CaCO₃ (M = 100.09 g mol⁻¹), calculate the volume of CO₂ gas at 298 K and p₀. Use V<sub>m</sub> = 24.5 L mol⁻¹.
#### Answer
6.12 L CO₂
#### Solution
1. n(CaCO₃) = 25.0 / 100.09 = 0.2498 mol.
2. The mole ratio CaCO₃ : CO₂ is 1 : 1, so n(CO₂) = 0.2498 mol.
3. V = n × V<sub>m</sub> = 0.2498 mol × 24.5 L mol⁻¹ = 6.12 L. Do not round until the last step: 0.249775 mol gives 6.12 L too.

### chem-c02-q08 | points 3 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty hard
#### Question
A 5.00 kg water sample from a swimming pool contains 25 mg of fluoride. Calculate the fluoride content in mass-ppm (mg kg⁻¹).
#### Answer
5.0 ppm (5.0 mg kg⁻¹)
#### Solution
1. Mass-ppm is the mass of the substance per million parts of the total mass, which equals mg per kg.
2. content = 25 mg / 5.00 kg = 5.0 mg kg⁻¹.
3. So the fluoride content is 5.0 ppm.
