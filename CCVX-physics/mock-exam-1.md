# CCVX Physics — Mock exam 1

**Fully original mock exam**, written for this prep. Nothing is copied from CCVX or CvTE exams and no CCVX/CvTE contexts are reused. Modelled on the CCVX physics syllabus of 15 August 2025 (in force from 1 September 2025). For CCVX's own free sample exams, see the CCVX [sample exam page](https://www.ccvx.nl/VoorbeeldTentamens/VoorbeeldTentamensNatuurkunde/VoorbeeldTentamensNatuurkunde.html) (external).

## Exam rules (as in the real sitting)

- **Time: 3 hours (180 minutes).** Utrecht-style written exam, open questions; partial credit for correct steps — always show your working: formula, substitution, result with unit.
- You may use **BINAS** (5th edition or later, English edition allowed) **with no notes in it**, one simple scientific calculator (no graphing calculator), pen and pencil (pencil only for graphs), a set square and a watch (no smartwatch).
- Constants not in the question should be taken from BINAS. Use **g = 9.81 m/s²**, unless stated otherwise. Give answers with a sensible number of significant figures and always with a unit.
- This exam has **16 questions**, total **72 points**. Your grade now = 1 + 9 × (score/72). Time guide: about 2½ minutes per point.
- The [marking scheme](mock-exam-1-marking-scheme.md) gives the points per step. Check your work only after your full attempt.

### phys-mock1-q01 | points 3 | subdomain P1 | difficulty easy

#### Question

A block (m = 0.50 kg) hangs from a spring (C = 2.0·10² N/m) and oscillates harmonically with amplitude 3.0 cm.

a. Compute the period.
b. Compute the maximum speed of the block.

### phys-mock1-q02 | points 4 | subdomain P1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A car drives towards you at 28 m/s, sounding its horn at 440 Hz. The speed of sound is 343 m/s.

a. Explain, with the spacing of the wavefronts, why you hear a pitch higher than 440 Hz.
b. Compute the wavelength of the sound ahead of the car.
c. Compute the frequency you hear.

### phys-mock1-q03 | points 4 | subdomain P3 | difficulty easy

#### Question

A motorcycle's ride is recorded: uniform acceleration from 0 to 15 m/s in 5.0 s, then 10 s at 15 m/s, then a uniform deceleration to 6.0 m/s in 2.5 s, then 5.0 s at 6.0 m/s.

a. Compute the distance of each phase and the total distance.
b. Compute the average velocity over the whole ride.

### phys-mock1-q04 | points 5 | subdomain P3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A sledge (m = 12 kg) on snow is pulled by a rope with F = 6.0·10¹ N at 25° above the horizontal.

a. Compute the horizontal and the vertical component of this pull.
b. Compute the normal force of the snow on the sledge.
c. The friction force is 30 N. Compute the acceleration of the sledge.
d. The sledge starts from rest. Compute its speed after 4.0 s.

### phys-mock1-q05 | points 4 | subdomain P4 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A fireworks ball is launched straight up at 25 m/s. Neglect air resistance.

a. Compute the maximum height above the launch point.
b. Compute its speed when it passes 12 m above the launch point.
c. Compute the total time until it is back at launch height.

### phys-mock1-q06 | points 4 | subdomain P6 | difficulty medium

#### Question

An electric kettle (2.0 kW) runs for 3.0 minutes to heat 0.80 kg of water from 12 °C to boiling (100 °C; c_water = 4.18·10³ J/(kg·K)).

a. Compute the electric energy taken in.
b. Compute the heat the water needed.
c. Compute the efficiency of the kettle.

### phys-mock1-q07 | points 5 | subdomain P6 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A battery of 24 V feeds a circuit: R₁ = 8.0 Ω in series with a parallel pair R₂ = 12 Ω and R₃ = 4.0 Ω.

a. Compute the replacement resistance of the parallel pair. (Use the rule as printed in the current syllabus: 1/R_tot = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …)
b. Compute the total current delivered by the battery.
c. Compute the voltage across the parallel pair and across R₁.
d. Compute the current through R₃.

### phys-mock1-q08 | points 5 | subdomain P5 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A polar-orbiting satellite flies a circular orbit at height h = 1.30·10³ km above the Earth (M_Earth = 5.972·10²⁴ kg, R_Earth = 6.378·10⁶ m, G = 6.674·10⁻¹¹, all BINAS).

a. Show that its orbital speed follows from v = √(G·M/r), and compute that speed.
b. Compute the orbital period in seconds and in minutes.
c. Is this a geostationary orbit? Answer with a reason.

### phys-mock1-q09 | points 5 | subdomain P2 | difficulty medium

#### Question

For a thyroid treatment a patient receives iodine-131 (¹³¹₅₃I), with half-life t½ = 8.0 d and administered activity 2.5·10⁸ Bq. (1 d = 8.64·10⁴ s.)

a. Give the decay equation of I-131 to xenon (Xe).
b. Compute the activity after 16 d.
c. Compute the number of I-131 nuclei in the administered dose at administration.
d. The patient's thyroid absorbs the β electrons right there, while the γ photons are used for imaging. Explain, with the properties of both radiation kinds, why this fits the medical purpose.

### phys-mock1-q10 | points 4 | subdomain P2 | difficulty medium

#### Question

In a PET scanner a positron annihilates with an electron.

a. Describe what is produced in the annihilation of one positron with one electron (directions included).
b. Verify with E = mc² (m_e = 9.109·10⁻³¹ kg, c = 3.00·10⁸ m/s, e = 1.602·10⁻¹⁹ C) the energy of one such photon in J and in MeV.
c. State the conservation law that forces the directions of part (a).

### phys-mock1-q11 | points 5 | subdomain P8 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A sauna stove heats its stones (m = 40 kg, c = 0.90·10³ J/(kg·K), given) from 20 °C to 80 °C with a 6.0 kW electric element. The cabin has a wooden wall (λ = 0.13 W/(m·K), given) of A = 20 m² and d = 0.10 m; the temperature difference across that wall in operation is 60 K.

a. Compute the heat needed for the stones.
b. Compute the shortest possible heating time of the stones (no losses).
c. Compute the heat current through that wall in operation, and comment in one sentence on what this means for the stove's cycling.

### phys-mock1-q12 | points 4 | subdomain P8 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A sealed camping-gas canister is rated "do not expose above 50 °C". At 7.0 °C the gas is at 6.0·10⁵ Pa (absolute, given). Treat the volume as constant and the gas as ideal.

a. Compute the pressure if the canister reaches 57 °C in a car in the sun.
b. Explain with the kinetic molecular model why the pressure rises with temperature (and why the warning exists).

### phys-mock1-q13 | points 5 | subdomain P9 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A light ray goes from air into a glass plate (n = 1.55) at an angle of incidence of 58°.

a. Compute the angle of refraction inside the glass.
b. Compute the critical angle of this glass with respect to air.
c. A second ray travels *inside* the same glass and meets the glass–air boundary at 50° to the normal. Describe and justify what happens.

### phys-mock1-q14 | points 5 | subdomain P10 | difficulty hard

#### Question

The star of question type "red giant" Z has its Planck peak at λ_max = 8.3·10⁻⁷ m and radiates a total power of 110 P_sun (Sun: T = 5.8·10³ K, P_sun = 3.85·10²⁶ W).

a. Compute the surface temperature of Z (k_W = 2.898·10⁻³ m·K).
b. Using P = σ·A·T⁴, compute the ratio A_Z/A_Sun of the surface areas, and from it the ratio of the radii R_Z/R_Sun.

### phys-mock1-q15 | points 5 | subdomain P11 | difficulty hard

#### Question

An electron (m = 9.109·10⁻³¹ kg) is confined in an infinitely deep one-dimensional box of width L = 5.0·10⁻¹⁰ m.

a. Compute the ground-state energy in J and in eV (h = 6.626·10⁻³⁴ J·s).
b. Compute the energy of state n = 3 and the wavelength of the photon emitted when the electron falls from n = 3 to the ground state (state the spectral region).

### phys-mock1-q16 | points 5 | subdomain P7 | difficulty hard

#### Question

An electron moves at v = 1.2·10⁷ m/s perpendicular into a homogeneous magnetic field B = 8.0·10⁻³ T (m_e and e from BINAS).

a. Compute the Lorentz force on the electron.
b. Compute the radius of its path, using F_L = B·q·v as centripetal force.
c. Explain, with the direction of the Lorentz force, why the path is a circle and why the speed does not change.
