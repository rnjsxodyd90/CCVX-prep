# P8 — Matter, heat and gases

- **Dutch:** *eigenschappen van stoffen en materialen (thermische leer)*
- **CCVX syllabus:** subdomain E1 — *Eigenschappen van stoffen en materialen*, specifications 1–10 (the optical specifications 11–14 of E1 form folder [P09](../P09-optics/)). **Syllabus version used: 15 August 2025 (in force from 1 September 2025).**

## Learning outcomes (CCVX syllabus)

The candidate can:

1. Use the molecular model of matter (*moleculaire model van materie*) to explain phases and phase transitions: gas, liquid, solid (*gas, vloeistof, vaste stof*), melting, solidifying, evaporating, condensing, subliming (*smelten, stollen, verdampen, condenseren, sublimeren*).
2. Explain heat transport (*warmtetransport*) with matter models — thermal conduction (*geleiding*), convection (*stroming*), radiation (*straling*) — and make simple calculations on the heat current (*warmtestroom* = heat per unit time through a wall; air layers at the wall faces may be neglected): **P = λ·A·ΔT/d**, with the thermal conductivity (λ) of the material; at minimum in the context of energy saving by insulation (*energiebesparing door isolatie*).
3. Describe temperature changes of a substance as a consequence of adding or removing heat: temperature in terms of particle motion, the absolute zero point (*absoluut nulpunt*), **Q = m·c·ΔT** with the specific heat (*soortelijke warmte*) as a material property (BINAS); convert Celsius to kelvin and back: **T[K] = T[°C] + 273**.
4. Describe and explain the relation between density and specific heat in metals with the concept atomic mass (*atomaire massa*).
5. Describe and explain the relation between heat conduction and electrical conduction in metals with the concept conduction electron (*geleidingselektron*).
6. Interpret stress–strain diagrams (*spanning-rekdiagrammen*) in terms of elastic and plastic deformation and make calculations on elastic deformation: tensile strength (*treksterkte*), stress **σ = F/A**, strain **ε = Δℓ/ℓ₀**, elastic modulus (Young's modulus) **E = σ/ε**.
7. Explain macroscopic phenomena in substances from the properties of molecules and their interaction: ideal and real gases (*ideale en reële gassen*), Van der Waals force; liquids and solids; phases and phase transitions (evaporation, vapour pressure — *dampdruk*); the kinetic view of pressure, internal energy and temperature.
8. Describe how pressure is measured and how pressure differences can cause flow (*stroming*): manometer, barometer, blood-pressure meter (*bloeddrukmeter*); overpressure (*overdruk*), underpressure (*onderdruk*); pressure **p = F/A**.
9. Apply the general gas law (*algemene gaswet*) for an ideal gas: **pV/T = n·R** (with n in mol, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), the absolute temperature scale); compute pressure, volume or temperature; read and use (p,V) and (p,T) diagrams.
10. Describe volume changes of a substance as a consequence of adding or removing heat with the linear and cubic expansion coefficients: **Δℓ/ℓ₀ = α·ΔT** and **ΔV/V₀ = γ·ΔT** (BINAS tables; for solids γ ≈ 3α).
