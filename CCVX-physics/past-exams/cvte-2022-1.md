# Walkthrough — CvTE vwo natuurkunde 2022, tijdvak 1

25 questions, 78 points, in five opgaven: a basketball free throw (with a numerical model), QLED television quantum dots, a heat-radiation practicum (measuring σ!), beta particles around the corner, and a special flute. Free official sources: [paper](https://www.examenblad.nl/2022/vwo/documenten/cse-1/vw-1023-a-22-1-o), [answer booklet](https://www.examenblad.nl/2022/vwo/documenten/cse-1/vw-1023-a-22-1-u), [correctievoorschrift](https://www.examenblad.nl/2022/vwo/documenten/cse-1/vw-1023-a-22-1-c) and the [supplement](https://www.examenblad.nl/2022/vwo/documenten/cse-1/vw-1023-a-22-1-c-A) on examenblad.nl. Nothing of the official text, figures, tables or answer-key wording is copied here: every line is our own phrasing and every number is derived by us. The quantum-dot opgave is the closest 2022 cousin of the revised 2025 quantum subdomain [P11](../P11-quantum-world/).

## Opgave A — Vrije worp bij basketbal (Q1–Q5)

**Subdomains:** [P03](../P03-force-and-motion/) (vectors, components), [P04](../P04-energy-and-interaction/) (work–kinetic), syllabus-A skills (numerical models). **BINAS:** g only.

### Q1 (4p) — launch velocity from two component graphs
**Solution:** read the two components from the (x,t) and (y,t) graphs at t = 0:
- x(t) is straight: v_x = (4.55 − 0.35)/1.2 = 3.5 m/s;
- y(t) curves: tangent at t = 0, e.g. (5.0 − 2.3)/0.41 → v_y ≈ 6.6 m/s (accepted band 6.2–7.7).
Combine like force vectors: v = √(v_x² + v_y²) = √(3.5² + 6.6²) = **7.5 m/s**. (Glasses route — also officially fine: from the time to the top, v_y = ½g·Δt_up × 2… e.g. v_y = 9.81·0.68 = 6.7 m/s.)
**Common mistakes:** using the table's 7.1 m/s (that is the *other*, photographed throw!); summing components directly instead of Pythagoras; reading slopes from the printed coarse grid without the magnified booklet figure.
**Points:** slope insight (1), v_x and v_y within margins (1), Pythagoras (1), completion + significance (1).

### Q2 (4p) — muscle work measured through the photo scale
**Solution:** the ball's real 24 cm diameter calibrates the photos: the hand-to-release displacement measures 0.277 ≈ 0.28 m (±0.05 with the supplement). Then the throwing muscular work goes to kinetic energy: F_res·s = ½m·v² → F_res = 0.600·7.1²/(2·0.277) = 55 N. (Kinematics alternative: Δt = s/v_gem = 0.277/(7.1/2) = 78 ms → F = m·Δv/Δt = 0.600·7.1/0.078 = 55 N ✓.)
**Common mistakes:** lifting the 4.6 m or 3.05 m of the basket data into this throw phase; W = Fs with v instead of v² inside E_k.
**Points:** scale from the ball (1), W = F·s and E_k = ½mv² (1+1), completion + significance (1).

### Q3 (2p) — the model's two stop conditions
**Solution:** the throw ends when the ball *arrives at the ring from above*: stop as soon as **y ≤ 3.05 m** (ring height) **and v_y < 0** (falling, not still climbing).
**Common mistakes:** omitting v_y < 0 (the model would stop when the ball passes 3.05 m coming *up*); stopping at x = 4.6 (height check comes first).
**Points:** y condition (1), v_y < 0 (1).

### Q4 (2p) — why C scores
**Solution:** curve C ends its run at height ≈ 3.05 m and horizontal position ≈ 4.6 m — comparing with the table in the paper (ring at 3.05 m, free-throw line at 4.6 m): that is the ring. Heights match **and** distances match → a point.
**Common mistakes:** checking only the height or only the x; assuming any curve that passes near scores.
**Points:** height match (1), distance match (1). **Per the supplement:** a candidate who, in Q3, named the stop condition of arriving at the ring and then shows the horizontal-distance insight in Q4 also earns 2 points.

### Q5 (3p) — which knob did each curve turn?
**Solution:** the maximum height of the trajectory is set by v_y only; the range is set by v_x (given time). Curves A and E reach a different maximum height than C → varied v_y. Curves B and D keep C's summit but land differently → varied v_x. A: v_y; B: v_x; D: v_x; E: v_y.
**Common mistakes:** attributing a *range* change to v_y (with fixed v_x, only gravity pulls down: the landing also slides, but the summit is the fingerprint marker).
**Points:** v_y ↔ summit (and v_x ↔ range) insight (1), A/E correct (1), B/D correct (1).

## Opgave B — Qled-tv (Q6–Q10)

**Subdomains:** [P11](../P11-quantum-world/) (band gaps, de Broglie, box), [P10](../P10-electromagnetic-radiation-and-stars/) (photon energy). **BINAS:** h, c, e, m_e.

### Q6 (2p) — why pump with violet
**Solution:** the dots re-emit at most the energy they received (the rest leaves as lattice heat). Blue, green and red all sit *below* violet in photon energy — so violet light can excite dots aimed at any of the TV's colours; redder pump light could not reach blue or green emission at all.
**Common mistakes:** reasoning in wavelengths ("violet is shortest" without the E = hc/λ explanation); claiming violet is chosen for efficiency (it isn't — it is chosen for reach).
**Points:** E_out ≤ E_in (1), violet tops the named colours (1).

### Q7 (2p) — which semiconductors pass the foton test
**Solution:** absorption needs E_photon ≥ E_gap. With violet at 2.75–3.10 eV: Si (1.12), CdSe (1.74), GaAs (1.43), GaP (2.26) all pass; only Si₃N₄ (5.00 eV) fails. (The supplement adds: also judging Si and GaAs unfit because of *visibility* of the emission is not penalised.)
**Common mistakes:** reading the table rows shifted (a table slip at 1p per row); comparing with the mean 2.92 eV instead of the given band (use the lower edge 2.75 for "can this gap be met at all").
**Points:** E_photon ≥ E_gap (1), table (1).

### Q8 (5p) — derive the effective-mass de Broglie formula, then compute
**Solution:** derivation: E_k = ½mv² = p²/2m (with p = m·v), so p = √(2mE_k); together with λ_B = h/p and m → m_eff: λ_B = h/√(2m_eff·E_k). ∎ Numbers: m_eff,e = 0.13·m_e = 1.18·10⁻³¹ kg, E_k = 1 eV = 1.60·10⁻¹⁹ J → λ_B = 6.63·10⁻³⁴/√(2·1.18·10⁻³¹·1.60·10⁻¹⁹) = 6.63·10⁻³⁴/1.95·10⁻²⁵ = **3·10⁻⁹ m** (about the dot's own diameter — confinement fits).
**Common mistakes:** carrying m_e into the square root instead of m_eff; J ↔ eV slip; writing λ = h/(mv) with v estimated (don't — the formula is the goal).
**Points:** λ = h/p + p = mv (1), E_k = ½mv² (1), completion of derivation (1), m_eff used (1), final computation (1).

### Q9 (4p) — derive E_f = E_gap + C/R² and compute C
**Solution:** the photon takes the band gap plus the *confined* zero-point energies of both partners: E_f = E_gap + E_nul,elektron + E_nul,gat. Box model (n = 1, L = R): E_nul = h²/(8·m_eff·L²) each, so E_f = E_gap + [h²/(8m_eff,e) + h²/(8m_eff,g)]·1/R². Hence C = h²/(8m_eff,e) + h²/(8m_eff,g) = h²/(8m_e)·(1/0.13 + 1/0.45) = h²/(8·9.11·10⁻³¹)·9.91 = **6.0·10⁻³⁷ J·m²**.
**Common mistakes:** one particle only (the pair shares the box!); losing L = R in the substitution; slipping the factor n = 1.
**Points:** E_f = E_gap + E_nul (1), box formula with n = 1, L = R (1), C-extraction (1), computation (1).

### Q10 (4p) — dot radius for a 534 nm green
**Solution:** the green photon: E_f = hc/λ = 1240/534 = 2.32 eV = 3.72·10⁻¹⁹ J. Drop that onto the linearised graph (E_f against R⁻²): E_gap is its intercept and per the figure R⁻² ≈ 0.17 nm⁻² → **R = 2.4 nm** (±0.02 nm⁻² margin). Match: E_gap + C/R² with C from Q9 and the figure's E_gap reproduces 2.32 eV ✓.
**Common mistakes:** reading the axes un-transformed; nm⁻² slipped into nm²; forgetting the square root at the end.
**Points:** hc/λ (1), J → eV (1), R⁻² read (1), completion + significance (1).

## Opgave C — Practicum warmtestraling (Q11–Q15)

**Subdomains:** [P06](../P06-electric-circuits/) (resistivity, power), [P08](../P08-matter-heat-and-gases/) (radiation), [P10](../P10-electromagnetic-radiation-and-stars/) (σ, Wien, inverse square). **BINAS:** ρ constantan, α constantan, k_W.

### Q11 (4p) — element power right after switching on
**Solution:** cross-section: A = ¼πd² = ¼π·(4.0·10⁻⁵)² = 1.26·10⁻⁹ m². Table value ρ = 0.45·10⁻⁶ Ωm (as in the exam's own information booklet): R = ρℓ/A = 0.45·10⁻⁶·0.35/1.26·10⁻⁹ = 1.25·10² Ω. P = U²/R = 120²/1.25·10² = **1.1·10² W**.
**Common mistakes:** A = πd² or A = πr² with d (both yield 4× off); the constantan ρ row mistaken for nichrome's.
**Points:** A with ¼πd² (1), ρ from the booklet (1), P = U²/R (1), completion (1).

### Q12 (3p) — settles the 5% argument
**Solution:** temperature coefficient of constantan: α = 0.05·10⁻³ K⁻¹ (per the booklet). The ρ-factor over 20 → 300 °C: 1 + α·ΔT = 1 + 0.05·10⁻³·280 = 1.014: the resistivity grows barely **1.4%** — less than 5%: **Tess is right**.
**Common mistakes:** ΔT = 300 instead of 280 °C (T₀ = 293 K = 20 °C!); reading α of a wrong alloy.
**Points:** α value (1), factor or Δρ computation (1), conclusion (1).

### Q13 (5p) — σ from a P/A versus T⁴ line
**Solution:** theory: P = σ·A·T⁴ → P/A = σ·T⁴: a proportional relation y = C·x — the plot is therefore a straight line through the origin, with **σ = slope**. Line fitted through the origin and the measured points: σ = 11.6·10³/20.0·10¹⁰ = **5.8·10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴)** (margin 0.2·10⁻⁸) — within the measurement scatter of BINAS's σ = 5.67·10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴), earned by an oven and gold-plated patience.
**Common mistakes:** a best-fit that misses the origin (P/A vs T⁴ has no intercept!); reading T in °C (kelvin!); slope read from one raw point at the noisy end.
**Points:** line through origin ⇔ proportionality (1), Stefan-Boltzmann link (1), line drawn (1), slope = σ (1), completion + significance (1).

### Q14 (3p) — Wien at the oven's hottest
**Solution:** T = 383 °C = 656 K (conversion first!). Wien: λ_max = k_W/T = 2.898·10⁻³/656 = **4.42·10⁻⁶ m** (3 significant figures, as the data allow) — mid-infrared; no visible glow snapshot at this temperature class.
**Common mistakes:** °C→K skipped (yields 7.6 μm, wrong).
**Points:** Wien (1), kelvin (1), completion + significance (1).

### Q15 (4p) — testing the inverse-square law on the detector
**Solution:** if I ∝ 1/x², then I₁/I₂ must equal (x₂/x₁)². Take the two edge rows: I(40)/I(70) = 4810/620 = 7.76, while (70/40)² = 3.06. Not even close → **the inverse-square law fails here** (at 40–70 cm the oven window is no point source; a point source would fit only at distances ≫ the window's own size).
**Common mistakes:** checking a single pair (a relation must hold for *all* pairs, so one mismatch kills it); squaring the intensity instead of the distance.
**Points:** inverse-square statement as I ∝ x⁻² (1), intensity ratio (1), distance ratio (1), conclusion (1).

## Opgave D — Om het hoekje (Q16–Q22)

**Subdomains:** [P02](../P02-medical-imaging-and-radiation/) (decay, background), [P07](../P07-electric-and-magnetic-fields/) (Lorentz bending). **BINAS:** nuclear table (Sr-90), c, e, m_e.

### Q16 (3p) — the beta channel producing Y-90
**Solution:** mass stays 90, charge steps up one: **⁹⁰₃₈Sr → ⁹⁰₃₉Y + ⁰₋₁β**. Strontium-90 is the classic "calcium-seeking" bone isotope.
**Common mistakes:** a helium nucleus out (that is the α channel never); writing ⁹⁰Y with Z unchanged.
**Points:** Y-90 + β⁻ right side (1), Sr identified via the periodic table (1), nucleon balance (1).

### Q17 (1p) — then why gamma after all?
**Solution:** the daughters keep the saga running: the Y-90 that forms is itself unstable and decays further (with its own gamma-emitting channels), so the source's output carries gamma not from the printed reaction but from the decay chain that follows.
**Common mistakes:** "the equation hides a γ" — it doesn't; chains do.
**Points:** daughter decays further (1).

### Q18 (3p) — which end of the horseshoe is north?
**Solution:** the betas need steering around the lead block. The particles are negative, so their conventional current runs *against* their flight. Applying a hand rule across the needed Lorentz deflection shows the field through the gap must run from R to L — field lines leave at north: **the north pole is at R**.
**Common mistakes:** directing the current *with* the electrons (they are negative!); using the right-hand grip rule where the left-hand flat rule belongs.
**Points:** negative-carrier current inversion (1), F_L direction (1), field-line direction + conclusion (1).

### Q19 (3p) — derive v = Bqr/m
**Solution:** the Lorentz force supplies the centripetal force (gravity neglected): F_mpz = F_L → m·v²/r = B·q·v → cancel one v and sort: **v = B·q·r/m**. ∎
**Common mistakes:** adding instead of *equating* the two forces; cancelling v from only one side conceptually (write F_mpz = F_L first!).
**Points:** F_mpz = F_L (1), both formulas (1), completion (1).

### Q20 (3p) — does the naive v exceed relativity's worry line?
**Solution:** v = B·q·r/m = 0.1·1.602·10⁻¹⁹·0.20/9.109·10⁻³¹ = 3.5·10⁹ m/s (the printed-data arithmetic even gives ≈ 4·10⁹) — that is more than ten times the speed of light! A formula outcome above c is physics' red flag: **the relativistic correction is mandatory here** (real beta speeds from Sr-90 sit close under c; the naive Newtonian v = Bqr/m simply breaks).
**Common mistakes:** comparing with escape velocity instead of c; concluding "fine, below 0.1c" without computing orders.
**Points:** v computed (1+1), compared with c and conclusion (1).

### Q21 (1p) — counts with no source at all
**Solution:** natural background radiation is always there: cosmic showers plus traces of radioactivity in ground and air strike the GM tube constantly — that is the ≈ 30 counts/min floor before any source arrives.
**Common mistakes:** blaming the device's own electronics (the GM tube is honest: the counts are real radiation from around us).
**Points:** background (1).

### Q22 (1p) — more counts already without the magnet
**Solution:** air is a pinball machine: betas scatter ("ricochet") off air molecules and a share of the cloud reaches the sensor via curved detours even without magnetic steering. (Mere gamma is *not* counted as the cause here — the experiment was designed to listen for the beta path.)
**Common mistakes:** gamma hand-waving (the paper excludes it); saying the lead leaks (it doesn't).
**Points:** scattering detour (1).

## Opgave E — Speciale fluit (Q23–Q25)

**Subdomains:** [P01](../P01-oscillations-and-waves/) (standing waves, end effects, temperature-sensitive sound speed). **BINAS:** v_sound = 343 m/s at 20 °C.

### Q23 (6p) — place the antinode B, then compare the tones
**Solution:**
- Tone I hums at 6.0·10² Hz: λ = v/f = 343/600 = 0.572 m. In a closed-open resonator the first antinode sits one quarter wave from the node: K–B = ¼λ = 0.143 m in reality, so at the drawing's 1:2 scale B lies **7.1 cm** from the closed end carrying K. (The drawing's own B sits at the open hole + a small outside-end correction — the marked distance anchors the point on paper.)
- Tone II closes the nearer hole: the K–B distance shrinks, so λ shrinks, so f = v/λ rises: **tone II is higher than tone I**.
**Common mistakes:** the antinode placed exactly at the open hole (it lives a touch beyond it, the end correction — but the computed ¼λ arm length is what the drawing demands); reading the printed scale as 1:1; concluding lower pitch from "shorter pipe" backwards.
**Points:** v = fλ with 343 (1), K–B = ¼λ insight (1), scale factor (1), completion + B placed (1), shorter distance (1), conclusion (1).

### Q24 (3p) — the pattern with both finger holes open
**Solution:** near **each open hole a belly (B)**; alternating K–B–K–B across the dotted region, all successive K–B spacings equal (the standing-wave toothcomb): two B's minimum with a K between them in the span shown. Either high-order drawing passes provided these three hold.
**Common mistakes:** a belly with an uneven rhythm (equidistant!); node at an open hole (holes vent pressure: bellies belong there).
**Points:** B near both holes (1), alternation (1), equal spacings (1).

### Q25 (3p) — hot flutes sing sharp
**Solution:** the speed of sound rises with air temperature (more beat per molecule). The length (and thereby λ) of the flute's standing wave is frozen by geometry, so f = v/λ rises with v: **the pitch climbs** as the instrument warms — orchestras retune for a reason.
**Common mistakes:** connecting "warm air expands → pipe longer" (tiny against the speed effect, and excluded by the question's premise anyway); reasoning via density instead of the speed of sound.
**Points:** v_sound rises with T (1), v = λf insight (1), conclusion (1).
