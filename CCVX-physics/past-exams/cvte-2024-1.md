# Walkthrough — CvTE vwo natuurkunde 2024, tijdvak 1

24 questions, 77 points, in five opgaven: the bicycle speed record, a TESS exoplanet in (or not in) the habitable zone, a battery train through a copper coil, temperature diagnostics of a fusion reactor, and ultrasound imaging. Free official sources: [paper](https://www.examenblad.nl/2024/vwo/documenten/cse-1/vw-1023-a-24-1-o), [answer booklet](https://www.examenblad.nl/2024/vwo/documenten/cse-1/vw-1023-a-24-1-u) and [correctievoorschrift](https://www.examenblad.nl/2024/vwo/documenten/cse-1/vw-1023-a-24-1-c) on examenblad.nl. Nothing of the official text, figures, tables or answer-key wording is copied here: every line is our own phrasing and every number is derived by us. Lovely for the new CCVX syllabus: the tunneling barrier of Q16 is pure [P11](../P11-quantum-world/) content (Q15 before it is the D–T nuclear channel of [P02](../P02-medical-imaging-and-radiation/)), and Q18–19 are [P10](../P10-electromagnetic-radiation-and-stars/) hydrogen spectroscopy.

## Opgave A — Snelheidsrecord op de fiets (Q1–Q5)

**Subdomains:** [P03](../P03-force-and-motion/) (diagrams, air resistance), [P04](../P04-energy-and-interaction/) (work–kinetic, P = Fv). **BINAS:** none beyond reading its drag formula.

### Q1 (3p) — timing the last mile
**Asked:** the last, exactly 1.000000-mile-long stretch was ridden at 183.932 miles per hour average: the time in seconds, with correct significance.
**Solution:** t = s/v = 1.000000/183.932 = 5.4368·10⁻³ h = ·3600 = 19.5725 s = **19.5725 s** (six significant figures, as the 6-figure speed and the 7-figure mile demand — a number that tells the whole sport it was worth the salt flats).
**Common mistakes:** converting by 3600 wrongly (×3600 the hours!); rounding to 20 s (the point of the measurement was precision).
**Points:** v = s/t (1), completion (1), significance (1).

### Q2 (4p) — frontal area from the drawing, then the drag force
**Solution:** the wheel's 46 cm sets the scale: one drawing box is 7.7 cm, box area 5.88·10⁻³ m²; counting the grid over the silhouette gives ≈ 54 boxes → A ≈ 0.32 m² (margin 0.03). F_w,l = ½ρc_wAv² = ½·1.1·0.70·0.32·82.2² = 0.83 kN. Behind a race car she dodged most of that wall.
**Common mistakes:** estimating the area from her height instead of the wheel's grid; forgetting ½; squaring v wrongly.
**Points:** scale from the wheel (1), area determination + significance (1), drag formula (1), completion (1).

### Q3 (2p) — the real resistance behind the slipstream
**Solution:** at constant speed her legs match the total resistance: P = F_w·v → F_w = 700/82.2 = **8.52 N**. Two orders less than the undrafted 0.83 kN — that is the entire reason for the car.
**Common mistakes:** computing with the Q2 drag instead of the stated 700 W; writing P = F/v.
**Points:** P = Fv (1), completion (1).

### Q4 (3p) — speed at decoupling from the (s,t) graph
**Solution:** draw a tangent to the (s,t) curve at s = 1609 m (one mile) on the uitwerkbijlage: its slope, e.g. Δs/Δt ≈ 8.2 km/2.43 min → v ≈ 2.0·10² km/h (margin 0.2·10²). She released the tow cable already at world-record pace.
**Common mistakes:** dividing the total distance by the total time (average, not the instantaneous speed); sloppy tangent construction (small window: use the whole line for slope counting).
**Points:** tangent (1), v = (Δs/Δt)_tangent (1), completion + significance (1).

### Q5 (3p) — average braking force
**Solution:** ΣW = ΔE_k: F·s = ½m(v_e² − v_b²) → F = ½·71·(50² − 82.2²)/1.5·10³ = 35.5·(−4257)/1.5·10³ = −1.0·10² N (the minus sign says braking). Kinematics check: t = s/v_gem = 1500/66.1 = 22.7 s; a = −32.2/22.7 = −1.42 m/s² → F = 71·(−1.42) = −1.0·10² N ✓.
**Common mistakes:** forgetting the minus (asked direction!); using Δv/v gem wrong; mixing v² and (Δv)².
**Points:** ΣW = ΔE_k or kinematics equivalent (1+1), completion (1).

## Opgave B — Goudlokje (Q6–Q9)

**Subdomains:** [P05](../P05-gravitation/) (Kepler 3), [P10](../P10-electromagnetic-radiation-and-stars/) (intensity, Stefan-Boltzmann). **BINAS:** G, M_sun.

### Q6 (3p) — orbital radius from Kepler's third law
**Solution:** star mass: 0.67·M_sun = 0.67·1.99·10³⁰ = 1.33·10³⁰ kg; period T = 36·86400 = 3.11·10⁶ s. Kepler 3 as the paper states it: r = (G·M·T²/4π²)⅓ = (6.67·10⁻¹¹·1.33·10³⁰·(3.11·10⁶)²/39.478)⅓ = (2.18·10³¹)⅓ = **2.8·10¹⁰ m**. ∎
**Common mistakes:** forgetting to cube-root (r³ = … is not r); T left in days; M = 0.67 not converted by M_sun from BINAS.
**Points:** Kepler 3 with G and M_sun looked up (1), T → s (1), completion (1).

### Q7 (4p) — the planet-to-star radius ratio from the transit dip
**Solution:** the relative intensity dips from 1.0000 to 0.99865 → the planet hides a fraction ΔI = 1 − 0.99865 = 0.00135 of the star's disc. Both circle: area ratio = (R_p/R_s)² = 0.00135 → **R_p/R_s = 0.0367** (±0.0007). A Neptune-plus world against a smallish star.
**Common mistakes:** reading the dip depth from the axis rather than the graph's bottom; taking √ of the *intensity* instead of the *dip fraction*; forgetting three significant figures.
**Points:** lowest point read (1), ΔI = area ratio (1), A ∝ R² (1), completion + significance (1).

### Q8 (4p) — derive the given absorption formula
**Solution:** the star's output spreads over a sphere: the intensity at the planet is I = P_bron/(4πr²). The planet's shadow catching that light is its circular silhouette A = πR²: P_in = I·A = P_bron·πR²/(4πr²) = P_bron·R²/(4r²). Of this, a fraction α reflects away, a fraction (1 − α) is absorbed: **P_abs = P_bron·(R²/4r²)(1 − α)**. ∎
**Common mistakes:** using the planet's full 4πR² for reception (only the silhouette receives!); α and (1 − α) swapped.
**Points:** I = P/4πr² (1), P = I·A with A = πR² (1), the (1 − α) share (1), completion (1).

### Q9 (5p) — show β = −2, and is it habitable?
**Solution:** equilibrium: P_abs = P_uit. With P_uit = σ·A·T⁴ = σ·4πR²·T⁴:
P_ster·(R²/4r²)(1 − α) = σ·4πR²·T⁴ → r² = [P_ster·R²(1 − α)/(16πσR²)]·T⁻⁴ → **r = C·T⁻², so β = −2**. ∎
Then the boundaries at 273 K and 373 K: r ∝ T⁻² gives r_binnen/r_buiten = (T_binnen/T_buiten)⁻² → r_binnen = 5.8·10¹⁰·(273/373)² = 3.1·10¹⁰ m. The planet orbits at 2.8·10¹⁰ m < 3.1·10¹⁰ m: inside the inner edge — **too hot for liquid water: not in the Goldilocks zone** (its own balance temperature from the same math comes out at 273·√(5.8·10¹⁰/2.8·10¹⁰) ≈ 3.9·10² K, confirming). ∎
**Common mistakes:** solving T⁴ ∝ 1/r² and then mis-assigning the exponent sign in r = C·T^β; comparing 2.8·10¹⁰ against r_buiten only (a zone has two edges!).
**Points:** P_uit = σAT⁴ (1), combine (2) + (3) (1), β determination (1), boundary-ratio or C route (1), conclusion (1).

## Opgave C — Batterijtrein (Q10–Q14)

**Subdomains:** [P06](../P06-electric-circuits/) (resistivity, series), [P07](../P07-electric-and-magnetic-fields/) (coil field, left-hand rule). **BINAS:** ρ of copper.

### Q10 (4p) — resistance of the shorted coil stretch
**Solution:** count the turns between the battery poles in the magnified photo: 15 (±2). Wire length: ℓ = 15·π·D = 15·π·0.019 = 0.90 m. Cross-section of the wire: A = π(d/2)² = π(5.5·10⁻⁴)² = 9.50·10⁻⁷ m². R = ρℓ/A = 17·10⁻⁹·0.895/9.50·10⁻⁷ = **1.6·10⁻² Ω**.
**Common mistakes:** swapping the 1.1 mm wire diameter with the 1.9 cm coil diameter (the key caps that at 2p!); using diameter instead of radius in A = πr².
**Points:** ℓ = N·πD with N measured (1), ρ-Cu from BINAS (1), A = πr² (1), completion (1).

### Q11 (1p) — how the field map shows strength
**Solution:** the field-line density is the field-strength meter: lines pack tighter at P (close to the coil) than at Q (farther out) → B(P) > B(Q).
**Common mistakes:** calling the field stronger because "the lines are longer" or by the pole direction alone.
**Points:** density insight (1).

### Q12 (1p) — current direction in the winding
**Solution:** figure 4 fixes the coil's outer north end; with the right-hand coil rule (curled fingers in the winding current's direction → thumb to the north pole) mark the current direction in at least one drawn winding consistently (up through the figure's visible front side, or down, opposite to the wrong choice).
**Common mistakes:** reversing *all* windings consistently in the wrong direction (thumb to south); using the left hand for a field rule.
**Points:** correct direction shown (1).

### Q13 (4p) — forces on the four magnet poles and their identities
**Solution:** the train moves right, so the top picture's resultant forces on L and R both point right. Poles 1 and 2 (ends of magnet L) experience oppositely directed forces that sum to L's rightward resultant: the coil-facing pole 1 is drawn already, so pole 2 gets its matching mirror. Poles 3 and 4 (of magnet R) play the same game against each other, again summing rightwards. Consistency check by attraction/repulsion to the coil poles → **1 and 4 are north, 2 and 3 are south** (the only assignment producing both rightward sums with equal-size arrows).
**Common mistakes:** drawing all four forces in the resultant's direction (pole pairs always push each other in opposite directions); mismatching magnitudes between mirrored poles.
**Points:** force pair of L consistent with L's resultant (1), poles 1+2 (1), same exercise for R (1), poles 3+4 (1).

### Q14 (3p) — which resistance is bigger, Ri or R_spoel?
**Solution:** same current through both (series). P = I²·R: heating power is proportional to resistance at equal current. Given P_i ≫ P_spoel, so R_i ≫ R_spoel: the **internal resistance of the battery is the larger one** — the battery cooks itself more readily than it cooks the coil. (Voltage-share route gives the same conclusion via U ∝ R in series.)
**Common mistakes:** concluding from P = U²/R with the same U (the two resistors do NOT share the same voltage!); mixing the spot order.
**Points:** equal current (1), P = I²R or voltage-share logic (1), conclusion (1).

## Opgave D — Kernfusiereactor (Q15–Q19)

**Subdomains:** [P02](../P02-medical-imaging-and-radiation/) (nuclear equations), [P11](../P11-quantum-world/) (tunneling), [P10](../P10-electromagnetic-radiation-and-stars/) (Balmer levels, spectra). **BINAS:** hydrogen levels, h, c, e.

### Q15 (3p) — the D–T fusion equation
**Solution:** balance both columns: mass 2 + A_in = 4 + 1 → A_in = 3; charge 1 + Z_in = 2 + 0 → Z_in = 1 → the partner is hydrogen-3 (tritium): **²₁H + ³₁H → ⁴₂He + ¹₀n**. This D+T reaction is the workhorse of every fusion experiment on earth (and of [P10](../P10-electromagnetic-radiation-and-stars/)'s mass-energy bookkeeping).
**Common mistakes:** writing ²H + ²H (= 3He + n, a real but different channel — the paper asks the route *to He-4 + neutron*); proton instead of neutron product.
**Points:** H-2 left, He-4 + n right (1), second H from balancing (1), nucleon balance (1).

### Q16 (3p) — classically only above the barrier; quantum through it
**Solution:**
- Classically a deuterium nucleus can only fuse if its energy exceeds the barrier's peak (the maximum of E_pot(x)): among the dotted options that holds only for **E1 and E2**. At E3 and E4 a classical particle simply bounces back before touching the other nucleus.
- Quantum mechanics gives the wave function an exponentially decaying *tail inside the barrier*: with a small but nonzero probability the nucleus appears on the far side without ever owning the barrier energy — **tunneling** (see [P11](../P11-quantum-world/)). That is why fusion picks up at temperatures far below the naive Coulomb estimate.
**Common mistakes:** including E3 classically "because it is close to the top"; saying tunneling "lowers the barrier" (it lets the particle *through*, the height stays).
**Points:** classical criterion (1), correct set (1), tunneling insight (1).

### Q17 (3p) — order of magnitude of wall hits
**Solution:** atomic cross-section ≈ (10² pm)² = (10⁻¹⁰ m)² = 10⁻²⁰ m² → ≈ 10²⁰ wall atoms per m² of surface. The wall counts ≈ 10³ m² → ≈ 10²³ surface atoms, and each takes one neutron per 10² s on average: 10²³/10² = **10²¹ neutrons per second**. (A rate telling you why first-wall material science decides reactor design.)
**Common mistakes:** using the diameter instead of its square for area; forgetting the 10² s per share; off-by-two orders somewhere.
**Points:** area ∝ d² (1), atoms per m² (1), completion (1).

### Q18 (4p) — the shortest wavelength watched
**Asked:** the Balmer lines from Hγ up to Hζ are monitored (transitions to n = 2): the *smallest* wavelength among them.
**Solution:** shortest λ means largest energy: the highest member of the monitored set, Hζ from n = 8 down to n = 2: ΔE = 13.6·(1/2² − 1/8²) = 13.6·(0.25 − 0.015625) = 3.19 eV. λ = hc/ΔE = 1240/3.188 = 3.89·10⁻⁷ m = **389 nm** (violet edge of the visible).
**Common mistakes:** stopping at n = 4 as if the series ended at Hβ — the monitored set runs to Hζ (n = 8); using the magnitude 13.6 eV unsigned but forgetting it is n = 8 in the denominator as 8² = 64.
**Points:** E_8 − E_2 insight (1), E_n = −13.6/n² (1), hc/λ link (1), completion (1).

### Q19 (3p) — reading a temperature out of Balmer ratios
**Solution:** read the spectrum's line intensities (fig 5), form a ratio, e.g. Hδ/Hγ ≈ 4.5/13.8 = 0.33, and on the calibration graph (fig 4) that ratio sits at **T = 3.3·10³ K** (±0.5·10³ K; two more line pairs land the same place).
**Common mistakes:** reading one line's absolute intensity instead of a ratio (the calibration curves are *ratio* curves); swapping numerator and denominator in the ratio.
**Points:** reading two intensities (1), ratio insight (1), reading T + significance (1).

## Opgave E — Echografie (Q20–Q24)

**Subdomains:** [P01](../P01-oscillations-and-waves/) (ultrasound, waves), [P02](../P02-medical-imaging-and-radiation/) (imaging), syllabus-A skills (units). **BINAS:** speed of sound in air (qualitative), nothing numeric.

### Q20 (3p) — N·s/m³ as unit of Z
**Solution:** with N = kg·m/s²: N·s·m⁻³ = kg·m·s⁻²·s·m⁻³ = kg·m⁻²·s⁻¹. And from Z = ρ·v: [Z] = kg·m⁻³·m·s⁻¹ = kg·m⁻²·s⁻¹. Equal → N·s/m³ is a unit of the acoustic impedance Z. ∎
**Common mistakes:** using N = kg·m/s (a newton is per second squared!); mixing the two units-m-paths and losing one m.
**Points:** N written in SI (1), ρ and v units (1), completion (1).

### Q21 (4p) — why the gel between skin and transducer matters
**Solution:** air's acoustic resistance Z = ρ·v is tiny against tissue's: both its density (~1.2 against ~10³ kg/m³) and the speed of sound (~343 vs ~1.5·10³ m/s) are far smaller. A huge Z-step at the transducer–skin transition would reflect nearly *all* of the sound at the surface, leaving nothing to echo from inside. The gel (water-based, Z close to tissue's) replaces the air layer with a tissue-like match → most of the energy enters the body instead of bouncing at the doorstep.
**Common mistakes:** saying gel "lubricates" without the impedance argument; forgetting *both* factors of Z (ρ and v of air are small).
**Points:** ρ-air ≪ ρ-tissue (1), v-air small (1), Z-step → reflection at the skin (1), gel removes the air layer (1).

### Q22 (5p) — maximum pulse repetition frequency
**Asked:** the layer of fat 1.5 cm thick lies right under the skin-and-gel path (transit 0.87 µs); pulses of 3 periods at 8.5 MHz: the maximal repetition rate.
**Solution:** one echo must be fully home before the next pulse leaves. Round path through the fat: 2·t_vet = 2·0.015/1.45·10³ = 2·1.034·10⁻⁵ = 2.069·10⁻⁵ s; skin+gel out and back: 2·0.87 µs = 1.7·10⁻⁶ s; the pulse's own length: 3/8.5·10⁶ = 3.5·10⁻⁷ s. Total: t_tot = 2.07·10⁻⁵ + 0.17·10⁻⁵ + 0.035·10⁻⁵ = 2.28·10⁻⁵ s. So f_max = 1/t_tot = **44 kHz**.
**Common mistakes:** forgetting the factor 2 for the out-and-back path; using 3 periods as the full time instead of adding the transit; inverting into MHz slips.
**Points:** t_tot composition (1), s = vt (1), t_puls = 3T (1), f = 1/t (1), completion (1).

### Q23 (3p) — smallest resolvable detail in fat
**Solution:** λ in fat = v/f = 1.45·10³/8.5·10⁶ = 1.71·10⁻⁴ m. Half a pulse = 1.5 λ = 2.56·10⁻⁴ m: details smaller than **0.26 mm** wash out under the pulse's smear.
**Common mistakes:** taking λ in air or water; using the full pulse (3λ) instead of the half; mixing the resolution unit into mm wrongly.
**Points:** λ = v/f (1), 1.5λ insight (1), completion (1).

### Q24 (2p) — what shadows behind gallstones say
**Solution:** the shadow below the stones means the sound did not pass through them. Two offered reasons work physically: the stones *absorb* the sound (it is gone) and/or *reflect* it (deflected, with the echo letting the scanner even see the stones brightly). Bending *around* the stones is wrong (no diffraction at that size ratio), and "penetration limited to 5 cm" is wrong too — the image clearly shows tissue beyond the shadow.
**Common mistakes:** accepting the diffraction statement because "waves bend" in general — scale matters.
**Points:** 2p for all four lines correct (1p for three).
