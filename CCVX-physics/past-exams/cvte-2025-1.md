This exam anticipated parts of the CCVX syllabus that became explicit in September 2025: the energy-well of lycopene (our [P11](../P11-quantum-world/)) and the Doppler-flavoured interference reasoning ([P01](../P01-oscillations-and-waves/)).

## Opgave A — Shorttrack (Q1–Q5)

**Subdomains:** [P03](../P03-force-and-motion/), [P04](../P04-energy-and-interaction/), [P05](../P05-gravitation/). **BINAS:** air density, g.

### Q1 (3p) — average speed over the kilometre
**Asked:** nine laps of a 111.11 m track in 1 min 29.80 s: the average speed, in correct significant figures.
**Solution:** distance = 9·111.11 = 1.000·10³ m (999.99 m); time = 89.80 s. v_gem = 999.99/89.80 = 11.14 m/s. Significance: the time is given to 4 figures, the lap length to 5 → answer with 4: **11.14 m/s**.
**Common mistakes:** forgetting nine laps; 89.80 read as 89.8 (throwing away the last significant figure); more than 4 significant figures in the final answer.
**Points:** formula with the correct distance (1), completing (1), significance (1).

### Q2 (2p) — time won by a constant early pace
**Asked:** apart from the first three laps the lap times are nearly constant — the time she would have won riding the whole race at that pace, from the graph.
**Solution:** a straight line fits the long flat train of measured points (constant speed); to the "no slowdown" world, extend this line backwards to t = 0 — it cuts the t-axis at about 6 s (±1 s margin) earlier than the real finish: about 0.6 of a lap at her 11.14 m/s pace (laps take ≈ 10.0 s). So the win would be ≈ **6 s**.
**Common mistakes:** averaging all nine laps instead of the long constant section; not extrapolating the line to the t-axis.
**Points:** insight about the straight line through the last points (1), extrapolation and determination (1).

### Q3 (3p) — derive the given power formula
**Asked:** derive P = f_d·m·g·v + ½c_w·A·ρ·v³ from the friction formula and book formulas.
**Solution:** the useful power to ride at constant v is P = F·v, with F the forward force. Constant speed ⇒ forward force = total resistance = sliding friction + air resistance. Sliding: F_w,s = f_d·F_N, and on level ice F_N = F_z = m·g; air: F_w,l = ½c_w·A·ρ·v². So F = f_d·m·g + ½c_w·A·ρ·v², and P = F·v = f_d·m·g·v + ½c_w·A·ρ·v³. ∎
**Common mistakes:** forgetting that F_N = m·g (level ice!); mixing v² into the friction term (the sliding friction is speed-independent).
**Points:** F_w,s with F_N = m·g (1), P = Fv with both resistance forces (1), completion (1).

### Q4 (3p) — estimate the frontal area, then the useful power
**Solution:** from the figures: model the skater's frontal silhouette by a rectangle — shoulder width ≈ 0.5 m, crouched height ≈ 1.0 m (reading her 1.70 m against the drawing: body folds to ~60%) → A ≈ 0.5 m² (accepted 0.4–0.6 wide / 0.8–1.2 high). Then P = 0.015·64·9.8·11.9 + ½·0.80·1.3·0.5·11.9³ = 1.1·10² + 4.4·10² ≈ **5.5·10² W** — i.e. roughly 6·10² W with the estimation band. A solid 500 W-class athlete, as expected for Olympic level.
**Common mistakes:** forgetting ρ = 1.29 kg/m³ from BINAS for the air term; punching the speed squared instead of cubed; using her full 1.70 m as the frontal height.
**Points:** reasoned frame sizes (1), formula 2 with ρ looked up (1), completing estimate + computation (1).

### Q5 (5p) — lean angle, resultant force, curve speed
**Asked:** construct the resultant force at point P on the uitwerkbijlage figure (reaction force of the ice + gravity) and measure it; then compute the curve speed (r = 8.20 m).
**Solution:** gravity is 64·9.81 = 628 N straight down at P; the ice's reaction work-line is given in the figure, so the vector sum along that line and the horizontal line to the centre is constructed by parallelogram — the resultant points horizontally towards the circle's centre (only that direction can balance a circular motion without vertical acceleration). Length ratio in the drawing ≈ 4.4:4.0 against the 628 N weight: F_res ≈ 6.9·10² N (±0.3·10²). Then F_res = F_mpz = m·v²/r → v = √(691·8.20/64) = **9.4 m/s**.
**Common mistakes:** the resultant not horizontal (it must be — F_z is vertical and nothing accelerates vertically); measuring against a wrong reference length; using the track's inside radius instead of 8.20 m.
**Points:** direction insight (1), construction (1), F_z = mg + measurement + significance (1), F_res = F_mpz identification (1), completing (1).

## Opgave B — Geluidssnelheid (Q6–Q9)

**Subdomains:** [P01](../P01-oscillations-and-waves/). **BINAS:** none (target: determine the speeds themselves).

### Q6 (3p) — from two resonance levels to the speed of sound
**Asked:** derive the formula for v from f, h₁ and h₂ (the two water levels at resonance).
**Solution:** both resonances are standing-wave fits of the same column: successive resonances grow the fit by exactly ½λ, so h₂ − h₁ = ½λ → λ = 2(h₂ − h₁). With v = λ·f: **v = 2(h₂ − h₁)·f**. ∎
**Common mistakes:** thinking (h₂ − h₁) = λ or ¼λ — go back to node-to-node = half a wave; writing v = f·λ with the wrong substitution.
**Points:** the ½λ insight (1), v = fλ (1), completion (1).

### Q7 (3p) — how ΔL falls out of the same data
**Asked:** the antinode at resonance lives a bit outside the open top (ΔL): show how the experiment yields it.
**Solution:** first resonance: h₁ + ΔL = ¼λ; second: h₂ + ΔL = ¾λ. With the ½λ relation from Q6: ¼λ = ½(h₂ − h₁), so **ΔL = ½(h₂ − h₁) − h₁ = ½h₂ − 1.5h₁**. No extra measurement needed — elegant use of both levels.
**Common mistakes:** assigning ΔL only to the top of the *first* resonance (it applies to both); sign slip when subtracting the equations.
**Points:** the ¼λ link (1), ½(h₂ − h₁) = ¼λ (1), completion (1).

### Q8 (3p) — frequency from the scope trace
**Asked:** the ultrasound scope shows 100 µs/cm; determine the frequency as precisely as possible (3 figures).
**Solution:** for precision, use all three full periods shown: 3T spans 12.6 cm = 1.26·10⁻³ s → T = 4.20·10⁻⁴ s → f = 1/T = 2.38·10³ Hz = **2.38 kHz** (margin 0.02).
**Common mistakes:** measuring one period (3× the relative error!); reading cm as ms without the 100 µs conversion.
**Points:** the 3-period measurement (1), f = 1/T (1), completion + significance (1).

### Q9 (3p) — speed of sound in water
**Asked:** the microphone signal lags the speaker signal by a readable time at 24 cm: the speed in water (2 figures).
**Solution:** the lag between matching peaks reads 0.17 ms for a 24 cm path: v = s/t = 0.24/1.7·10⁻⁴ = 1.4·10³ m/s (margin 0.1·10³). And the brief note that the microphone never matched phase confirms you are not a whole period off.
**Common mistakes:** taking 0.24 m as the round trip (it is the one-way path); reading the lag off the wrong peak pair.
**Points:** the correct lag identification (1), s = vt (1), completion + significance (1).

## Opgave C — Opslag van energie (Q10–Q16)

**Subdomains:** [P04](../P04-energy-and-interaction/) (energy, efficiency), [P06](../P06-electric-circuits/) (charge, rectifier), [P07](../P07-electric-and-magnetic-fields/) (dynamo). **BINAS:** none beyond g.

### Q10 (2p) — mAh is charge
**Solution:** the coulomb is 1 A·s = 1 A·s, so current × time = charge: 1 mAh = 10⁻³ A × 3600 s = 3.6 C. An hour is just a large second: any current×time unit is a charge unit, mAh included. ∎
**Common mistakes:** calling mAh a unit of energy or of capacitance in the electrical sense.
**Points:** C = A·s (1), completion (1).

### Q11 (4p) — one AA battery versus the mountain
**Solution:** AA: E = U·I·t = 1.5 V × 2.200 Ah × 3600 s/h = 1.19·10⁴ J. The build: E_z = m·g·h = 1200·10³·9.81·65 = 7.7·10⁸ J. Ratio: 7.65·10⁸/1.19·10⁴ ≈ **6.4·10⁴ batteries** — saying "64 000 AAs = one small mountain of gravel" wins every discussion of scale.
**Common mistakes:** mAh taken as 2200 C directly (it is 7920 C); tonnes in tonnes (1200 t = 1.2·10⁶ kg!).
**Points:** E = U·I·t / U = ΔE/Q (1), AA energy (1), E_z = mgh (1), ratio (1).

### Q12 (4p) — to-trap efficiency
**Asked:** 46 buckets around a stair cableway (always 18 full descending), 140 g of sand per bucket, the setup feeds four 1.2 W lamps at v = 2.6 m/s; from figure 2 (height 3.96 m drop, 6.51 m path along the slope): the efficiency.
**Solution:** per bucket ΔE_z = 0.140·9.81·3.96 = 5.44 J; one trip down takes 6.51/2.6 = 2.5 s. Always 18 buckets are descending at once, each surrendering its 5.44 J over its 2.5 s transit: P_z = 18·5.44/2.5 = 39.1 W. The lamps take 4 × 1.2 = 4.8 W → η = 4.8/39.1 = 0.12 ≈ 12%. (Neat invariant check: P_z = 18·m·g·v·(h/s) = 18·0.140·9.81·2.6·(3.96/6.51) = 39.1 W ✓.)
**Common mistakes:** using 36 or 46 buckets instead of the 18 descending ones; computing the energy per bucket but forgetting to divide by the run time; mixing slope length and height (only h matters for energy, s only for time).
**Points:** E_z + P = E/t (1), s = vt with the slope path (1), η = P_el/P_z (1), completion (1).

### Q13 (3p) — from voltage peaks to cableway speed
**Asked:** six magnets on the dynamo disc (diameter 0.32 m per figure 3); the (U,t) trace shows three periods per 0.54 s: the cableway's speed (2 figures).
**Solution:** six pulses per disc revolution → 6·T = 1.08 s per revolution (3 periods mean 0.54 s... careful: 3 periods pass in 0.54 s, so 6 periods = one turn in 1.08 s ✓). The cable runs one circumference: s = π·d = π·0.32 = 1.01 m. v = 1.01/1.08 = **0.93 m/s** (margin 0.01).
**Common mistakes:** counting one period as one revolution (it is one magnet!); using the radius in the circumference.
**Points:** peaks ↔ revolutions (1), s = πd with v = s/t (1), completion + significance (1).

### Q14 (3p) — steering the dynamo: size and frequency of the voltage
**Solution:** faster cableway: both flux change per second *and* rotation frequency grow → voltage up, frequency up. More windings: U_ind ∝ N (the syllabus proportionality): voltage up, frequency unchanged. More magnets: per magnet the same bump but more bumps per revolution → total voltage unchanged, frequency up.
**Common mistakes:** swapping the second and third rows' columns (the classic); thinking more magnets also grow the voltage per magnet induced (it doesn't).
**Points:** 1 per correct row (3).

### Q15 (1p) — the rectifying bridge
**Solution:** a four-diode bridge rectifier, which routes either polarity into the same current direction for the smartphone — that is figure B's bridge layout (the other two drawings either short the source or leave AC through at one polarity).
**Common mistakes:** trusting any "looks-plausible" diode arrangement without tracing both half-cycles.
**Points:** 1p, multiple choice (unticked = 0).

### Q16 (2p) — why AC, and why ramping frequency
**Solution:** a mounted magnet must first be *attracted* to the coil and after passing be *repelled* — force direction must flip each half-turn, so the coil needs alternating current. As the cableway speeds up, each magnet's transit takes less time, so the flips must come faster: the applied AC frequency must rise to stay in step with the motion (a small electromechanical PLL, done by their computer).
**Common mistakes:** saying "because a dynamo makes AC" — here the *supply* must alternate by design; claiming constant frequency works at all speeds.
**Points:** attract-then-repel insight (1), frequency-speed link (1).

## Opgave D — Lycopeen (Q17–Q18)

**Subdomains:** [P11](../P11-quantum-world/) (particle in a box, Pauli, absorption). **BINAS:** h, m_e, e, c.

### Q17 (5p) — the longest absorbed wavelength, box-modelled
**Asked:** 22 π-electrons free to move along a 22-carbon chain; well length L = 21 × mean C–C distance 1.4·10⁻¹⁰ m; show by computation that the maximally absorbed wavelength from this model exceeds the graph's range (> 560 nm).
**Solution:** L = 21·1.4·10⁻¹⁰ = 2.94·10⁻⁹ m. By Pauli's principle each box level holds 2 electrons (paired spins), so 22 electrons fill levels n = 1…11. The longest photon jump is between the highest occupied and the next level: ΔE = E₁₂ − E₁₁ = (144 − 121)·h²/(8m·L²) = 23·h²/(8m·L²) = 23·(6.626·10⁻³⁴)²/(8·9.109·10⁻³¹·(2.94·10⁻⁹)²) = 1.6·10⁻¹⁹ J. Then λ = hc/ΔE = 6.626·10⁻³⁴·3.00·10⁸/1.60·10⁻¹⁹ = 1.24·10⁻⁶ m = 1.24·10³ nm — indeed larger than the 560 nm edge of the printed absorption spectrum. (Sanity: ~2× the true 500 nm absorption peak — the flat box overestimates the well, see Q18.)
**Common mistakes:** filling one electron per level (Pauli gives 2 per level!); computing E₁₁ only instead of the *difference* E₁₂ − E₁₁; miscounting the 21 link lengths between 22 carbons (L = 21·1.4·10⁻¹⁰, not 22); numerics slipping by a factor 10 in 8mL².
**Points:** levels 1–11 filled (1), ΔE formula (1), box formula (1), E_f = hc/λ (1), completion + comparison (1).

### Q18 (2p) — is the effective well longer or shorter than L?
**Solution:** measured λ (≈ 500 nm) is *smaller* than the model's 1.24·10³ nm, so the real transition is energetically *bigger* than the model's ΔE. In E_n = n²h²/(8mL²) the levels rise when L shrinks. So the effective well must be **smaller than L**: the electrons really live between somewhat tighter boundaries than the P–Q marks (the ends of the chain bound the wave function more strictly than the naive picture).
**Common mistakes:** the inverted conclusion (bigger λ → longer well); reasoning from energy in eV directly without anchoring the sign via E ∝ 1/L².
**Points:** not-u > needed direction (1), consistent conclusion (1).

## Opgave E — Stikstof-13 (Q19–Q20)

**Subdomains:** [P02](../P02-medical-imaging-and-radiation/) (nuclear reaction, half-life/activity). **BINAS:** half-life of ¹³N (9.97 min, isotopes table).

### Q19 (3p) — making N-13 in the cyclotron
**Asked:** oxygen-16 is shot with a proton, yielding nitrogen-13 and one other particle: the nuclear reaction equation.
**Solution:** mass numbers: 16 + 1 = 13 + A → A = 4; atomic numbers: 8 + 1 = 7 + Z → Z = 2. The second particle is ⁴₂He, an α particle: **¹⁶₈O + ¹₁H → ¹³₇N + ⁴₂He**.
**Common mistakes:** a neutron as the second particle (charge would not balance); writing ¹³N on the left side.
**Points:** only O + proton left / N right (1), second particle consistent (1), nucleon balance (1).

### Q20 (3p) — how long until the scan?
**Asked:** injected with 0.74 GBq of ¹³N; the photo can start at 0.56 GBq still — the waiting time.
**Solution:** A(t) = A₀·½^(t/t½) with t½ = 9.97 min (BINAS): 0.56/0.74 = 0.757 = ½^x → x = log(0.757)/log(0.5) = 0.40 → t = 0.40·9.97 = **4.0 min**. (Fast enough that the tracer barely decays appreciably — and still made on site because it cannot be shipped.)
**Common mistakes:** rounding the ratio 0.757 to a "nice" fraction — to ¾ (would give 4.1 min, still wrong) or worse to ½ (would give a full half-life, 10 min): compute x = log(0.757)/log(0.5) = 0.40 properly; using t½ = 10 min by heart instead of the isotopes table's 9.97 min.
**Points:** decay formula (1), t½ looked up (1), completion (1).

## Opgave F — H-alfafilter (Q21–Q25)

**Subdomains:** [P10](../P10-electromagnetic-radiation-and-stars/) (Wien, Stefan-Boltzmann, levels), [P01](../P01-oscillations-and-waves/) (interference, phase). **BINAS:** k_W, σ, hydrogen levels.

### Q21 (3p) — temperature of the photosphere
**Solution:** the printed Planck curve peaks at ≈ 5.1·10⁻⁷ m (margin 0.48–0.53 μm readable). Wien: T = k_W/λ_max = 2.898·10⁻³/5.1·10⁻⁷ = 5.7·10³ K.
**Common mistakes:** reading the wavelength at some shoulder rather than the top; inverting k_W.
**Points:** λ_max within band (1), Wien's law (1), completion (1).

### Q22 (3p) — the sunspot's factor in T
**Asked:** inside a sunspot the emitted intensity is 16× lower than outside: how many times lower is the temperature?
**Solution:** P_bron = σ·A·T⁴ and I = P/A → per unit area I ∝ T⁴. Sixteen times less I gives T_spot/T_free = (16)^(1/4) = 2.0 (the fourth root of 16): the spot's surface temperature is **2.0× lower** (about ½ × 5.7·10³ ≈ 2.9·10³ K in absolute terms — still glowing!).
**Common mistakes:** concluding 16× lower T (the T⁴!); applying Wien in reverse for the answer instead of Stefan–Boltzmann as asked by the context.
**Points:** Stefan–Boltzmann (1), I = P/A (1), 16^(1/4) (1).

### Q23 (1p) — which hydrogen transition is Hα?
**Solution:** Hα = 656.28 nm: ΔE = 1240/656.28 = 1.9 eV — the jump from the first excited state (n = 2, −3.4 eV) to the second (n = 3, −1.5 eV) spans exactly that: **option C** (between eerste and tweede aangeslagen toestand). The ground-state transition at 10.2 eV is the deep-UV Lyman α of [P10](../P10-electromagnetic-radiation-and-stars/).
**Common mistakes:** B (n = 1 → 3 sounds grander but is 12.1 eV = 102 nm UV); any Lyman-family choice.
**Points:** 1p, multiple choice.

### Q24 (2p) — why λ = 2d/n for the etalon
**Solution:** rays that bounce once between the half-mirrors travel an extra 2d (down and back) versus the directly transmitted leg. Transmission maxima need those legs *in phase* at the far side, so the path difference must be a whole number of wavelengths: 2d = n·λ → λ = 2d/n. (The phase flips at reflections cancel in pairs, as the paper's setup says.) ∎
**Common mistakes:** writing the constructive condition 2d = (n + ½)λ from grating habits — here design picks integer multiples; forgetting the back-and-forth factor 2.
**Points:** path difference = nλ (1), completion (1).

### Q25 (4p) — etalon order and peak spacing
**Asked:** d = 42.0 μm: the order n of the Hα peak, and Δλ between peaks there.
**Solution:** n = 2d/λ = 2·42.0·10⁻⁶/656.28·10⁻⁹ = 128. The neighbour: λ_{129} = 84.0·10⁻⁶/129 = 651 nm → spacing Δλ = 656 − 651 = 5 nm (≈ 5.1 nm by the unrounded way; also Δλ ≈ λ²/2d × ... = λ/(2d/λ)/... a handy estimate: Δλ ≈ λ/n ≈ 5 nm ✓ the same within rounding). So the second filter's window may be about 5 nm wide — quite a sharp filter to build.
**Common mistakes:** computing n from d instead of 2d (the round trip!); spacing to the (n−1) side with sign flips; giving Δf instead of Δλ.
**Points:** formula (1), n (1), Δλ = λ_n − λ_{n+1} insight (1), completion (1).
