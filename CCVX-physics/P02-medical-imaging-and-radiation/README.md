# P2 — Medical imaging and radiation

- **Dutch:** *medische beeldvorming en ioniserende straling*
- **CCVX syllabus:** subdomain B2 — *Medische beeldvorming*. **Syllabus version used: 15 August 2025 (in force from 1 September 2025).**

## Learning outcomes (CCVX syllabus)

The candidate can:

1. Describe the emission, propagation and absorption of electromagnetic radiation: the concepts absorption (*absorptie*), emission (*emissie*), electromagnetic wave (*elektromagnetische golf*) and photon (*foton*); use E_f = hf and c = λf.
2. Name the different kinds of ionising radiation, their origin and their properties, as well as the risks of these kinds of radiation for humans and the environment, and make calculations with the (equivalent) dose: radioactive decay (*radioactief verval*), isotope (*isotoop*), nucleus (*kern*), proton, neutron, electron, atomic mass unit (*atomaire massaeenheid*, u), ionising and penetrating power (*ioniserend en doordringend vermogen*), range (*dracht*), X-radiation (*röntgenstraling*), α-, β- and γ-radiation, cosmic radiation (*kosmische straling*), background radiation (*achtergrondstraling*), irradiation (*bestraling*) versus contamination (*besmetting*), effective total body dose in relation to radiation protection standards, dosimeter; set up the equation of a nuclear reaction (conservation of mass number A and charge Z, with A = Z + N); dose D = E/m (gray), equivalent dose H = w_R·D (sievert); compute the activity at a given moment and determine it from an (N,t) diagram, with A = −dN/dt and A = (ln 2/t½)·N.
3. Solve problems in which the half-life (*halveringstijd*) or the half-value thickness (*halveringsdikte*) plays a role: N(t) = N(0)·½^(t/t½), A(t) = A(0)·½^(t/t½), I(x) = I(0)·½^(x/d½); read decay curves (*vervalkromme*) and transmission curves (*doorlaatkromme*); at minimum in the context of medical diagnostics and radiation protection (*stralingsbescherming*).
4. Describe medical imaging techniques on the basis of their physical background, name their advantages and disadvantages, and on that basis argue a choice of technique in a given situation: X-ray photo (*röntgenopname*), CT scan, MRI scan, PET scan, ultrasound (*echografie*) and nuclear diagnostics (*nucleaire diagnostiek*); physical backgrounds: half-value thickness of human tissues, magnetic field and resonance, annihilation (*annihilatie*), creation of an electron–positron pair, ultrasound waves, speed of sound in human tissues, absorption, transmission, reflection, tracer (*tracer*).
