# P1 — Oscillations and waves

- **Dutch:** *trillingen en golven*
- **CCVX syllabus:** subdomain B1 — *Informatieoverdracht* (information transfer), including the phenomena sound and echo assumed known. **Syllabus version used: 15 August 2025 (in force from 1 September 2025)** — this version added the Doppler effect (specification 6).

## Learning outcomes (CCVX syllabus)

The candidate can:

1. Analyse oscillation phenomena (*trillingsverschijnselen*) and represent them graphically: a harmonic oscillation (*harmonische trilling*) occurs when the resulting force is proportional to and opposite to the displacement, F⃗_res = −C·u⃗; the displacement then follows u(t) = A·sin(2πt/T) with amplitude A and period (*periode*) T; use f = 1/T, the reduced phase (*gereduceerde fase*) and the phase difference (*faseverschil*) Δφ = Δt/T; compute the maximum speed v_max = 2πA/T. (Given u, computing t is not required.)
2. Make calculations on the natural oscillation (*eigentrilling*) of a mass–spring system (*massa-veersysteem*): T = 2π√(m/C) with m in kg and spring constant (*veerconstante*) C in N/m; know the terms natural frequency (*eigenfrequentie*) and resonance (*resonantie*).
3. Analyse wave phenomena (*golfverschijnselen*) and represent them graphically, using that sound is a wave phenomenon: running wave (*lopende golf*), propagation speed (*voortplantingssnelheid*) v = fλ, echo, speed of sound (*geluidssnelheid*, in air about 343 m/s at 20 °C, BINAS), speed of light (*lichtsnelheid*) c = 3.00·10⁸ m/s, transverse (*transversaal*) and longitudinal (*longitudinaal*); the phase difference between points Δx apart along a wave is Δφ = Δx/λ.
4. Determine the physical properties of oscillations and waves from (u,t) and (u,x) diagrams — at least in the contexts cardiogram and oscillogram — and know the links between the amplitude of an oscillogram and the loudness (*geluidssterkte*) of the tone, and between frequency and pitch (*toonhoogte*).
5. For a standing wave (*staande golf*) analyse the relation between the wavelength and the length of the vibrating medium: node (*knoop*), antinode (*buik*), fundamental tone (*grondtoon*), overtone (*boventoon*); ℓ = n·½λ (n = 1, 2, …) for a medium fixed or open at both ends, ℓ = (2n − 1)·¼λ for a medium closed at one end; at minimum in the context of musical instruments (*muziekinstrumenten*).
6. Explain how the observed wavelength and frequency of a wave change when source and receiver move relative to each other (*dopplereffect*, new in the 2025 syllabus).
