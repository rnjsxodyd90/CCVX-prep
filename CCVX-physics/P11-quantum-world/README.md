# P11 — Quantum world

- **Dutch:** *quantumwereld*
- **CCVX syllabus:** subdomain F1 — *Quantumwereld* (domain: *Quantumwereld en relativiteit*; the relativistic part beyond E = mc² in E2 is not examined). **Syllabus version used: 15 August 2025 (in force from 1 September 2025)** — the quantum-mechanics content was **revised** in this version, exactly as the CCVX change notice says: double-slit reasoning, de Broglie, the particle-in-a-box model with wave functions, tunneling, Pauli's exclusion principle, and energy bands/band gaps with their applications.

## Learning outcomes (CCVX syllabus)

The candidate can:

1. Explain to what extent light shows wave and particle behaviour (*golf-deeltjedualiteit*): explain the origin of diffraction at a single slit (*enkelspleet*); explain how and under which conditions maxima and minima arise at a double slit (*dubbelspleet*); compute the maxima at a grating (*tralie*) with **d·sin(α_n) = n·λ**; interpret interference patterns as *probability distributions* for the detection of photons; describe how these patterns build up at low intensity and mention that photons interfere *with themselves*; reduce constructive and destructive interference to phase differences and path-length differences (*weglengteverschillen*) and vice versa; apply indivisibility (*ondeelbaarheid*) as the characteristic particle property of a photon (context of E2.1); apply the principle that information about the path taken ("which-way", *welke-spleetdetectie*) prevents interference — the one aspect of Heisenberg's uncertainty relation (*onbepaaldheidsrelatie van Heisenberg*) in the syllabus: you cannot speak of wave behaviour and particle behaviour at the same time.
2. Explain, analogously, to what extent electrons show wave and particle behaviour: make calculations with the de Broglie wavelength **λ = h/(mv)**; at minimum in the context of diffraction at crystal lattices (*diffractie aan kristalroosters*).
3. Describe quantum phenomena for some models of confined particles (*opgesloten deeltjes*):
   - describe a particle in an infinitely deep one-dimensional box (*oneindig diepe één-dimensionale energieput*) qualitatively with nodes and antinodes in a wave function (*golffunctie*), and compute its possible energies with **E_n = n²·h²/(8·m·L²)** (E_n in eV);
   - handle that at a finitely deep box the wave function and its probability distribution continue into the barrier, exponentially decaying (context of tunneling);
   - handle the energy spectrum of the hydrogen atom and explain that its discrete character is connected with the arising of nodes and antinodes in the wave function of the electron;
   - interpret (the square of) the position-dependent amplitude of a wave function as a measure of the probability of finding the particle at that spot;
   - the concepts: quantisation (*quantisatie*), ground state, excited state, tunneling (*tunnelen*).
4. Apply the principle that at most one electron can be in the same state (*uitsluitingsprincipe van Pauli*): use electron spin as a little magnet that can lie with or against an external field (e.g. two electrons with opposite spins); mention that in solids the discrete atomic states combine into nearly continuous energy bands (*energiebanden*) with possibly band gaps in between.
5. Analyse materials and electrical components with the band-gap concept:
   - the solar cell (*zonnecel*);
   - distinguish conductors, semiconductors (*halfgeleiders*) and insulators;
   - describe the working of an LDR and an NTC with it.
