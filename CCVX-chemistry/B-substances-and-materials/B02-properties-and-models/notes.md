# B2 — Properties and Models: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX and CvTE syllabi and standard VWO chemistry, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question (see [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) for the statuses).

Dutch terms are in brackets. A code such as *(CvTE M1.4)* points to the matching specification in the [CvTE 2027 syllabus](../../syllabus/cvte-syllabus-scheikunde-vwo-2027.pdf); Binas tables are named by subject, because their numbers differ between editions.

## B2.1 Substances, materials and substance properties

- A substance (*stof*) is one particular kind of matter, such as water, iron or sodium chloride. A material (*materiaal*) is what an object is made of, chosen for its use; it is often a mixture or a combination of substances, such as steel, concrete or a plastic. Both belong to the macro level (*macroniveau*). *(CvTE M6.1)*
- Substance properties (*stofeigenschappen*) belong to the substance itself, so they don't depend on the size or shape of the sample: melting point, boiling point, density, solubility, colour, smell, electrical conductivity and combustibility. Mass, volume, shape and temperature are not substance properties. *(CvTE M2.1)*
- You observe and measure substance properties at macro level, in a sample. The micro level (*microniveau*) is that of the particles: molecules, atoms and ions. This subdomain explains macro properties with particle models. *(CvTE M6.1)*
- Example: water melts at 0 °C, boils at 100 °C at standard pressure p₀ and has a density of about 1.0 g cm⁻³. A colourless liquid with these three values is very probably water. Binas lists melting points, boiling points and densities for many substances. *(CvTE M2.1)*
- Separation methods use differences in substance properties: distillation uses a difference in boiling point, extraction a difference in solubility. See [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md) and [01 Pure substances and mixtures](../../0-basic-knowledge/01-pure-substances-and-mixtures/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.2)*
- **Trap:** density is a substance property, but mass and volume are not. A boiling point is only a substance property at a stated pressure: high in the mountains water boils below 100 °C.

## B2.2 Melting point and melting range

- At a given pressure, a pure substance (*zuivere stof*) melts at one temperature, its melting point (*smeltpunt*), and boils at one temperature, its boiling point (*kookpunt*). While it melts or boils, the temperature stays constant until the phase change is complete. The liquid solidifies at the same temperature as the solid melts (*stolpunt* = *smeltpunt*). *(CvTE M2.2)*
- A mixture (*mengsel*) melts over a melting range (*smelttraject*) and boils over a boiling range (*kooktraject*): the temperature keeps rising during the phase change, because the composition of what is left changes. When salt water boils, the water evaporates and the salt stays behind, so the solution gets more concentrated and its boiling temperature rises further. *(CvTE M2.2)*
- In a heating curve (temperature against time while you heat steadily), a pure substance shows horizontal sections at its melting point and boiling point; a mixture shows sloping sections instead. During a horizontal section the heat breaks bonds between the particles instead of raising the temperature (see [B03](../B03-bonds-and-properties/notes.md)). *(CvTE M2.2)*
- Example: pure water melts at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C (at p₀); sea water starts to freeze below 0 °C and boils above 100 °C, over a range. The phase transitions themselves are in [01 Pure substances and mixtures](../../0-basic-knowledge/01-pure-substances-and-mixtures/notes.md). *(CvTE M2.2)*
- **Trap:** one fixed melting or boiling point points to a pure substance, not to a compound. An element such as bromine also has one boiling point.

## B2.3 Pure substances and mixtures at micro level

- At micro level a pure substance consists of one kind of particle: one kind of molecule (water: only H₂O molecules), one kind of atom (copper: only Cu atoms; helium: only He atoms) or, for a salt, one combination of ions in a fixed ratio (sodium chloride: Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in the ratio 1 : 1). *(CvTE M2.1)*
- A mixture contains two or more kinds of particle, in a ratio that can vary. Air contains N₂ and O₂ molecules, Ar atoms and a little CO₂; salt water contains H₂O molecules, Na⁺ ions and Cl⁻ ions. *(CvTE M2.1)*
- In a particle drawing of a pure molecular substance all the molecules are identical; in a drawing of a mixture you see different particles side by side. *(CvTE M2.1)*
- **Trap:** a compound is not a mixture of elements. In water the H and O atoms are bonded in H₂O molecules in a fixed ratio; a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen consists of separate H₂ and O₂ molecules in any ratio.
- **Trap:** "pure" in daily language is not chemically pure: pure orange juice and mineral water are mixtures.

## B2.4 Decomposable and non-decomposable substances

- A non-decomposable substance (*niet-ontleedbare stof*) is an element (*element*): at micro level its particles contain only one kind of atom (one atomic species, *atoomsoort*). They can be single atoms (He, or the Cu atoms in copper) or molecules (O₂, Cl₂). No chemical reaction can split an element into other substances. *(CvTE M1.8)*
- A decomposable substance (*ontleedbare stof*) is a compound (*verbinding*): its particles contain two or more kinds of atom in a fixed ratio, as in H₂O, CO₂, NaCl and C₆H₁₂O₆. *(CvTE M1.8)*
- A decomposition reaction (*ontledingsreactie*) splits a compound into simpler substances, in the end into elements. By electrolysis (*elektrolyse*): 2 H₂O(l) → 2 H₂(g) + O₂(g). Decomposition by heat is thermolysis (*thermolyse*) and by light photolysis (*fotolyse*); these reaction types are in [C07](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C07-classification-of-reactions/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.3)*
- One element can occur in more than one form: diamond and graphite both consist only of C atoms (their structures are in [B03](../B03-bonds-and-properties/notes.md)), and oxygen O₂ and ozone O₃ only of O atoms. Each is still an element. *(CvTE M1.8)*
- **Trap:** "element" has two meanings: an atomic species (the element oxygen in H₂O) and a non-decomposable substance (the substance oxygen, O₂). A substance made of molecules can still be an element: O₂ and Cl₂ consist of molecules, but of one kind of atom. More in [02 Elements and compounds](../../0-basic-knowledge/02-elements-and-compounds/notes.md).

## B2.5 Molecular substances and salts

- A molecular substance (*moleculaire stof*) consists of molecules. A molecule is a group of non-metal atoms held together by atomic bonds (covalent bonds, *atoombindingen*), as in H₂O, CO₂, NH₃ and C₆H₁₂O₆. Between the molecules act much weaker Van der Waals bonds (*vanderwaalsbindingen*, molecular bonds), plus hydrogen bridges (*waterstofbruggen*) when the molecules have OH or NH groups. *(CvTE M1.9; CvTE M3.1)*
- A salt (*zout*) consists of ions: positive metal ions (or NH₄⁺) and negative ions, held in an ion lattice (*ionrooster*) by ion bonds (*ionbindingen*), the attraction between opposite charges. A salt has no molecules, so its formula is a ratio formula (*verhoudingsformule*): NaCl has Na⁺ and Cl⁻ in the ratio 1 : 1, CaCl₂ has Ca²⁺ and Cl⁻ in the ratio 1 : 2 and Al₂(SO₄)₃ has Al³⁺ and SO₄²⁻ in the ratio 2 : 3. Names and formulas of salts are in [B01](../B01-particle-models/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.9; CvTE M1.10)*
- From the formula: a metal combined with a non-metal, or an ammonium compound such as NH₄Cl, is a salt; only metal atoms means a metal or an alloy; only non-metal atoms usually means a molecular substance. The main exceptions are substances with an atomic lattice, such as diamond (C) and quartz (SiO₂), see [B03](../B03-bonds-and-properties/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.7)*
- At macro level the difference shows in the properties: salts have high melting points and conduct electricity when molten or dissolved; molecular substances have lower melting points and don't conduct as pure substances. See [B04](../B04-bonds-structures-and-properties/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.9)*
- **Trap:** there are no NaCl molecules. Dissolving a salt splits the lattice into separate hydrated ions: NaCl(s) → Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq). Which bonds break and form when this happens is in [C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md).
- **Trap:** a composite ion such as SO₄²⁻ or NH₄⁺ has atomic bonds inside it, but the salt is held together by ion bonds between the ions.

## B2.6 Types of mixture

### Solution: unsaturated and saturated

- A solution (*oplossing*) is a homogeneous mixture in which a substance (the solute, *opgeloste stof*) is spread as separate molecules or ions through a solvent (*oplosmiddel*). It is clear, though it can be coloured, and passes through a filter unchanged: sugar in water, or a blue copper(II) sulfate solution. *(CvTE M2.3)*
- The solubility (*oplosbaarheid*) is the largest amount that dissolves in a given amount of solvent at a given temperature, for example in grams per 100 g of water. An unsaturated solution (*onverzadigde oplossing*) can still dissolve more at that temperature; a saturated solution (*verzadigde oplossing*) holds the maximum, so extra solute stays undissolved and the concentration no longer rises. *(CvTE M2.3)*
- Example: suppose at most 36 g of a salt dissolves in 100 g of water at 20 °C. Then 20 g in 100 g of water is an unsaturated solution, and 50 g in 100 g of water gives a saturated solution with 14 g of solid left at the bottom. *(CvTE M2.3)*
- The solubility of most solids rises with temperature, so a saturated solution that cools down usually forms crystals; gases dissolve less well at higher temperature. *(CvTE M2.3)*
- **Trap:** saturated is not the same as concentrated: a saturated solution of a poorly soluble salt is very dilute (see [B03](../B03-bonds-and-properties/notes.md)).
- **Trap:** sugar dissolves in tea; it doesn't melt.

### Suspension

- A suspension (*suspensie*) is a heterogeneous mixture of small solid particles spread through a liquid, such as chalk (calcium carbonate, CaCO₃) in water or muddy water. It is cloudy, the solid settles on standing (*bezinken*) and you can filter it off (*filtreren*). A precipitate (*neerslag*) in a liquid is also a suspension. *(CvTE M2.3)*
- **Trap:** a filter separates a suspension but not a solution. Dissolved particles pass through the filter with the solvent, so a clear filtrate (*filtraat*) can still contain a dissolved substance.

### Emulsion and emulsifier

- An emulsion (*emulsie*) is a heterogeneous mixture of small droplets of one liquid spread through another liquid that it doesn't mix with: milk (fat droplets in water) and mayonnaise (oil droplets in water). Without help the droplets merge and the liquids separate into two layers (*ontmengen*). *(CvTE M2.3)*
- An emulsifier (*emulgator*) keeps an emulsion stable. Its molecules have a hydrophilic part (*hydrofiel*) that sits in the water and a hydrophobic part (*hydrofoob*) that sits in the oil, so each droplet gets a hydrophilic outer layer and the droplets can't merge. Examples: soap, such as sodium octadecanoate C₁₇H₃₅COO⁻ Na⁺ (an ionic COO⁻ head on a long hydrocarbon tail), and lecithin from egg yolk, which stabilises mayonnaise. Hydrophilic and hydrophobic are explained in [B03](../B03-bonds-and-properties/notes.md). *(CvTE M2.3; CvTE M3.6)*
- **Trap:** an emulsifier doesn't dissolve the oil in the water. The oil stays as droplets, so the emulsion remains cloudy and heterogeneous.

### Alloy

- An alloy (*legering*) is a mixture of a metal with one or more other elements, usually metals, made by melting them together and letting the melt solidify: brass (*messing*: copper and zinc), bronze (*brons*: copper and tin), steel (*staal*: iron with a little carbon, below about 2%) and stainless steel (*roestvrij staal*: iron with chromium and often nickel). *(CvTE M2.3; CvTE M1.7)*
- At micro level the atoms of the added element take places in the metal lattice, or fit between the metal atoms, as carbon does in steel. The different atoms disturb the layers of the lattice, which makes most alloys harder than the pure metal (see [B04](../B04-bonds-structures-and-properties/notes.md)). Because an alloy is a mixture, it usually melts over a range (B2.2). *(CvTE M2.3)*
- **Trap:** an alloy is a mixture, not a compound: its composition can vary, as in the many kinds of brass and steel.

### Homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures

- A homogeneous mixture (*homogeen mengsel*) has the same composition everywhere, and you can't see separate parts, not even with a microscope: solutions (including gas mixtures such as air) and many alloys. *(CvTE M2.3)*
- A heterogeneous mixture (*heterogeen mengsel*) has parts that you can see, by eye or with a microscope, and a liquid one is usually cloudy: suspensions and emulsions. Milk looks the same throughout, but a microscope shows the fat droplets, so it is heterogeneous. *(CvTE M2.3)*
- **Trap:** clear doesn't mean colourless. A blue copper(II) sulfate solution is clear and homogeneous; cloudiness points to a heterogeneous mixture.

## B2.7 Using these concepts in a described study

- B2.7 asks you to apply B2.1–B2.6 to an experiment or study described in the question. The Dutch syllabus says the same in the end term (*eindterm*) of M2: explain macroscopic properties in a described study with particle models. Typical tasks follow. *(CvTE M2 end term)*
- Judging purity from a melting range: suppose a table gives 122 °C as the melting point of pure substance X, and the crystals from a synthesis of X melt between 115 °C and 119 °C. The range and the lower temperature show that the product is impure. After recrystallisation (*herkristallisatie*: dissolving in hot solvent and letting the substance crystallise on cooling) the range should get narrower and move up towards 122 °C. *(CvTE M2.2)*
- Identifying a substance: compare a measured melting point, boiling point and density with Binas values. Different substances can share one value, so use more than one property. *(CvTE M2.1)*
- Reading a heating or cooling curve: a horizontal section means a pure substance changing phase; a sloping section during a phase change means a mixture. *(CvTE M2.2)*
- Explaining observations with particles: a liquid that stays cloudy is a suspension or an emulsion; solid that stays at the bottom of a solution means the solution is saturated; a substance that conducts when molten but not as a solid is a salt. *(CvTE M2.3; CvTE M1.9)*
- Choosing a separation method from the type of mixture: filter a suspension, evaporate (*indampen*) a solution to get the solid back, and distil (*destilleren*) to separate liquids with different boiling points. The methods are in [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.2)*
- **Trap:** a sharp melting point at the table value is strong evidence of purity and identity, not proof: a few special mixtures also melt at one temperature, and different substances can have the same melting point.
