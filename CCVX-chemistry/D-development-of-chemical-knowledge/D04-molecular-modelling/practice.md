# D4 — Molecular modelling: practice

Six original questions on the D4 learning outcomes, in CCVX style.

### chem-d04-q01 | points 3 | outcome D4 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Write the two-step mechanism for the acid-catalysed hydrolysis of methyl ethanoate, and state the role of the acid in the mechanism.
#### Answer
Step 1: a first H₃O⁺ protonates the ester's carbonyl oxygen; Step 2: water attacks the (now electrophilic) carbonyl carbon, the C–O bond to the methoxy group breaks, the proton is released to a water molecule — giving ethanoic acid and methanol. The H₃O⁺ is used in step 1 and re-formed in step 2: the acid is a catalyst.
#### Solution
1. Mechanism means steps with an intermediate: the protonated ester is AC-like (charged intermediate between the steps).
2. Bond-wise: the C=O of the ester accepts a proton; H₂O donates a lone pair (the nucleophile); the –OCH₃ leaves as CH₃OH; the extra proton returns to the water (Cat regenerated).
3. The catalyst works by lowering the activation energy with a different path (see C10); it appears in the steps but not in the overall equation (element conservation).

### chem-d04-q02 | points 3 | outcome D4 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Give the structural formulas of the monomer and of the piece of polymer chain of poly(methyl 2-methylpropenoate) (PMMA), and name the two kinds of monomers that build a condensation polymer instead of an addition polymer.
#### Answer
Monomer CH₂=C(CH₃)–COOCH₃; polymer piece –[CH₂–C(CH₃)(COOCH₃)]–ₙ. A condensation polymer (polycondensation product) is built from monomers with two functional groups each, for example a diacid and a dialcohol, which link with loss of water.
#### Solution
1. The C=C of the methacrylate opens at initiation (radical or ionic), and each monomer adds on: the backbone is –CH₂–C(CH₃)(COOCH₃)– repeated.
2. Addition polymerisation: one monomer type with C=C, no small molecule lost.
3. Polycondensation (condensation polymerisation): two kinds of bifunctional monomers (e.g. HOOC–R–COOH + HO–R′–OH), each step splits off H₂O (compare chem-d03-q03).

### chem-d04-q03 | points 4 | outcome D4 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Write the mechanism for the formation of a copolymer of ethene and propene in three abstract steps (initiation, propagation, termination) in radical form, and explain what "molecular modelling" of the chain means for the properties of the resulting material.
#### Answer
Initiation: R· (from an initiator) adds to CH₂=CH₂ or CH₂=CH–CH₃ → chain radical. Propagation: the radical adds the next monomer, whichever it meets — a random sequence of ethene and propene units. Termination: two chain radicals combine. Molecular modelling of the chain means: the order, branching and length of the units determine the material — for example, more propene disrupts crystallisation and makes the copolymer softer (plastomer), while chain length and crosslinks set strength (see D03, B04).
#### Solution
1. A radical initiator opens the double bond of whatever monomer is present at that place: the chain grows with a mixed backbone.
2. Writing the mechanism in steps (initiation/propagation/termination) is exactly the molecular modelling D4 asks: from the molecular level to the macroscopic carrying structure.
3. Sequence control (random vs blocks) is a real industrial knob: the same two monomers can give an elastic or a rigid copolymer depending on the model of the chain.

### chem-d04-q04 | points 4 | outcome D4 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Limonene, CH₃–C₆H₈–CH=CH₂ with a stereocentre at the ring carbon bearing the vinyl group, exists as (R)- and (S)-limonene (orange and lemon smell). (1) Explain the enantiomer pair. (2) What is optical activity? (3) Why do two enantiomers still behave identically in a lab distillation?
#### Answer
1. The ring carbon carries four different groups (–CH₃, –CH=CH₂, and two different ring paths): one asymmetric carbon gives two mirror-image forms (enantiomers). 2. Optical activity: a solution of one enantiomer rotates the plane of polarised light; the two forms rotate it equally but in opposite directions (dextrorotatory/levorotatory, + and −). 3. Melting point, boiling point and solubility in an apolar (non-chiral) solvent are identical, because no chiral partner distinguishes them in the distillation: they separate identically by boiling point.
#### Solution
1. One stereocentre → two non-superimposable mirror images (see chem-e02-q02).
2. Optical activity is the light-rotation property measured by a polarimeter (only present with excess of one form).
3. Distillation separates by boiling point: identical boiling points → no separation there; chiral resolution needs a chiral partner (chiral chromatography or enzymes).

### chem-d04-q05 | points 3 | outcome D4 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Model the addition of HBr to but-1-ene via the initial carbocation, and explain with that mechanism why the product contains both buta-2-position products and why two enantiomers form if one product has a stereocentre.
#### Answer
Step 1: the H of HBr adds to C1 (giving the more stable secondary carbocation at C2: CH₃–CH₂–CH⁺–CH₃); Step 2: Br⁻ attacks the planar cation equally from both sides, giving CH₃–CH₂–CHBr–CH₃ (2-bromobutane) as a racemic mixture of the two enantiomers, since the C2 stereocentre is formed with both faces equally likely.
#### Solution
1. The H adds at C1 (more substituted cation is more stable): the positive charge sits at C2.
2. A carbocation is planar (steric number 3): the Br⁻ nucleophile comes in equally from top and bottom: two configurations at C2, 50/50: a racemic mixture.
3. Molecular modelling of the intermediate (planar or pyramidal) predicts the stereochemistry of the product — enantiomers in equal amounts where no chiral director exists (see chem-d04-q04 for what that means for optical activity).

### chem-d04-q06 | points 4 | outcome D4 | difficulty hard
#### Question
A research group writes a catalysed polycondensation: n HO–R–COOH (hydroxyl acids) → –[O–R–CO]–ₙ + n H₂O, catalysed by a lipase enzyme. (1) Which kind of monomer is this, and why can one molecule polymerise alone? (2) Write the two steps of the condensation at one ester bond. (3) What advantage does the enzyme catalyst offer the mechanism over a strong-acid catalyst?
#### Answer
1. A monomer with both functional groups in one molecule (a hydroxyl acid / AB monomer), so it can esterify with its own kind head-to-tail; one kind of molecule is enough to grow a chain. 2. Step 1: the COOH group of one and the OH of another condense (their bonds break, C–O ester bond forms) with enzyme-held transition state; Step 2: H₂O leaves, the enzyme releases the ester product and is free again. 3. The enzyme works at mild pH and temperature and with high selectivity (fewer side reactions), while strong acid would attack other groups of R and give coloured by-products; also the enzyme is a biodegradable (protein) catalyst.
#### Solution
1. AB monomers (one A and one B group per unit) polymerise by themselves — in contrast with the diacid+dialcohol (AA+BB) case of chem-d03-q03/q04.
2. Polycondensation = repeating condensation: each ester bond formation splits off H₂O.
3. A catalyst is part of the mechanism steps; choosing one that works gently and specifically is how chemists model and control the chain (D4 core: mechanism + catalysis).
