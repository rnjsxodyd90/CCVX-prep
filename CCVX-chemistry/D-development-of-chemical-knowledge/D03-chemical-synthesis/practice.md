# D3 — Chemical synthesis: practice

Eight original questions on the D3 learning outcomes, in CCVX style.

### chem-d03-q01 | points 3 | outcome D3.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Polyethene is made from ethene: n CH₂=CH₂ → –[CH₂–CH₂]–ₙ. Give the same relation for polypropene (propene, CH₂=CH–CH₃): write the structural formula of the repeating unit of polypropene.
#### Answer
–[CH₂–CH(CH₃)]–ₙ (with the CH₃ group as a side group on every second C atom of the chain)
#### Solution
1. In an addition polymerisation one bond of each C=C double bond opens and links to the next monomer.
2. For propene the CH₃ group does not react — it hangs off the chain: the repeating unit is –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–.
3. Written in CCVX style: –[CH₂–CH(CH₃)]–ₙ (the brackets mark the repeating unit).

### chem-d03-q02 | points 3 | outcome D3.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Name the monomer of each addition polymer: (1) –[CH₂–CCl₂]–ₙ, (2) –[CH₂–CH(OCOCH₃)]–ₙ.
#### Answer
1. 1,1-dichloroethene, CH₂=CCl₂. 2. Vinyl ethanoate (ethenyl ethanoate), CH₂=CH–OCOCH₃.
#### Solution
1. To find the monomer of an addition polymer, close the double bond between the two chain carbons of the repeating unit and remove the chain ends: CH₂=CCl₂ (two Cl on the same C).
2. The same rule gives CH₂=CH–OCOCH₃; the side group –OCOCH₃ is not part of the backbone that formed the chain.
3. Rule for the reverse direction (monomer → polymer): write the C=C opened with a free valency at each side, then link n of them into a chain.

### chem-d03-q03 | points 3 | outcome D3.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
PET is a polyester made from terephthalic acid (benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid) and ethane-1,2-diol: HO–CH₂–CH₂–OH. (1) Which bond type links the two monomers in the polymer chain? (2) Which small molecule is released at every link? (3) Why is this called a condensation polymerisation?
#### Answer
1. An ester bond (–COO–). 2. Water. 3. Because each link step joins two molecules and splits off a small molecule (water) — a condensation reaction, repeated many times.
#### Solution
1. The –COOH group of the acid and the –OH of the alcohol react to an ester: –COO– (just like the small-scale esterifications in C01).
2. H₂O is split off per link (OH from the acid, H from the alcohol).
3. A condensation joins molecules while a small molecule leaves; repeated along the two reactive ends of each monomer, it forms a chain — a condensation polymer, in contrast to the addition polymers of q01–q02 where nothing is split off.

### chem-d03-q04 | points 4 | outcome D3.1 | difficulty hard
#### Question
A piece of a polymer chain reads: –NH–(CH₂)₆–NH–CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–NH–(CH₂)₆–NH–CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–. (1) Name the polymer class and the bond that is repeated. (2) Give the structural formulas of the two monomers.
#### Answer
1. A polyamide (nylon-6,6): the amide (peptide) link –CO–NH– is repeated. 2. Hexane-1,6-diamine, H₂N–(CH₂)₆–NH₂, and hexanedioic acid (adipic acid), HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH.
#### Solution
1. The repeated –CO–NH– is an amide link: a polyamide. The sample with (CH₂)₆ and (CH₂)₄ is nylon-6,6.
2. To find the monomers of a condensation polymer, cut the amide bonds and put H₂O back: the N side takes H → H₂N–(CH₂)₆–NH₂; the C=O side takes OH → HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH.
3. Each monomer has two functional groups; only then can small links grow into a chain of condensation steps (with two reactive groups on only one monomer you would get a dimer, not a polymer, see C01 traps).

### chem-d03-q05 | points 3 | outcome D3.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Describe the three steps of an addition polymerisation mechanism — initiation, propagation, termination — for the polymerisation of phenylethene (styrene) started by a peroxide initiator R–O–O–R′.
#### Answer
Initiation: the initiator splits into radicals (R–O·) that open a monomer's double bond, creating a chain radical. Propagation: the chain radical repeatedly adds monomer molecules, each time opening a C=C and passing the radical to the new chain end. Termination: two chain radicals meet and combine (or disproportionate), ending the chains.
#### Solution
1. Initiation: R–O–O–R′ → 2 R–O· (weak O–O bond, easily split by heat); R–O· adds to CH₂=CH(Ph) and forms R–O–CH₂–CH·(Ph): the chain radical, with an unpaired electron on C2.
2. Propagation: the chain radical opens a next monomer: –CH·(Ph) + CH₂=CH(Ph) → –CH₂–CH(Ph)–CH·(Ph), thousands of times; the chain grows one unit per hit.
3. Termination: two radicals meet: combination (two chains merge into one longer chain) or disproportionation (one radical takes an H atom of the other, leaving a saturated and an unsaturated chain end); the unpaired electrons pair up and growth stops.

### chem-d03-q06 | points 3 | outcome D3.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Why is a radical initiator needed to start many addition polymerisations, and why does the polymerisation stop being dependent on initiator once it has started?
#### Answer
An initiator is needed because the monomer's C=C bond does not open by itself at moderate temperature: the initiator supplies the first free radicals that open the bond. Once running, propagation keeps creating a new radical at every step, so the chains keep growing from few radicals — only when radicals pair up (termination) is new initiator needed.
#### Solution
1. A free radical is a particle with an unpaired electron (see C04); only at high temperature or with an initiator do C=C bonds start reacting with themselves, because the first attack on the strong C=C bond needs the highly reactive radical.
2. Every propagation step consumes one radical and creates one radical — the chain carries its own "spark" onward, so the small amount of initiator starts very many monomer molecules (a chain reaction).
3. Termination removes radicals; if terminations outpace initiations, the reaction stops — which is why handling, concentration and temperature are chosen carefully.

### chem-d03-q07 | points 4 | outcome D3.3 | difficulty medium
#### Question
A sample of polyethene has an average molar mass of 1.0·10⁵ g mol⁻¹. The repeating unit –CH₂–CH₂– weighs 28.05 g mol⁻¹. (1) Calculate the average degree of polymerisation (number of monomers per chain) of this polymer. (2) Explain, using propagation and termination, why adding more initiator leads to shorter chains on average.
#### Answer
1. About 3.6·10³ (∼3600) monomers per chain. 2. More initiator creates more radicals, hence more chains growing at the same time on the same amount of monomer, so each chain gets less monomer before a termination ends it: shorter average chain length.
#### Solution
1. Degree of polymerisation = M(polymer) / M(repeating unit) = 1.0·10⁵ / 28.05 = 3.6·10³ (two significant figures).
2. With twice as much initiator, twice as many chains start simultaneously; the total amount of monomer is fixed, and chains terminate after a stochastic time — so each chain is shorter on average.
3. The distribution of chain lengths is why polymer samples speak of an average molar mass (and why a sample never has one exact mass spectrum m/z).

### chem-d03-q08 | points 3 | outcome D3.3 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Car tyres are rubber that has been "vulcanised" with sulfur. Explain (1) what the sulfur does to the polymer molecules, (2) which property that gives the material, and (3) one reason why a vulcanised rubber, unlike a normal thermoplastic, cannot simply be melted down for recycling.
#### Answer
1. The sulfur builds crosslinks (–S–S– bridges) between the rubber chains. 2. The crosslinks hold the chains together: the rubber becomes elastic and keeps its shape instead of flowing when heated (a thermosetting behaviour). 3. Because the whole tyre is effectively one crosslinked molecule: melting would require breaking atomic bonds, so the material chars rather than melts.
#### Solution
1. Vulcanisation forms –S–S– bridges between the polyisoprene chains: crosslinks.
2. Crosslinks stop chain-flow under tension: elastic recovery and dimensional stability under heat — wanted properties in a tyre.
3. A thermoplastic's chains stay separate and slide over each other on heating (see B04); with crosslinks that sliding is impossible, so reheating destroys the material rather than softening it: very hard to recycle by remelting.
