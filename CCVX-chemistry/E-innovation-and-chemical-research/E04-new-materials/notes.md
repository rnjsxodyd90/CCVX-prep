# E4 — New Materials: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX and CvTE syllabi and standard VWO chemistry, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question (see [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) for the statuses).

## New applications in existing and new markets

- The outcome asks you to explain, with at least one example from medicines, food or materials, how the chemical industry develops a new application: a known substance finds a new use, or a new material enters an existing market or opens a new one. The route runs from a wanted property, through the structure that gives it ([E01](../E01-chemical-research/notes.md)), to tests, scaling up, safety approval and production. *(SLO E4)*
- Example, a known material in a new market: titanium dioxide, TiO₂, has long been the white pigment in paint. As nanoparticles it no longer scatters visible light, so it is transparent, but it still absorbs UV. That gave transparent sunscreens and, as a photocatalyst, self-cleaning glass.
- Example, a new material in existing markets: polyethene fibres with extremely long, aligned chains (Dyneema, developed by DSM) are very strong for their low mass. They replace steel cable and nylon in ropes and fishing nets, and are used in cut-resistant gloves and light bullet-resistant vests.
- **Trap:** the question is about the reasoning: which property is needed, which structure gives it, and why the new product is better than the existing one (cost, safety, sustainability). Name the property and the structure, not only the product.

## Nanotechnology

- Nanotechnology (*nanotechnologie*) makes and uses particles and structures with at least one dimension between about 1 and 100 nm (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m), only a few to a few hundred atoms across. *(SLO E4)*
- The smaller the particles, the larger the surface area per volume: for a cube with edge a, surface / volume = 6a² / a³ = 6/a. Cutting a cube with an edge of 1.0 cm into cubes with an edge of 10 nm gives 10¹⁸ small cubes with a total surface of 6.0·10² m², instead of 6.0·10⁻⁴ m²: a million times more. A larger surface means faster reactions and more active catalysts ([C04](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C04-reaction-kinetics/notes.md)). *(CvTE R3.1)*
- At this scale properties depend on the size. Gold nanoparticles are red instead of gold-coloured and act as catalysts; TiO₂ and ZnO nanoparticles are transparent but still absorb UV; silver nanoparticles slowly release Ag⁺ ions, which kill bacteria.
- Application, medicine: the mRNA vaccines against COVID-19 pack the fragile mRNA in lipid nanoparticles, which protect it and help it into the cells.
- **Trap:** a nanoparticle is the same substance as the bulk material; only the size, and so the surface, differs. The health and environmental risks of nanoparticles are still being studied, partly because such small particles can enter cells.

## Nanocoatings

- A nanocoating is a surface layer whose structure at the nanometre scale gives a material a new surface property without changing the material underneath. *(SLO E4)*
- Lotus effect: lotus leaves are covered with hydrophobic wax on a pattern of tiny bumps at micrometre and nanometre scale. Water touches only the tips of the bumps, forms almost round drops and rolls off, taking dirt with it. Coatings that copy this keep fabrics, glass and paint clean (water repellence, [E01](../E01-chemical-research/notes.md)).
- Photocatalytic TiO₂: a very thin TiO₂ layer on glass absorbs UV from sunlight and forms radicals that break down organic dirt. Under UV the surface also becomes very hydrophilic, so rainwater spreads into a thin film that rinses the loosened dirt off instead of forming drops.
- Other examples: coatings filled with hard silica (SiO₂) nanoparticles make plastic spectacle lenses and car paint more scratch-resistant, and silver coatings on catheters and wound dressings kill bacteria.
- **Trap:** "self-cleaning" can work in two opposite ways: the lotus effect is extremely water-repellent, the TiO₂ coating extremely water-attracting. Say which mechanism a described coating uses.

## Ceramic materials

- Ceramic materials (*keramische materialen*) are inorganic, non-metallic solids, made by shaping a fine powder and heating it below its melting point until the grains grow together (sintering). Technical ceramics are oxides such as Al₂O₃ and ZrO₂ (ion lattices) or compounds such as SiC and Si₃N₄ (atomic lattices). *(CvTE M4.2; SLO E4)*
- The strong bonds in all directions make them hard, heat-resistant and chemically stable, and most are electrical insulators. They are also brittle: the lattice can't deform, so a crack runs straight through ([E01](../E01-chemical-research/notes.md)).
- Applications: hip-implant heads and dental crowns of Al₂O₃ or ZrO₂; cutting and grinding tools of SiC; the insulator of a spark plug (Al₂O₃); ZrO₂ kitchen knives that stay sharp for a long time. In a composite, fibres in the ceramic stop cracks from running through, so it is less brittle: carbon-fibre-reinforced silicon carbide is used for the brake discs of sports cars.
- **Trap:** hard isn't the same as tough: a ceramic resists scratching and wear, but breaks when it is hit or bent.

## Molecular structure

- Developing a new material or medicine means designing the molecular structure that gives the wanted property: choosing the characteristic groups, the chain length, the crosslinks or the spatial structure. *(CvTE M4.2; SLO E4)*
- Examples of that reasoning are in [E01](../E01-chemical-research/notes.md): COO⁻ Na⁺ groups and crosslinks for a superabsorbent, ester bonds for a biodegradable plastic, very long chains for strong fibres, a plasticizer for flexible PVC.
- In medicines, chemists change one part of a molecule at a time and test what that does to the effect, the side effects and the uptake: a polar or charged group improves solubility in water, a hydrophobic group passage through membranes ([E02](../E02-selectivity-and-specificity/notes.md)). Computer models of how a molecule fits its target help to choose what to make ([D04](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D04-molecular-modelling/notes.md)).
- **Trap:** a change in structure rarely improves only one property: a group that makes a medicine more soluble in water can also make it pass membranes less easily.

## Antibiotics

- Antibiotics (*antibiotica*) are substances that kill bacteria or stop their growth. They act selectively because they block something that bacteria have and human cells lack, or have in a different form, such as the bacterial cell wall or the bacterial ribosome. They don't work against viruses. *(SLO E4)*
- Penicillin, discovered by Fleming in 1928 in a mould, contains a strained four-membered ring with an amide bond, the β-lactam ring. It binds permanently in the active position of the enzyme that crosslinks the chains of the bacterial cell wall. Growing bacteria then form weak cell walls and burst. Human cells have no cell wall, so they aren't affected ([E02](../E02-selectivity-and-specificity/notes.md)).
- Resistance (*resistentie*): some bacteria make an enzyme, β-lactamase, that hydrolyses the amide bond in the β-lactam ring and makes the penicillin inactive; others have a changed target enzyme that the antibiotic no longer fits, as in MRSA. Each use of an antibiotic kills the sensitive bacteria and leaves the resistant ones, which then spread.
- Responses: combining amoxicillin with clavulanic acid, which blocks β-lactamase; prescribing antibiotics only when they are needed; and developing new antibiotics. That last step is commercially unattractive, because a new antibiotic is kept in reserve and used as little as possible, so governments and foundations support the research.
- **Trap:** the bacteria become resistant, not the patient.

## Pharmaceutical research

- Developing a medicine (*geneesmiddelenonderzoek*) usually takes ten years or more and follows fixed steps. *(SLO E4)*
    - Target: a molecule in the body or in a pathogen, usually an enzyme or a receptor, whose activity has to be blocked or changed.
    - Lead compound: a substance with some of the wanted effect, found by testing many substances, from nature or from a computer model of the active position.
    - Modification: chemists make related molecules and test which change gives a stronger effect, fewer side effects or better uptake (molecular structure, above).
    - Testing: preclinical tests on cells and animals, then clinical trials in people. Phase 1 tests safety in a small group, usually healthy volunteers; phase 2 the effect and the dose in a small group of patients; phase 3 compares the medicine with a placebo or an existing medicine in a large group of patients. After approval, in Europe by the EMA, side effects are still monitored.
- Example of modification: salicylic acid relieves pain and fever but irritates the stomach. In 1897 Bayer turned it into acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) by reacting it with ethanoic anhydride: C₇H₆O₃ + C₄H₆O₃ → C₉H₈O₄ + C₂H₄O₂.
- Enantiomers can differ in effect ([E02](../E02-selectivity-and-specificity/notes.md)). Thalidomide (Softenon) was sold around 1960 as a sedative, and one of its enantiomers caused severe birth defects. The two enantiomers turn into each other in the body, so even the pure "safe" enantiomer wouldn't have been safe. Since then the enantiomers of a chiral medicine are studied separately, and many new medicines are sold as a single enantiomer.
- Markets: a patent protects a new medicine for 20 years, much of which the development itself uses up. After that, other companies can sell cheaper copies (generics).
- **Trap:** a lead compound is a starting point, not the final medicine. And selling a single enantiomer only helps if the enantiomers don't turn into each other in the body, as thalidomide shows.

## CCVX versus the Dutch syllabus

- **In both:** the structure and properties of ceramics, polymers, alloys and composites are central-exam material in the Netherlands (CvTE M4.2).
- **Dutch syllabus only:** the SLO list for E4 also names lab-on-a-chip: a small chip with narrow channels in which chemical analyses are done on tiny samples. CCVX leaves it out.
