Dutch terms are in brackets. A code such as *(CvTE M1.4)* points to the matching specification in the [CvTE 2027 syllabus](../../syllabus/cvte-syllabus-scheikunde-vwo-2027.pdf); Binas tables are named by subject, because their numbers differ between editions.

## Life cycles of substances, materials and products

- Sustainable (*duurzaam*) means meeting today's needs in a way that doesn't make it harder for future generations to meet theirs. The Dutch syllabus moved the four outcomes of this folder, unchanged, to T6.1–T6.4. *(CvTE T6.1)*
- The life cycle (*levenscyclus*) of a substance, material or product covers every stage: extracting the raw materials, making it, transporting it, using it, and what happens at the end: reuse, recycling, incineration or landfill. A life-cycle analysis (*levenscyclusanalyse*, LCA) adds up the raw materials, energy, emissions and waste over all those stages.
- Example: a single-use plastic bottle against a returnable glass bottle. The glass bottle is heavier, so transport costs more energy, and it has to be washed, but it is used many times. Which one is more sustainable depends on how often the glass bottle is reused and how far it travels.
- Cradle to cradle (*cradle-to-cradle*) means designing a product so that, at the end of its life, all its materials can serve as raw materials for new products; see the cycles in [C03](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C03-conservation-laws-and-cycles/notes.md).
- **Trap:** judging only the use phase. An electric car emits no CO₂ while driving, but making its battery and generating its electricity can cause emissions.

## Choosing between alternatives

- With knowledge of life cycles you can propose a choice between alternatives (*keuze tussen alternatieven*): substances, materials or processes. Typical criteria: renewable or finite raw materials, energy use and energy source, atom economy and waste, emissions, toxicity and risks, recyclability, and transport. See [F02](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F02-green-chemistry/notes.md) and [E03](../../E-innovation-and-chemical-research/E03-sustainability/notes.md). *(CvTE T6.2)*
- Example: making hydrogen. The atom economy (*atoomeconomie*) is the mass of the wanted product as a percentage of the total mass of the reactants.
  - From natural gas, overall CH₄(g) + 2 H₂O(g) → CO₂(g) + 4 H₂(g): 4 × 2.016 / (16.04 + 2 × 18.02) × 100% = 15.5%.
  - By electrolysis of water, 2 H₂O(l) → 2 H₂(g) + O₂(g): 2 × 2.016 / (2 × 18.02) × 100% = 11.2%, if the oxygen isn't used.
  - On atom economy alone, natural gas wins. But it uses a finite raw material and gives CO₂, while electrolysis with renewable electricity uses only water and gives harmless oxygen. Over the whole life cycle, electrolysis with renewable electricity is the more sustainable choice *(CvTE T6.5)*.
- **Trap:** basing the choice on one criterion; weigh all of them, and say which ones decide.

## The social importance of chemistry

- The social importance of chemistry (*maatschappelijke betekenis van de chemie*) shows in two ways. It supplies much of what we depend on (fertilisers, medicines, materials, clean water, batteries), and it supplies the knowledge to measure and reduce the harm those products and processes can do. *(CvTE T6.3)*
- Examples: chemical analysis detects pollutants at concentrations of ppm or ppb, which makes environmental standards (*grenswaarden*) possible ([G04](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G04-environmental-requirements/notes.md)); green chemistry makes processes cleaner ([F02](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F02-green-chemistry/notes.md)); and chemists found why CFCs destroy the ozone layer and developed replacements ([A15](../A15-development-of-chemical-knowledge/notes.md)).

## World problems and chemical concepts

- The Dutch syllabus and CCVX name six world problems (*wereldvraagstukken*); for each, know which chemical concepts help to understand and solve it. *(CvTE T6.4)*

### Global food supply

- World food supply (*wereldvoedselvoorziening*): crops need nitrogen, and the ammonia synthesis, N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) ⇌ 2 NH₃(g), turns nitrogen from the air into fertilisers such as ammonium nitrate, which is 35.00% nitrogen by mass. Concepts: equilibrium and catalysis ([C05](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C05-chemical-equilibrium/notes.md)), plus crop protection and food preservation. *(CvTE T6.4)*

### Sustainable energy supply

- Sustainable energy supply (*duurzame energievoorziening*): biofuels such as bioethanol from fermentation, C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) → 2 C₂H₅OH(aq) + 2 CO₂(g); solar energy; hydrogen from electrolysis of water with renewable electricity; storing electricity in batteries *(CvTE T6.5)*. Concepts: redox and electrochemical cells, heat of reaction, the carbon cycle. See [F03](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F03-energy-conversion/notes.md) and [G03](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G03-energy-and-industry/notes.md). *(CvTE T6.4)*
- **Trap:** a biofuel is not automatically CO₂-neutral: growing, transporting and processing the crop also cost energy, often from fossil fuels.

### Drinking water

- Drinking water supply (*(drink)watervoorziening*): purifying water by filtration, adsorption on activated carbon, killing bacteria with ozone or UV light, and desalinating seawater by distillation or with membranes. Concepts: separation methods ([D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md)), solubility, and the ions in water. *(CvTE T6.4)*

### Availability of raw materials

- Availability of raw materials (*beschikbaarheid van grondstoffen*): oil, natural gas, metal ores and phosphate are finite, biomass is renewable. Recycling keeps elements in the cycle: aluminium made from scrap takes only a small fraction of the energy needed to make it from ore by electrolysis. Concepts: conservation of mass, cycles ([C03](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C03-conservation-laws-and-cycles/notes.md)), redox for extracting metals. *(CvTE T6.4)*

### Global warming

- Global warming (*opwarming van de aarde*): CO₂ and other greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels strengthen the greenhouse effect, the enhanced greenhouse effect (*versterkt broeikaseffect*). *(CvTE T5.2, T6.4)*
- Fuels with relatively more hydrogen give less CO₂ per joule. Using Binas: heats of formation, with H₂O(l), burning 1 mol of methane releases about 8.9·10⁵ J and gives 1 mol CO₂: 1.12 mol CO₂ per MJ. Burning 1 mol of propane releases about 22.2·10⁵ J and gives 3 mol CO₂: 1.35 mol CO₂ per MJ. *(CvTE T5.2)*
- A biofuel releases carbon that the plants took from the air shortly before (a short carbon cycle), while a fossil fuel adds carbon that was stored underground for millions of years. See [F03](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F03-energy-conversion/notes.md).
- **Trap:** the hole in the ozone layer and the enhanced greenhouse effect are different problems; don't explain global warming with the ozone hole.

### Global pollution

- Pollution of the Earth (*vervuiling van de aarde*): fuels contain some sulfur, so burning them emits SO₂, written simply as S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g). At the high temperature in an engine, nitrogen and oxygen from the air form nitrogen oxides: N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2 NO(g). SO₂ and nitrogen oxides cause acid rain, and nitrogen oxides also cause smog. A catalytic converter removes CO and NO from car exhaust: 2 CO(g) + 2 NO(g) → 2 CO₂(g) + N₂(g). Plastics such as polyethene hardly break down in nature, while polyesters can be broken down by hydrolysis of their ester bonds, under the right conditions such as industrial composting. Concepts: combustion, catalysis, hydrolysis, polymers. See [G02](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G02-environmental-impact-assessment/notes.md). *(CvTE T5.2, T6.4)*
