## A10.1 Chemical concepts

- In the CvTE syllabus the A10 end term has no specifications of its own: each concept was placed in the sphere where it fits, in italics if it is assumed knowledge from lower school, the school exam or another subject. Such a concept can appear in a central-exam question without any explanation, but it isn't asked about for its own sake. The codes below say where each concept went.
- **Aggregation state/phase** (*aggregatietoestand, fase*): solid, liquid or gas. The state symbol follows the formula: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water: NaCl(s), H₂O(l), CO₂(g), Na⁺(aq). See [sb1–sb5](../../0-basic-knowledge/01-pure-substances-and-mixtures/notes.md) and [sb6–sb10](../../0-basic-knowledge/02-elements-and-compounds/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.3)*
- **Alcohols** (*alcoholen*): organic compounds with a hydroxyl group (–OH) on a carbon atom that has only single bonds; the systematic names (alkanols) end in -ol: methanol CH₃OH, ethanol CH₃–CH₂–OH. See [B01](../../B-substances-and-materials/B01-particle-models/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.3)*
- **Ammonia** (*ammonia*): in this list the Dutch word *ammonia*, a solution of NH₃ in water, written NH₃(aq); it is a weak base. The substance NH₃ itself is Dutch *ammoniak*, also "ammonia" in English. See [sb17–sb18](../../0-basic-knowledge/05-acids-and-bases/notes.md) and [C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.4)*
- **Atomic mass unit** (*atomaire massa-eenheid*), u: one twelfth of the mass of a ¹²C atom, 1.66·10⁻²⁷ kg. Atomic and molecular masses are given in u: Cl 35.45 u, H₂O 18.02 u. See [B01](../../B-substances-and-materials/B01-particle-models/notes.md) and [C02](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C02-chemical-calculations/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.3, R&A1.2)*
- **Greenhouse effect** (*broeikaseffect*): gases such as CO₂, CH₄ and water vapour absorb infrared radiation from the Earth's surface and send part of it back, which keeps the lower atmosphere warmer. Extra emissions cause the enhanced greenhouse effect (*versterkt broeikaseffect*). See [F03](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F03-energy-conversion/notes.md) and [A14](../A14-sustainability/notes.md). *(CvTE T5.2)*
- **Carbon acids** (*carbonzuren*): carboxylic acids, with the carboxyl group –COOH; they are weak acids, and their names end in -oic acid: ethanoic acid CH₃COOH. See [B01](../../B-substances-and-materials/B01-particle-models/notes.md) and [C05](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C05-chemical-equilibrium/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.3)*
- **Coefficient** (*coëfficiënt*): the number in front of a formula in a reaction equation, such as the 2 in 2 H₂O. You balance an equation by changing coefficients only. See [C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.1)*
- **Distillate** (*destillaat*): the liquid collected after a distillation, i.e. the vapour that was condensed; what stays behind in the flask is the residue. See [sb1–sb5](../../0-basic-knowledge/01-pure-substances-and-mixtures/notes.md) and [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Extracting agent** (*extractiemiddel*): the solvent used in an extraction, chosen because the wanted substance dissolves well in it and the rest of the mixture doesn't. Making coffee or tea extracts flavour and caffeine with hot water. See [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Phase transition** (*fase-overgang*): a change of phase: melting and solidifying (*smelten, stollen*), evaporating and condensing (*verdampen, condenseren*), subliming and deposition (*sublimeren, rijpen*). Bonds between the particles break or form; the particles themselves stay the same. See [sb1–sb5](../../0-basic-knowledge/01-pure-substances-and-mixtures/notes.md) and [B03](../../B-substances-and-materials/B03-bonds-and-properties/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.3)*
- **Filtrate** (*filtraat*): the liquid that passes through a filter; the solid left on the filter is the residue. See [sb1–sb5](../../0-basic-knowledge/01-pure-substances-and-mixtures/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Index** (*index*): the subscript number in a formula, giving how many atoms or groups one formula unit contains. In H₂O the index 2 belongs to H; in Ca(NO₃)₂ the 3 belongs to O and the 2 to the whole NO₃ group. See [sb6–sb10](../../0-basic-knowledge/02-elements-and-compounds/notes.md) and [B01](../../B-substances-and-materials/B01-particle-models/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.3)*
- **Indicator** (*indicator*): a substance whose colour depends on the pH. Each indicator changes colour over its own pH range, listed in the Binas table of acid–base indicators; it shows the end point of a titration. See [C05](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C05-chemical-equilibrium/notes.md) and [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Mobile phase/eluent** (*mobiele fase, loopvloeistof*): in chromatography, the liquid or gas that moves along the stationary phase and carries the components with it. Components that dissolve well in it and stick little to the stationary phase move fastest. In paper chromatography the eluent is a solvent; in gas chromatography the mobile phase is a carrier gas. See [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Molarity/molar** (*molariteit, molair*), M: the concentration in mol per litre of solution; 0.10 M HCl means 0.10 mol L⁻¹. See [C02](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C02-chemical-calculations/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A1.2)*
- **Caustic soda** (*natronloog*): sodium hydroxide solution, written Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq). In everyday English the name also means solid NaOH, but in this syllabus it is the solution. See [sb17–sb18](../../0-basic-knowledge/05-acids-and-bases/notes.md) and [C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.4)*
- **Ignition temperature** (*ontbrandingstemperatuur*): the lowest temperature to which a fuel must be heated, with enough oxygen present, for it to start burning. See [sb12–sb16](../../0-basic-knowledge/04-reactions/notes.md) and [C06](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C06-energy-calculations/notes.md). *(CvTE R5.1)*
- **Decomposition reaction** (*ontledingsreactie*): one substance splits into two or more. By an electric current, electrolysis (*elektrolyse*): 2 H₂O(l) → 2 H₂(g) + O₂(g). By light, photolysis (*fotolyse*): 2 AgBr → 2 Ag + Br₂. By heat, thermolysis (*thermolyse*): CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g). See [sb12–sb16](../../0-basic-knowledge/04-reactions/notes.md) and [C07](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C07-classification-of-reactions/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.3)*
- **Incomplete combustion** (*onvolledige verbranding*): combustion with too little oxygen, which gives carbon monoxide or soot (C) as well as, or instead of, CO₂: 2 CH₄ + 3 O₂ → 2 CO + 4 H₂O. Carbon monoxide is toxic. See [sb12–sb16](../../0-basic-knowledge/04-reactions/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.3)*
- **Solvent** (*oplosmiddel*): the substance in which another substance dissolves, such as water, ethanol or hexane; polar and hydrophilic substances dissolve well in water. See [B03](../../B-substances-and-materials/B03-bonds-and-properties/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Reagent** (*reagens*): a substance used to detect another one by a characteristic change. Limewater turns cloudy with CO₂: Ca²⁺(aq) + 2 OH⁻(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l). White anhydrous copper(II) sulfate turns blue with water: CuSO₄(s) + 5 H₂O(l) → CuSO₄·5H₂O(s). See [sb12–sb16](../../0-basic-knowledge/04-reactions/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Residue** (*residu*): what stays behind, on the filter after filtration or in the flask after distillation or evaporation. See [sb1–sb5](../../0-basic-knowledge/01-pure-substances-and-mixtures/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Titration** (*titratie*): adding a solution of known concentration from a burette to a measured volume of sample until the reaction is just complete (the equivalence point, shown by an indicator or a pH meter), and calculating the unknown amount from the volume used. Example: 25.00 mL of hydrochloric acid needs 12.50 mL of 0.100 M caustic soda (H₃O⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → 2 H₂O(l)), so [H₃O⁺] = 0.01250 × 0.100 / 0.02500 = 0.0500 mol L⁻¹. See [C02](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C02-chemical-calculations/notes.md) and [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Trivial name** (*triviale naam*): a traditional, non-systematic name, such as acetic acid (systematic: ethanoic acid), glucose or ammonia. See [B01](../../B-substances-and-materials/B01-particle-models/notes.md). *(CvTE M1.3)*
- **Calibration line** (*ijklijn*): a graph of a measured signal (absorbance, peak area) against the known concentrations of standard solutions; the concentration of a sample is read off it. See [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md) and [A08](../A08-scientific-instruments/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A2.1)*
- **Hydrochloric acid** (*zoutzuur*): a solution of hydrogen chloride in water, written H₃O⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq); the gas HCl(g) is hydrogen chloride (*waterstofchloride*). See [sb17–sb18](../../0-basic-knowledge/05-acids-and-bases/notes.md) and [C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.4)*
- **Trap:** ammonia, caustic soda and hydrochloric acid are solutions here; write the particles that are present: NH₃(aq), Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq), H₃O⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq). And (l) is only for a pure liquid: a solution in water gets (aq).
- **Trap:** balance with coefficients, never by changing an index: changing H₂O into H₂O₂ makes a different substance.

## A10.2 Biological concepts

- **Respiration** (*ademhaling*; the Dutch syllabus now writes *ademhaling/gaswisseling*): in cells, glucose is oxidised by oxygen to carbon dioxide and water, which releases energy: C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) + 6 O₂(g) → 6 CO₂(g) + 6 H₂O(l). Breathing and gas exchange (*gaswisseling*) in the lungs supply the O₂ and remove the CO₂. It is the reverse of photosynthesis. See [G01](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G01-chemistry-of-life/notes.md). *(CvTE L1.1)*
- **Blood** (*bloed*): carries O₂, CO₂, nutrients, ions and heat through the body; buffers such as the hydrogen carbonate buffer (CO₂ + H₂O / HCO₃⁻) keep its pH at about 7.4. See [C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md) and [E02](../../E-innovation-and-chemical-research/E02-selectivity-and-specificity/notes.md). *(CvTE L1.1)*
- **Cell** (*cel*): the smallest unit of living matter, enclosed by a cell membrane; processes such as respiration and protein synthesis take place in it. See [G01](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G01-chemistry-of-life/notes.md). *(CvTE L1.1)*
- **Cell membrane** (*celmembraan*): a double layer of phospholipids, hydrophilic heads outwards and hydrophobic tails inwards, with proteins in it. Small apolar molecules pass through it more easily than ions. See [E02](../../E-innovation-and-chemical-research/E02-selectivity-and-specificity/notes.md). *(CvTE L1.1, L2.3)*
- **Chromosomes** (*chromosomen*): long DNA molecules packed with proteins in the cell nucleus; they carry the genes. CCVX still lists them, but the Dutch syllabus dropped them and now names the gene (*gen*) instead. See [G01](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G01-chemistry-of-life/notes.md).
- **Ecosystem** (*ecosysteem*): all organisms in an area together with their non-living surroundings, linked by cycles of matter, such as the carbon cycle, and a flow of energy. Dropped from the Dutch syllabus. See [C03](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C03-conservation-laws-and-cycles/notes.md).
- **Heredity** (*erfelijkheid*): the passing on of traits from parents to offspring through DNA. Dropped from the Dutch syllabus. See [G01](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G01-chemistry-of-life/notes.md).
- **Organism** (*organisme*): a living individual, from a bacterium to a plant or an animal. *(CvTE L1.1)*
- **Digestion** (*spijsvertering*): enzymes break large food molecules down by hydrolysis into small ones that can be absorbed: proteins into amino acids, starch into glucose, fats into glycerol and fatty acids. See [G01](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G01-chemistry-of-life/notes.md). *(CvTE L1.1, L1.4)*
- **Transport** (*transport*): the movement of substances through the body, in the blood, and across membranes; it depends on whether a substance is hydrophilic or hydrophobic, on the membranes and on the pH. See [E02](../../E-innovation-and-chemical-research/E02-selectivity-and-specificity/notes.md). *(CvTE L1.1, L2.3)*
- **Gene** (*gen*), Dutch syllabus only: a section of DNA that codes for a protein through the genetic code. See [G01](../../G-society-chemistry-and-technology/G01-chemistry-of-life/notes.md). *(CvTE L1.1)*
- **Trap:** in chemistry, respiration means the oxidation of glucose in cells, not just breathing in and out.

## A10.3 Physics concepts

- **Pressure** (*druk*), p, in pascal (Pa = N m⁻²); also in bar (1 bar = 10⁵ Pa). Compressing a gas mixture raises the concentrations, so the rate goes up, and an equilibrium shifts to the side with fewer gas particles if the two sides differ in that number. See [C04](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C04-reaction-kinetics/notes.md) and [C05](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C05-chemical-equilibrium/notes.md). *(CvTE R3.1)*
- **Energy** (*energie*), E, in joule (J): chemical energy, heat, electrical energy and light can be converted into each other, but energy is never created or destroyed (the law of conservation of energy). See [C06](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C06-energy-calculations/notes.md) and [C09](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C09-quality-of-energy/notes.md). *(CvTE R5.5)*
- **Force** (*kracht*), F, in newton (N = kg m s⁻²): pressure is force per area, p = F / A. No longer a separate concept in the Dutch syllabus.
- **Light** (*licht*): electromagnetic radiation that the eye can see, with wavelengths of about 400–700 nm. Light provides the energy for photosynthesis and photolysis, and UV light can break bonds in polymers. See [F03](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F03-energy-conversion/notes.md) and [B04](../../B-substances-and-materials/B04-bonds-structures-and-properties/notes.md). *(CvTE L1.2)*
- **Mass** (*massa*), m, in kilogram (kg); in chemistry mostly in g. See [C02](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C02-chemical-calculations/notes.md). *(CvTE R&A1.2)*
- **Radioactivity** (*radioactiviteit*): unstable atomic nuclei (radioactive isotopes) decay by themselves, emitting α, β or γ radiation. The activity is in becquerel (Bq), one decay per second. No longer in the Dutch syllabus. See [sb11](../../0-basic-knowledge/03-atomic-structure/notes.md).
- **Potential/voltage** (*spanning*), U, in volt (V). The voltage of an electrochemical cell follows from the standard electrode potentials: under standard conditions a zinc–copper cell gives 0.34 − (−0.76) = 1.10 V (Binas: standard electrode potentials). The Dutch syllabus no longer lists voltage as a concept, but uses the standard electrode potential. See [C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md) and [F03](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F03-energy-conversion/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.10)*
- **Radiation** (*straling*): energy carried by electromagnetic waves (radio waves, infrared, visible light, UV, X-rays, γ rays) or by fast particles (α and β). In chemistry: infrared radiation and the greenhouse effect, and UV radiation breaking bonds (photolysis, the ozone layer). No longer a separate concept in the Dutch syllabus. See [B04](../../B-substances-and-materials/B04-bonds-structures-and-properties/notes.md) for UV-light sensitivity and [A14](../A14-sustainability/notes.md) for global warming.
- **Current** (*stroomsterkte*), I, in ampere (A = C s⁻¹): the flow of charge. In a cell or an electrolysis, electrons carry it through the external wire and ions through the solution. See [C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md) and [F03](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F03-energy-conversion/notes.md). *(CvTE R1.14)*
- **Temperature** (*temperatuur*), T, in kelvin (K): T = t + 273 with t in °C. It is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles. See [C04](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C04-reaction-kinetics/notes.md). *(CvTE R3.1)*
- **Heat** (*warmte*), Q, in joule (J): energy transferred because of a temperature difference, Q = c<sub>w</sub> × m × ΔT for water. Warming 1.00 kg of water by 10.0 K takes 4.18·10³ × 1.00 × 10.0 = 4.18·10⁴ J. See [C06](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C06-energy-calculations/notes.md). *(CvTE R5.5)*
- **Trap:** heat and temperature are different things: temperature (K) describes the particles of a substance, heat (J) is energy that flows from hot to cold.

## Relating the concepts

- The end term also asks you to connect concepts across subjects. Respiration (biology) is an oxidation of glucose (chemistry) whose energy is partly released as heat (physics). The greenhouse effect links CO₂ (chemistry) to infrared radiation (physics) and to ecosystems (biology). Electrolysis links current and voltage (physics) to redox reactions (chemistry).
- **Trap:** a biology or physics word in a chemistry question still needs a chemistry answer: name the substances, particles or reactions involved.

## CCVX versus the Dutch syllabus

- **In both:** most concepts, spread over the CvTE spheres (codes above); many are printed in italics there as assumed knowledge.
- **CCVX only:** chromosomes, ecosystem and heredity, which the Dutch syllabus dropped. Force, radioactivity, voltage and radiation don't appear as concepts in the CvTE text either; its A10 section says that concepts adding nothing in chemistry to their everyday meaning are no longer included.
- **Dutch syllabus only:** gene (*gen*) and gas exchange (*gaswisseling*) in L1.1, the enhanced (*versterkt*) greenhouse effect in T5.2, and the stationary phase (*stationaire fase*) next to the mobile phase in R&A2.1.
