# A10 — Using chemical concepts: practice

Five original questions on the A10 learning outcomes (recognise and relate chemical, physical and biological concepts), in CCVX style.

### chem-a10-q01 | points 3 | outcome A10 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Give the exact concept each sentence needs (choose correctly, not a synonym):
1. "A solution of NH₃ in water" as the Dutch name *ammonia*;
2. the proportionality constant in c = n / V with c in mol L⁻¹;
3. the white solid left on a filter after filtering a suspension.
#### Answer
1. Ammonia (the ammonium-free solution of NH₃ — not the gas *ammoniak*). 2. Molarity (c is the molarity of the solution; "0.1 M"). 3. The residue (on the filter); the filtrate is the liquid that passed.
#### Solution
1. The CCVX vocabulary makes ammonia (base dissolved in water, NH₃(aq)) a usable concept; the substance NH₃ is ammonia in English (NH₃ itself).
2. The molarity (ordinary speech) = concentration in mol L⁻¹.
3. Residue on the filter, filtrate through it; in distillation, the distillate condenses and the residue stays behind — A10 keeps those pairs straight.

### chem-a10-q02 | points 3 | outcome A10 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Choose the correct physical concept in each item: (1) a battery pushes electrons through a wire: current / voltage?; (2) the pop of colliding molecules in warm gas relates to: temperature / pressure?; (3) a radioactive source's activity is counted in which unit?
#### Answer
1. Current (the flow of charge, ampère); voltage (potential difference) is what pushes it. 2. Temperature (average kinetic energy of the particles; pressure is the number of collisions on the wall per area). 3. Becquerel (Bq = decays per second).
#### Solution
1. Voltage drives, current flows (electrical pressure and electrical flow).
2. Temperature is the energy scale of random motion; pressure is a mechanical consequence (per area).
3. Radioactivity is measured in Bq (1 Bq = one decay per second); related but different: the becquerel to absorbed-dose concepts (Gy/Sv), A10's precision here is what CCVX asks.

### chem-a10-q03 | points 4 | outcome A10 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Link the biological and chemical concepts of one event: "after a heavy meal, blood in the gut wall takes up glucose." (1) What biological concepts are at work here? (2) Where does digestion by chemical formulas sit in this chain? (3) Connect: what transport concept links biology with F3/F01 in the syllabus?
#### Answer
1. Organism and digestion (breaking down starch in food), cells (gut wall cells of the small intestine taking up glucose), cell membranes (selective passage), transport (blood to the liver and muscles). 2. The chemistry in terms of formula: enzymatic hydrolysis of starch to glucose (C₆H₁₀O₅ units + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆) — the abiotic equation which underlies "digestion". 3. Transport: the blood brings nutrients/glucose to cells everywhere; in industrial cells and processes the transport of substances through pipes and membranes is the same concept at a different scale.
#### Solution
1. The biological skeleton: ecosystems → organism → cells → cell membrane: nutrient processing happens in specialised structures.
2. The chemical part: hydrolysis of the polysaccharide into the monosaccharide (the body's digestion at the level of bonds).
3. Transport connects the two worlds (body versus block diagram): substances move to where they work — the A10 task is to keep both vocabularies side by side and translate.

### chem-a10-q04 | points 4 | outcome A10 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Examine a physical/chemical crossover: the pressure in an aerosol can rises when the sun catches your bike basket. (1) Describe what happens with the particles inside. (2) Two possible consequences for the spray: more pressure and a formula that says how much more.
#### Answer
1. The gas particles (propellant) move faster at higher temperature and hit the walls more often and harder: the pressure rises (particle collision model at its purest). 2. Ideal-gas relation: p·V = n·R·T, so at fixed V and n, p is proportional to T (in kelvin): from 293 K to 313 K the pressure rises by 313/293 ≈ 1.07, so 7% more pressure (plus extra from the liquid's vapour pressure rising with temperature, a second effect).
#### Solution
1. Particle picture: higher T means higher kinetic energy, more and harder collisions per unit area: higher pressure.
2. p = nRT/V: T absolute in kelvin; conversion as in c02 concepts (298 K is the default room temperature).
3. Aerosol cans state "do not heat above 50 °C" – the physical-concept story that goes with the hazard.

### chem-a10-q05 | points 4 | outcome A10 | difficulty hard
#### Question
A journalist writes: "'Carbonic acid' in sparkling water is felt by the body through the trigeminal nerve; it is not the same as tasting sour." Rewrite this in chemical-neutral terms of the A10 concepts (acids, receptors, indicators at most in words), and explain the value of the distinction between 'chemical acidity' and 'the felt senses'.
#### Answer
Renamed: sparkling water contains dissolved CO₂ with a small acid share (CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCO₃⁻ + H₃O⁺): the classic concepts here are the weak acid 'H₂CO₃' and pH. The reported sensation (tingling) is mediated by the somatosensory nerve (biological nerve-receptor concept), which is a different channel from the taste receptors for sour (which respond to H₃O⁺-level acidity directly). The distinction matters: "acidity" as a chemical concept is a measured [H₃O⁺]; "sour" and "tingling" are biological responses of the perceiver — drugs/drinks can tickle receptors via CO₂ at the same pH at which they wouldn't taste that sour. Keep the two vocabularies apart; A10 is the work of keeping them linked but distinct.
#### Solution
1. Chemical side: dissolution equilibrium and pH (the weak-acid equilibrium from C01/05-acids-and-bases).
2. Biological side: receptor concept (an organism responding to a chemical stimulus through one of several channels).
3. Value of the separation: chemical and biological concepts share objects but not meaning — a careful A10 practitioner cites the right vocabulary on the right level.
