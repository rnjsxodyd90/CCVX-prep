## A11 Context and concepts

- A context (*context*) is the situation a question is about: daily life (*leefwereld*), such as food, medicines or cleaning; a profession (*beroep*), such as a factory, a hospital lab or water treatment; or science (*wetenschap*), such as new research. Concepts (*concepten*) are the chemistry you use in it: particles and bonds, reactions, equilibrium, energy, amounts. Reasoning in terms of context and concepts (*redeneren in termen van context-concept*) means recognising which concepts apply in a context and using them to make predictions (*voorspellingen*) and estimates (*schattingen*), do calculations (*berekeningen*), and give arguments (*argumentatie*). *(CvTE A11.1)*
- In the exam almost every question starts with a context text, often about something you have never heard of, but the concepts are always ones from the syllabus. So the skill is to look through the unfamiliar story to the familiar chemistry.
- Translate everyday words into chemical concepts: fizzing means a gas is formed; rusting means iron is oxidised (a redox reaction); hard water contains Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions; "dissolves in fat, not in water" means hydrophobic; "kept in a cool place" points to reaction rate.
- **Trap:** using the everyday meaning of a word: in chemistry a salt is any ionic compound, not just table salt, and a pure substance (*zuivere stof*) is one substance, not "natural" or "healthy".

## Worked outline: an unfamiliar context

- Context: an antacid tablet against heartburn contains 0.500 g of magnesium hydroxide, Mg(OH)₂. How much stomach acid can one tablet neutralise? Suppose stomach acid is hydrochloric acid with [H₃O⁺] = 0.10 mol L⁻¹. *(CvTE A11.1)*
- Step 1, recognise the concepts. OH⁻ is a base and H₃O⁺ an acid, so this is an acid–base reaction ([C01](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C01-chemical-processes/notes.md)). Magnesium hydroxide hardly dissolves in water (Binas: solubility of salts), so it reacts as a solid.
- Step 2, write the equation: Mg(OH)₂(s) + 2 H₃O⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + 4 H₂O(l).
- Step 3, calculate ([C02](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C02-chemical-calculations/notes.md)). M(Mg(OH)₂) = 24.31 + 2 × (16.00 + 1.008) = 58.33 g mol⁻¹, so the tablet contains 0.500 / 58.33 = 8.57·10⁻³ mol. That reacts with 2 × 8.57·10⁻³ = 1.71·10⁻² mol H₃O⁺, which is in 1.71·10⁻² / 0.10 = 0.17 L = 1.7·10² mL of stomach acid.
- Step 4, predict and argue. The pH in the stomach rises as H₃O⁺ is used up. An antacid with sodium hydrogen carbonate instead would also form a gas: HCO₃⁻(aq) + H₃O⁺(aq) → CO₂(g) + 2 H₂O(l), which can be a reason to prefer magnesium hydroxide.
- **Trap:** one Mg(OH)₂ neutralises two H₃O⁺; using a 1 : 1 ratio gives half the right volume.
