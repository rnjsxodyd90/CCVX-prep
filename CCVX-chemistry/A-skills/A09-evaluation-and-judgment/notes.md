## A9.1 Reasoned judgment and choice

- A reasoned judgment (*beargumenteerd oordeel*) is a conclusion about a situation, or a choice between alternatives, that rests on stated arguments, data and chemical knowledge. *(CvTE A9.1)*
- Work in steps: state the question and the options; choose criteria (yield, energy use, emissions, safety, cost, sustainability); collect or calculate the data for each option; weigh them; conclude, and say how certain the conclusion is.
- In Dutch exams this is the verb *beargumenteren* (argue, reason): an answer that uses the given data and chemical knowledge in a logical line to support a choice or a claim.
- Example: should a country promote bioethanol as a fuel? Part of the argument can be calculated: C₂H₅OH(l) + 3 O₂(g) → 2 CO₂(g) + 3 H₂O(l) has a heat of reaction (*reactiewarmte*) ΔE = [2 × (−3.935·10⁵) + 3 × (−2.86·10⁵)] − (−2.78·10⁵) = −13.67·10⁵ J per mol ethanol (Binas: heats of formation). The CO₂ comes from carbon that the crop took from the air shortly before (a short carbon cycle, see [F03](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F03-energy-conversion/notes.md)). But growing the crop, fermenting and distilling also cost energy, often from fossil fuels, so the net CO₂ saving is smaller than it looks.
- **Trap:** listing only the advantages of your choice; a reasoned choice also says why the other option is worse on the criteria that matter.

## A9.2 Scientific arguments, social considerations and personal views

- Scientific arguments (*wetenschappelijke argumenten*) rest on measurements, data and theory. Anyone can check them, and they don't depend on who says them: "this substance breaks down slowly in soil", "burning ethanol releases 13.67·10⁵ J mol⁻¹". *(CvTE A9.2)*
- Normative social considerations (*normatieve maatschappelijke overwegingen*) are about what a society values or finds acceptable: health, safety, fairness, cost, jobs, nature, the law. They often contain "should", "acceptable" or "fair": "farmland should be used for food first".
- Personal views (*persoonlijke opvattingen*) are one person's preferences or beliefs: "I don't like the idea of chemicals on my food".
- Example, a proposed ban on a pesticide. Scientific: the substance is toxic to bees above a certain concentration and is found in surface water. Normative: protecting pollinators and drinking water outweighs the extra cost for farmers (or the reverse), or the precautionary principle (*voorzorgsbeginsel*): act before the harm is fully proven. Personal: "I only buy organic vegetables".
- **Trap:** a value judgment dressed up as a fact. "Natural substances are safer" sounds scientific but isn't (many natural substances are toxic), and "the risk is small" is a scientific statement while "the risk is acceptable" is a normative one.

## A9.3 Citing sources

- Back up facts with their source (*bron*): author or organisation, title, date, and publisher or web address, so that a reader can check them. *(CvTE A9.3)*
- In an exam answer, say which data you used: "according to the table in the introduction", "Binas: heats of formation".
- The CvTE text prints this specification in grey italics: school exam only.

## A9.4 Judging reliability

- To judge the reliability (*betrouwbaarheid*) of information, ask of every source: who wrote it, and with what expertise? Was it checked by other experts before publication (peer review)? How recent is it? Does the author or the funder have an interest in the outcome, such as a manufacturer judging its own product? Are the data and the method given, so that others can check them? Do independent sources agree? *(CvTE A9.4)*
- Then judge its value for this question: a reliable source can still be irrelevant, for example a study of a different substance or under different conditions.
- Example: a company website claiming that its bioplastic "breaks down completely in nature" is less reliable than a peer-reviewed study that measured the breakdown under stated conditions. And even that study, if it was done in an industrial composting plant, says little about breakdown in the sea.
- The CvTE text prints this specification in grey italics: school exam only. See also [A01](../A01-using-information-skills/notes.md) and, on how reliable scientific knowledge is, [A15](../A15-development-of-chemical-knowledge/notes.md).
- **Trap:** treating "published" or "on a university website" as proof; check the points above.
