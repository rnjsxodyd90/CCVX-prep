# A8 — Scientific instruments and skills: practice

Six original questions on the A8 learning outcomes, in CCVX style. These skills are marked inside content questions throughout the exam.

### chem-a08-q01 | points 3 | outcome A8.3 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A student pipettes 25.0 mL of ethanol and weighs it: 19.95 g. Calculate the density of ethanol in the correct number of significant figures.
#### Answer
0.798 g mL⁻¹ (three significant figures)
#### Solution
1. ρ = m / V = 19.95 g / 25.0 mL = 0.798 g mL⁻¹.
2. Multiplying and dividing: the answer gets as many significant figures as the measurement with the fewest — here 25.0 (three) against 19.95 (four), so three.
3. The calculator shows 0.798 exactly; if it showed 0.7980, the trailing zero would still not be written (that would claim four figures).

### chem-a08-q02 | points 3 | outcome A8.3 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Apply the significant-figures rules to: (1) the mass of two portions weighed off as 12.35 g and 0.8 g, added together; (2) the pH of a solution with [H₃O⁺] = 4.0·10⁻³ mol L⁻¹.
#### Answer
1. 13.2 g (one decimal); 2. pH = 2.40 (two decimals).
#### Solution
1. Adding and subtracting: the answer gets as many decimals as the measurement with the fewest decimals — 0.8 g has one decimal, so 12.35 + 0.8 = 13.15 rounds to 13.2 g.
2. A logarithm gets as many decimals as the original value has significant figures: 4.0·10⁻³ has two significant figures, so pH = −log (4.0·10⁻³) = 2.40 with two decimals.
3. Note that the zero after the decimal in 2.40 counts and must be written.

### chem-a08-q03 | points 4 | outcome A8.4 | difficulty medium
#### Question
For each job, choose the most accurate glassware from the list: volumetric flask, volumetric pipette, graduated pipette, burette, measuring cylinder, erlenmeyer flask.
1. Make exactly 250.0 mL of a 0.100 M standard solution from a solid.
2. Deliver exactly 10.00 mL of the unknown solution into a flask for a titration.
3. Add small, varying amounts of titrant until the colour changes.
4. Measure roughly 50 mL of water to dissolve the solid.
#### Answer
1. Volumetric flask; 2. volumetric pipette; 3. burette; 4. measuring cylinder.
#### Solution
1. The volumetric flask (with mark) is made to hold one exact volume: dissolve, fill to the mark, mix.
2. The volumetric pipette delivers one exact volume (10.00 mL); a graduated pipette is for varied but still accurate volumes.
3. The burette releases a variable volume drop by drop while you read the added volume to 0.05 mL.
4. "Roughly" means high accuracy is wasted: a measuring cylinder is quick and sufficient. Accuracy of the titration chain is set by its least accurate step.

### chem-a08-q04 | points 4 | outcome A8.6 | difficulty medium
#### Question
In an equilibrium calculation you end up with the equation x² / (0.100 − x) = 1.8·10⁻⁴. Solve it for x exactly (quadratic equation) and give the answer in the correct number of significant figures. Check that your root is chemically meaningful.
#### Answer
x = 4.2·10⁻³
#### Solution
1. Cross-multiply: x² = 1.8·10⁻⁴ × (0.100 − x), so x² + 1.8·10⁻⁴·x − 1.8·10⁻⁵ = 0.
2. Quadratic formula with a = 1, b = 1.8·10⁻⁴, c = −1.8·10⁻⁵: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a = (−1.8·10⁻⁴ + √(3.2·10⁻⁸ + 7.2·10⁻⁵)) / 2 = (−1.8·10⁻⁴ + 8.49·10⁻³) / 2 = 8.31·10⁻³ / 2.
3. x = 4.2·10⁻³ (two significant figures, as the given constants have two).
4. The other root is negative (−4.3·10⁻³) and has no chemical meaning (a concentration cannot be negative). The result is larger than 0.100 × 0.05? x/0.100 ≈ 4 %, so in a real equilibrium problem you would still check whether an approximation would have been allowed (it is, just).

### chem-a08-q05 | points 3 | outcome A8.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
In an experiment you measure the mass of a piece of magnesium ribbon as it reacts in a gas flame every 10 seconds for one minute. Describe how you present these results so that the trend is clearest, and how you then read off the mass at 25 s.
#### Answer
Draw a graph of mass against time, plot the points, draw one smooth best-fit curve, and read the 25 s value from the curve (interpolation).
#### Solution
1. A graph shows a trend far better than a table: time on the horizontal axis (the quantity you chose), mass on the vertical axis (the quantity you measured), with units on both axes.
2. Plot the measured points with correct scale and labels, then draw one smooth curve of best fit — measurement points always contain small errors, so connecting dot-to-dot is wrong.
3. Reading a value between the points from that curve is interpolation (25 s lies within the measured range); reading beyond it would be extrapolation, which is less reliable.

### chem-a08-q06 | points 3 | outcome A8.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Look up the density of ethanol in Binas (table of densities of organic liquids) and calculate the mass of exactly 50 mL of ethanol. Give the answer in the correct number of significant figures.
#### Answer
39 g (two significant figures)
#### Solution
1. Binas gives ρ(ethanol) = 789 kg m⁻³ = 0.789 g mL⁻¹ (three significant figures).
2. m = ρ × V = 0.789 × 50 = 39.45 g.
3. The volume 50 mL has two significant figures (no decimal point), so the answer gets two: 39 g. If the volume had been measured with a pipette as 50.00 mL, the answer would have been 39.45 g — the instrument decides the figures.
