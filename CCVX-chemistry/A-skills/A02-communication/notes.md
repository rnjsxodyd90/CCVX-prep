# A2 — Communication: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX and CvTE syllabi and standard VWO chemistry, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question (see [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) for the statuses).

Dutch terms are in brackets. A code such as *(CvTE M1.4)* points to the matching specification in the [CvTE 2027 syllabus](../../syllabus/cvte-syllabus-scheikunde-vwo-2027.pdf); Binas tables are named by subject, because their numbers differ between editions. The CvTE syllabus gives this end term without specifications: *(SLO A2)* points to the [SLO handreiking](https://www.slo.nl/handreikingen/havo-vwo/handreiking-se-schei-hv/toetsen-schoolexamen/domein-vaardigheden/), which elaborates it.

## A2 Communication

- Communicating (*communiceren*) means reporting clearly about chemistry in writing, orally and digitally, also to people outside the field ("in the public domain"). In Dutch schools this is assessed through reports, posters, presentations and essays. *(SLO A2)*
- The CvTE text prints "orally and digitally" (*mondeling en digitaal*) in grey italics, which means the Dutch central exam doesn't test those parts; only written communication is left.
- For a CCVX candidate it means writing clear exam answers in English: full sentences when a question says describe or explain, the CCVX terms from the glossary in [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md), a unit with every result, and structural formulas drawn by the usual conventions.
- It can't be tested as a separate skill in a written exam, so it is low priority: it counts through the quality of every written answer.
