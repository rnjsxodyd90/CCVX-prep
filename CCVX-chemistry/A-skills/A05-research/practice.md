# A5 — Research: practice

Five original questions on the A5 learning outcome (analyse a problem into specific research, do it, draw conclusions), in CCVX style.

### chem-a05-q01 | points 3 | outcome A5 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A school says: "Students want to know if magnesium firework contains magnesium." Turn the wish into (1) a researchable question and (2) a short first investigation you would propose with what you can do in a school lab.
#### Answer
1. "Does a flame test of the firework residue show the spectra/emission that magnesium sources produce, and does adding a precipitant for Mg²⁺ give a precipitate after dissolving the residue?" 2. Dissolve residue, do a flame test (white bright flame) and precipitate with NaOH: white Mg(OH)₂ that dissolves on acidifying confirms Mg²⁺; second line: compare against a known magnesium salt solution under the same protocol.
#### Solution
1. The researchable question states what is measured (flame colour / precipitate) and in which sample (the residue).
2. Proposed investigation = the tools you actually have (school lab: flame, filtration, simple reagents), plus the comparison with a known standard — otherwise the observation has no baseline.
3. A5 demands exactly this turn: from "I want to know if" into a question with a measuring strategy.

### chem-a05-q02 | points 3 | outcome A5 | difficulty medium
#### Question
In an experiment to measure the content of vitamin C in juices by titration with iodine, a student pours "juice into one flask" and titrates once. Criticise this plan and give three improvements.
#### Answer
1. One titration is a single measurement: no precision estimate; repeat each juice at least three times and report the spread.
2. "Pours juice in one flask" gives no defined quantity: pipette a measured volume (e.g. 10.00 mL) with a volumetric pipette.
3. No blank/reference: run a titration with water (or a known ascorbic-acid standard) to calibrate the endpoint and the iodine solution.
#### Solution
1. Repeatability: spread shows the precision; without it, no numbers mean anything.
2. Volume measurement with the accuracy of the rest of the chain (volumetric pipette).
3. Calibration/blank: the titrant's exact strength must be standardised (against a standard solution), and a blank corrects any fast side oxidation.
4. These are standard hygiene of analytical research — the A5 skills the exam packs into real questions.

### chem-a05-q03 | points 4 | outcome A5 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Results of measuring the nitrate content of a river at five occasions (mg per L): 42, 45, 41, 118, 43. (1) Give the appropriate descriptive statistics you would use. (2) Which processing step must be taken with the 118 result before doing more? (3) Give a conclusion in one sentence.
#### Answer
1. Median (43 mg/L) with the outlier excluded, or mean after justifying an exclusion; the ordinary mean (57.8) is distorted. 2. Check whether the 118 is a measurement error or a real spike: repeat analysis on the retained portion of that sample, and record lab notes — only with a documented reason may an outlier be discarded. 3. "The river's nitrate content was around 43 mg/L at the measured days, with one as-yet-unexplained high measurement" (honest, bounded).
#### Solution
1. With an obvious outlier the median is the robust average; means are sensitive to outliers.
2. You never silently delete data: the lab's rule is repeat/retain, and justify any exclusion on evidence (pipetting error, diluted second take, etc.).
3. Conclusions must name the anomaly, not airbrush it.

### chem-a05-q04 | points 4 | outcome A5 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Formulate a conclusion for a study that compared two anti-acid chewing tablets with acid via titer titration: tablet A neutralised 1.8 mmol of acid per tablet, tablet B 1.2 mmol per tablet, each measured in triplicate with similar spread. Write the full conclusion as the report demands: answer to the question and evidence with numbers.
#### Answer
"Under the tested conditions (back-titration with NaOH after excess HCl), tablet A neutralised 1.8 mmol of acid per tablet and tablet B 1.2 mmol per tablet (each n = 3): tablet A neutralised 1.5 times as much acid per tablet as tablet B. The conclusion that A is stronger per tablet follows directly from these measurements; price and comfort conclusions fall outside it."
#### Solution
1. Answer first: the measured numbers, with the conditions and the ratio.
2. Bound the claim: per tablet, in vitro titration — no claim about what happens in the stomach.
3. Evidence with numbers is the A5-discipline: 1.8 vs 1.2 mmol, ratio 1.5, n = 3 each.

### chem-a05-q05 | points 4 | outcome A5 | difficulty hard
#### Question
A follow-up study after a finding "solubility of salt S at 20 °C = 60 g/L" is planned: is the measured value consistent with the saturated-solution method used? Design the verification study briefly (hypothesis, plan, acceptance criterion).
#### Answer
Hypothesis: "If the saturation step reaches equilibrium, a second, longer-saturated sample at 20 °C gives the same solubility (within the repeatability, say ±2 g/L)." Plan: prepare two samples in parallel with stirring for 1 h and for 4 h at 20.0 °C thermostated, filter at temperature through pre-warmed filters, evaporate aliquots to dryness, weigh; do both in duplicate. Acceptance criterion: the 1 h and 4 h averages and duplicates agree within ±2 g/L; if the 4 h sample is higher, the saturation was not complete in the first study's protocol, so that protocol must change.
#### Solution
1. Verification studies test the method, not only the number: varying the saturation time probes equilibrium.
2. Temperature/precision programme spelled out: duplicates, temp control, evaporation with precision weighing.
3. An acceptance criterion set in advance doubles as the study's honesty device — the A5/A09 line between measuring and judging.
