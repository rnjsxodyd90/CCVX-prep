# A13 — Systems, change and energy: practice

Five original questions on the A13 learning outcome (describe chemical processes as systems in terms of substances, particles, reactivity and energy), in CCVX style.

### chem-a13-q01 | points 3 | outcome A13 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A cooling earth-mixture of ammonium nitrate and water cools a sprained ankle. Describe the setup as a system in the A13 way: define the system boundary, the particles and energy flows across it, and the reactivity at play.
#### Answer
System: the cold pack with its ammonium nitrate and water (the boundary is the plastic bag). Particles: NH₄⁺ and NO₃⁻ ions splitting apart from their lattice into water (hydration), and the body/ankle outside the boundary. Energy: the dissolution is endothermic (the system absorbs heat from the surroundings — the ankle), so the ankle cools: energy crosses the boundary from surroundings into the system; the reactivity here is the breaking of the ionic lattice and the ion-dipole bonding to water (see C01), chosen because it is endothermic.
#### Solution
1. System/boundary first: inside = pack contents, outside = ankle+air.
2. What crosses the boundary: only energy (heat in); matter does not escape, so it is a closed system in terms of matter.
3. Reactivity = which bonds break/form and the resulting energy direction (endothermic dissolution) — A13 expects all three elements (particles/system/energy) connected.

### chem-a13-q02 | points 3 | outcome A13 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A campfire is an "open system." Give: the system boundary, what crosses it in matter and energy, and classify the reactivity and energy direction in one sentence.
#### Answer
Boundary: the immediate wood-air region where the reaction happens. Matter: oxygen enters (air flow), CO₂/water vapour/ash leave; energy: heat and light flow out. The reaction is strong exothermic oxidation of wood (C/H), and the released energy sustains the reaction further above ignition temperature — a self-sustaining system.
#### Solution
1. Open system = matter and energy both cross the boundary.
2. Inventory: fuel wood + O₂ in → ash + CO₂ + H₂O out, with the heat/light going to surroundings.
3. The released heat keeps the fresh fuel above ignition temperature (feedback in the system): reactivity + energy in one loop.

### chem-a13-q03 | points 4 | outcome A13 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Classify: (1) a pressure cooker as nearly-closed system; (2) a stirred beaker as open system. Describe how particle and energy exchange differ across each boundary and derive how each behaves differently for a hot reaction.
#### Answer
1. In a pressure cooker almost no matter crosses (heavy lid with a small relief valve): pressure builds up, boiling points of water exceed 100 °C (system property: higher T of the liquid), so food cooks faster; energy still crosses by the stove's heat. 2. In a beaker on a hot plate: matter (water vapour) and energy (heat) both freely cross the boundary, so temperature cannot exceed ≈ 100 °C (the plateau of boiling: added heat leaves as steam).
#### Solution
1. The boundary decides what the system can do: close it up and pressure raises the boiling point; open it and the boiling stays at 100 °C.
2. Energy crosses in both cases; matter only in the open one.
3. A13 shows the exact payoff of the systems view: same chemicals but different behaviour, explained by the boundary conditions.

### chem-a13-q04 | points 4 | outcome A13 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Your body is a textbook case of systems/change/energy. Explain with subsystems: where your chemical "fuel" goes in, where it is oxidised, where the energy becomes usable and where waste crosses the system boundary outward. Use nutrient transport, cells, respiration.
#### Answer
Fuel goes in at the digestive system (subsystem boundary 1): starch etc. hydrolysed to glucose, glucose absorbed through the cell walls of the small intestine into blood (transport system). Cells (tiny subsystems): glucose + oxygen are oxidised in respiration (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy); the released energy is stored as ATP — usable work for muscles/nerves. Waste crosses the organism's boundary at the lungs (CO₂ out), kidneys (urea in urine) and intestines (indigestible matter). Energy taken in = energy stored + energy spent on work + energy dissipated as body heat: total energy balance like any technical process.
#### Solution
1. Subsystems with their own boundaries: digestion, blood circulation, cells.
2. Reactivity centred in cells; thermodynamic release everywhere else.
3. Conservation of energy is the bookkeeping at every boundary — the A13 message in biological dress.

### chem-a13-q05 | points 4 | outcome A13 | difficulty hard
#### Question
A greenhouse grower burns methane in a boiler inside the greenhouse to heat it AND to raise the CO₂ level for faster plant growth. Analyse this system with input/output logic: which subsystems and boundaries exist, how do matter and energy flow between them, and identify the two failures this design can suffer (using conservation laws).
#### Answer
Subsystems: boiler (CH₄ + O₂), greenhouse air (warm, humid, CO₂-rich), plants (photosynthesis part of it). Flows: chemical energy enters at the boiler boundary as gas; heat and CO₂ pass the boiler-greenhouse boundary; light arrives across the glass; O₂ is taken up by plants and CO₂ taken in, biomass grows (subsystem store); water and gases leak/drift through vents. Failures: (1) incomplete combustion if ventilation is poor → CO in the greenhouse (toxic to people working there: conservation of elements with too little O); (2) energy balance: in warm weather the burner must idle while light still brings the heat, so the heat subsystem either dumps energy through vents (loss) or the plants overheat — boundary management is the design's hard point.
#### Solution
1. Define the subsystems: their products/services.
2. Track matter and energy across boundaries (boiler flue into air; air into leaves; heat through glass and vents).
3. Failures named via conservation laws and balance dynamics, not as impressions — A13's full systems/change/energy logic at work.
