# A12 — Reasoning in terms of Structure and Properties: notes

These are English explanations of every learning outcome in [README.md](README.md), and every topic is *drafted*: written from the CCVX and CvTE syllabi and standard VWO chemistry, not yet checked against an open source text or exam question (see [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) for the statuses).

Dutch terms are in brackets. A code such as *(CvTE M1.4)* points to the matching specification in the [CvTE 2027 syllabus](../../syllabus/cvte-syllabus-scheikunde-vwo-2027.pdf); Binas tables are named by subject, because their numbers differ between editions.

## Micro level

- The micro level (*microniveau*, microstructure) is the level of single particles: atoms, molecules and ions, and the bonds within and between them. Particles are about 10⁻¹⁰ m across, far too small to see. *(CvTE M6.1)*
- The end term also names a submicro level, but the concept list defines only micro, meso and macro, with atoms, molecules and ions at the micro level; use those three.
- Example: polyethene consists of long chain molecules of –CH₂– units, with atomic bonds (covalent bonds) inside each chain and Van der Waals bonds between the chains. A metal consists of positive metal ions held together by free electrons (the metallic bond, *metaalbinding*).
- When an exam question asks you to describe something at the micro level (*op microniveau beschrijven*), use particle terms: which particles, which bonds break or form, how the particles move or are arranged.
- **Trap:** when water boils, the hydrogen bridges between the molecules break, not the atomic bonds inside them: the particles stay H₂O.

## Meso level

- The meso level (*mesoniveau*, mesostructure) is the level of larger structures formed by many particles grouped together, between the single particles and the bulk material. Some properties can't be explained from the particles alone, because the way they are arranged matters too, for example fibres in polymers and crystal structures in metals. *(CvTE M6.1)*
- Polyethene: in high-density polyethene (HDPE) the chains are unbranched and can lie close together in ordered (crystalline) regions; in low-density polyethene (LDPE) the chains have many side branches, so they can't pack as closely and the material has fewer ordered regions and more disordered (amorphous) ones.
- Metals: the ions are stacked in regular layers in crystals (grains). In an alloy (*legering*) atoms of a different size disturb the layers.
- Thermoplastics and thermosets: in a thermoplastic the chains are separate; in a thermoset they are joined by cross-links (*crosslinks*) into one network.

## Macro level

- The macro level (*macroniveau*, macrostructure) is the level of substances and materials as you see and handle them, with their substance and material properties (*stof- en materiaaleigenschappen*): melting and boiling point, density, hardness, strength, deformability, conductivity, solubility, colour. *(CvTE M6.1)*
- Example: HDPE is stiffer, denser and softens at a higher temperature than LDPE, so HDPE is used for crates and bottles and LDPE for plastic bags and film.
- **Trap:** a single atom isn't red, hard or ductile; such properties belong to the substance, i.e. to very many particles together. Copper metal conducts and is red; one copper atom is neither.

## Reasoning between the levels

- The end term asks you to explain macro properties from the structure at micro and meso level, recognising aspects of scale (*aspecten van schaal*), and the other way round, to predict macro properties from a given structure. In exam answers, name the level you are talking about and make the link explicit: "because the chains … (meso), the material … (macro)". *(CvTE M6.1)*
- Polyethene: the unbranched HDPE chains pack more closely, so the Van der Waals bonds between them are stronger. That makes HDPE denser, stiffer and higher-melting than LDPE.
- Metals: free electrons can move through the metal, so metals conduct electricity and heat. The layers of ions can slide over each other while the free electrons keep holding them together, so metals can be hammered and bent. In an alloy the different atoms hinder that sliding, so an alloy is usually harder and less deformable than the pure metal.
- Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon. In diamond each carbon atom has four atomic bonds in a three-dimensional network, so diamond is very hard and doesn't conduct. In graphite each atom is bonded to three others in flat layers, and the remaining electrons can move within the layers: graphite conducts along the layers, and the layers slide over each other easily, which makes it soft.
- Thermoplastic versus thermoset: the separate chains of a thermoplastic can slide past each other once the Van der Waals bonds are weakened by heating, so it softens and can be moulded again. The cross-linked network of a thermoset can't do that, so it doesn't soften on heating and decomposes when heated strongly.
- Predicting from a structure: ethanol (46 u) is a liquid at room temperature while propane (44 u) is a gas, because the –OH group lets ethanol molecules form hydrogen bridges. So a substance with –OH or –NH groups is expected to have a higher boiling point, and to mix better with water, than one of similar mass without them.

## CCVX versus the Dutch syllabus

- **In both:** the end term and the three levels; the Dutch syllabus moved them, almost unchanged, from domain A to the sphere Matter as M6.1.
- **Dutch syllabus only:** the micro level in M6.1 also names (functional) groups and bonds, next to atoms, molecules and ions.
