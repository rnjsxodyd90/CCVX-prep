# C3 — Conservation laws and cycles: practice

Eight original questions on the C3 learning outcomes, in CCVX style. T = 298 K, p = p₀ unless stated (V_m = 24.5 L mol⁻¹).

### chem-c03-q01 | points 3 | outcome C3.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A lime kiln is fed 500 kg of limestone (CaCO₃) per hour and discharges 280 kg of quicklime (CaO) per hour, the rest leaving as carbon dioxide. (1) Check the mass balance: how many kg of CO₂ leave per hour? (2) Check it against the equation CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (M(CaCO₃) = 100.1, M(CO₂) = 44.01 g mol⁻¹).
#### Answer
1. 220 kg CO₂ per hour. 2. Consistent: per mole, 100.1 g CaCO₃ → 44.01 g CO₂, so from 500 kg/h: 500 × 44.01/100.1 = 220 kg/h — matching the mass balance (500 = 280 + 220).
#### Solution
1. Mass conservation: mass in = mass out (the kiln accumulates nothing): m(CO₂) = 500 − 280 = 220 kg h⁻¹.
2. Per mole of reaction: 100.1 g → 44.01 g CO₂. Scaling: 500 kg CaCO₃/h gives 500 × 44.01/100.1 = 220 kg CO₂/h. The two methods agree because both are the same conservation law.

### chem-c03-q02 | points 3 | outcome C3.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Check that the equation Fe₂O₃ + 3 CO → 2 Fe + 3 CO₂ satisfies the law of conservation of elements, and that Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s) satisfies both element and charge conservation.
#### Answer
Fe 2 = 2, O 3 + 3 = 6 = 3 × 2, C 3 = 3: balanced. Zn: Zn 1 = 1, Cu 1 = 1; charge left +2, right +2: balanced.
#### Solution
1. Element balance: Fe: left 2, right 2. O: left 3 (Fe₂O₃) + 3 (CO) = 6, right 3 × 2 = 6. C: left 3, right 3. Satisfied.
2. Zn and Cu balance directly (one each side). Charge: left side +2 (from Cu²⁺), right side +2 (from Zn²⁺): satisfied.
3. Every correct equation obeys these three balances (element, mass, charge); a coefficient that breaks one of them is wrong.

### chem-c03-q03 | points 2 | outcome C3.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A student proposes the equation Ag⁺ + Cu → Ag + Cu²⁺ for dipping copper in a silver nitrate solution. Use the charge balance to show that the equation as written cannot be right, and repair it.
#### Answer
Charge: left +1, right +2 — not balanced, so wrong. Correct: 2 Ag⁺ + Cu → 2 Ag + Cu²⁺ (charge: +2 = +2; Ag: 2 = 2; Cu: 1 = 1).
#### Solution
1. Left side: the total charge is +1 (one Ag⁺); right side +2 (one Cu²⁺). Charge is conserved in every reaction, so a balanced equation must have equal charge on both sides.
2. Repair by doubling the silver: 2 Ag⁺ + Cu → 2 Ag + Cu²⁺.
3. Check: charge +2 = +2; element balance: Ag 2 = 2, Cu 1 = 1. (This one-electron/two-electron mismatch also shows up as unequal electrons in the half-reactions: Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag and Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2 e⁻, see C01.)

### chem-c03-q04 | points 3 | outcome C3.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Show how photosynthesis and the respiration of living things together form an element cycle, by writing both equations and indicating which substances are exchanged.
#### Answer
Photosynthesis: 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂; respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O. The products of the one are the reactants of the other: the carbon and oxygen atoms cycle between CO₂/H₂O and glucose/O₂.
#### Solution
1. Green plants fix CO₂ with water into glucose and give off O₂ (light energy stored as chemical energy).
2. Plants, animals and microbes use glucose and O₂, releasing CO₂ and water (chemical energy released).
3. Written back to back, the two equations are each other's reverse: nothing of the elements C, H, O is created or destroyed — they run around a cycle, kept going by sunlight, while energy itself is not cycled (it degrades to heat, see C09).

### chem-c03-q05 | points 3 | outcome C3.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Burning wood from a sustainably managed forest and burning natural gas both release CO₂. Argue, with the element cycle, why the first is called climate-neutral (within the cycle) and the second is not.
#### Answer
The CO₂ from burning wood was recently taken from the atmosphere by the trees and will be taken up again by regrowth: the carbon cycles on a short time scale. The CO₂ from natural gas comes from carbon that was buried for millions of years: it adds fossil carbon to the atmosphere without a matching uptake, so the atmospheric carbon stock grows.
#### Solution
1. Wood carbon: fixed from atmospheric CO₂ by photosynthesis years–decades ago; burning returns the same CO₂; replanting closes the cycle: no net addition (ignoring fuel for harvesting and transport).
2. Natural gas carbon: fixed by ancient organisms and locked away as fossil fuel for 10⁷–10⁸ years, effectively outside the fast cycle. Burning injects this old carbon into the atmosphere where no equally fast sink exists.
3. The difference is not the molecule (CO₂ is CO₂) but the time scale of the cycle you are part of: fast-cycled versus one-way extraction — the heart of the climate argument about fossil fuels.

### chem-c03-q06 | points 3 | outcome C3.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
PET soft-drink bottles are collected and processed into fleece sweaters. (1) Is this recycling in the sense of a closed materials cycle? (2) Why is it often called "downcycling"? (3) What would cradle-to-cradle design of the bottle aim for?
#### Answer
1. It is recycling of material, but not closed: the fibre is not turned back into bottles. 2. The material loses quality at every round (chain shortening and contamination lower the polymer value), so it ends up in lower-grade products until it is finally burned or landfilled. 3. Cradle-to-cradle aims to keep materials at the same quality in closed loops: e.g. bottles that are depolymerised to monomer and repolymerised into new bottles, with collection designed in.
#### Solution
1. The substance cycle is open: bottle → fibre is one direction; the sweater does not become a bottle.
2. During melting and reprocessing, polymer chains shorten and impurities accumulate, so each generation of material is poorer: "downcycling", with final disposal at the end.
3. Cradle-to-cradle designs products so that after use they are source material for the same product again (technical cycle) or return safely to the environment (biological cycle): the cycle is planned at the design table, not patched at the waste heap.

### chem-c03-q07 | points 4 | outcome C3.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
A power plant scrubs its flue gas: 1000 m³ of flue gas per hour contains 0.30 vol% SO₂ (measured at 298 K, p₀), all of which is absorbed. (1) Calculate how many kg of SO₂ the plant absorbs per hour. (2) The SO₂ is oxidised and neutralised with limestone: 2 SO₂ + 2 CaCO₃ + O₂ → 2 CaSO₄ + 2 CO₂. Calculate the mass of CaSO₄ formed per hour (M(CaSO₄) = 136.1 g mol⁻¹).
#### Answer
1. 7.8 kg SO₂ per hour; 2. 17 kg CaSO₄ per hour.
#### Solution
1. SO₂ volume: 0.30% of 1000 m³ = 3.0 m³ = 3.0·10³ L. n = 3.0·10³ / 24.5 = 122 mol h⁻¹. M(SO₂) = 32.07 + 2 × 16.00 = 64.07 g mol⁻¹. m = 122 × 64.07 = 7.8·10³ g h⁻¹ = 7.8 kg h⁻¹ (two significant figures, as 0.30% has two).
2. The mole ratio SO₂ : CaSO₄ is 2 : 2 = 1 : 1, so n(CaSO₄) = 122 mol h⁻¹; m = 122 × 136.1 = 1.7·10⁴ g h⁻¹ = 17 kg h⁻¹.
3. This is an element balance applied to a plant: every sulfur atom entering with the flue gas leaves in the gypsum — nothing lost, only moved.

### chem-c03-q08 | points 4 | outcome C3.1–C3.2 | difficulty hard
#### Question
"On a spaceship to Mars, matter must run in cycles, but energy never can." (1) Explain what this statement means for a spaceship's life-support system. (2) Why can matter run in cycles at all — which conservation law makes it possible? (3) Energy is conserved too, so why is an energy cycle impossible?
#### Answer
1. Water, air and food wastes must be regenerated on board (for example CO₂ scrubbed and plants regrown from waste), because no new matter arrives. 2. The law of conservation of mass/elements: atoms are neither created nor destroyed in chemical processes, so given energy they can be rearranged indefinitely. 3. Every use of energy degrades it into less useful heat at the ship's temperature (energy quality, C09); the total stays constant, but the useful part is spent and must come from outside (sunlight, a reactor). So matter can cycle while energy must flow through.
#### Solution
1. Closed cabin: the crew's CO₂ would accumulate and O₂ would deplete unless water, oxygen and food are regenerated — mass cycles are life support.
2. Mass conservation: each atom that enters a process leaves it (in some compound), so purification/reprocessing can always, in principle, undo the mixing.
3. Energy is also conserved, but its quality drops at every conversion (chemical → motion → heat), until only low-temperature heat remains; that is why a perpetuum mobile (and an "energy cycle") is impossible even though the first law holds. The ship needs a continuous energy source and must radiate the degraded heat away.
