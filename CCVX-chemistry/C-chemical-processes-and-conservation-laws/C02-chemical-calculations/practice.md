# C2 — Chemical calculations: practice

Fourteen original questions on the C2 learning outcomes, in CCVX style. Assume T = 298 K and p = p₀ unless a question says otherwise; then V_m = 24.5 L mol⁻¹. Use Binas for molar masses. Give units in every answer.

### chem-c02-q01 | points 3 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A vitamin tablet contains 500 mg of ascorbic acid (C₆H₈O₆). Calculate the amount, in mol, of ascorbic acid in one tablet.
#### Answer
2.84·10⁻³ mol
#### Solution
1. M(C₆H₈O₆) = 6 × 12.01 + 8 × 1.008 + 6 × 16.00 = 176.12 g mol⁻¹ (Binas, table of molar masses or atomic masses).
2. Convert the mass: 500 mg = 0.500 g.
3. n = m / M = 0.500 / 176.12 = 2.84·10⁻³ mol (three significant figures, as in 0.500 g).

### chem-c02-q02 | points 3 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
A lab technician dissolves 6.24 g of sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄) in water and makes the volume up to 250.0 mL. Calculate the molarity of the Na⁺ ions in this solution.
#### Answer
0.351 mol L⁻¹
#### Solution
1. M(Na₂SO₄) = 2 × 22.99 + 32.07 + 4 × 16.00 = 142.04 g mol⁻¹.
2. n(Na₂SO₄) = 6.24 / 142.04 = 4.39·10⁻² mol in 250.0 mL = 0.2500 L.
3. c(Na₂SO₄) = 4.39·10⁻² / 0.2500 = 0.176 mol L⁻¹.
4. Each Na₂SO₄ gives two Na⁺ ions: [Na⁺] = 2 × 0.176 = 0.351 mol L⁻¹ (three significant figures).

### chem-c02-q03 | points 3 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Calculate the pH of a 0.0250 M solution of nitric acid (HNO₃) at 298 K.
#### Answer
pH = 1.602
#### Solution
1. Nitric acid is a strong acid (Binas, table 49): it ionises completely, so [H₃O⁺] = 0.0250 mol L⁻¹.
2. pH = −log [H₃O⁺] = −log (0.0250) = 1.602.
3. [H₃O⁺] has three significant figures, so the pH gets three decimals.

### chem-c02-q04 | points 4 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
1.28 g of barium hydroxide octahydrate (Ba(OH)₂·8H₂O) is dissolved in water and made up to 400.0 mL at 298 K. Calculate the pH of this solution. Assume complete dissociation; Ba(OH)₂ is a strong base.
#### Answer
pH = 12.31
#### Solution
1. M(Ba(OH)₂·8H₂O) = 137.33 + 2 × (16.00 + 1.008) + 8 × 18.016 = 315.5 g mol⁻¹ (adding the Binas table-99 atomic masses).
2. n = m / M = 1.28 / 315.5 = 4.06·10⁻³ mol Ba(OH)₂.
3. Each unit gives two OH⁻ ions: n(OH⁻) = 8.11·10⁻³ mol in 0.4000 L, so [OH⁻] = 2.03·10⁻² mol L⁻¹.
4. pOH = −log (2.03·10⁻²) = 1.693; pH = 14.00 − 1.693 = 12.31.

### chem-c02-q05 | points 4 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
A blast furnace extracts iron from haematite: 2 Fe₂O₃ + 3 C → 4 Fe + 3 CO₂. A batch of ore has a mass of 1.00 tonne (1.00·10⁶ g) and consists of 92.0% Fe₂O₃ by mass. Carbon is present in excess. Calculate the maximum mass of iron that can form from this batch.
#### Answer
6.43·10⁵ g (643 kg)
#### Solution
1. Mass of Fe₂O₃: 0.920 × 1.00·10⁶ = 9.20·10⁵ g.
2. M(Fe₂O₃) = 2 × 55.85 + 3 × 16.00 = 159.7 g mol⁻¹, so n(Fe₂O₃) = 9.20·10⁵ / 159.7 = 5.76·10³ mol.
3. Mole ratio Fe₂O₃ : Fe = 2 : 4 = 1 : 2, so n(Fe) = 2 × 5.76·10³ = 1.15·10⁴ mol.
4. m(Fe) = n × M = 1.15·10⁴ × 55.85 = 6.43·10⁵ g = 643 kg (three significant figures, as in 92.0%).

### chem-c02-q06 | points 4 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Butane burns completely: 2 C₄H₁₀ + 13 O₂ → 8 CO₂ + 10 H₂O(l). Calculate the volume of oxygen (in L) needed for the complete combustion of 5.00 L of butane, and the volume of carbon dioxide formed. All gas volumes are measured at 298 K and p = p₀.
#### Answer
32.5 L O₂; 20.0 L CO₂
#### Solution
1. At the same T and p, gas volumes stand in the same ratio as the mole numbers (Avogadro's law), so V-ratios equal the coefficient ratios.
2. V(O₂) = (13/2) × 5.00 = 32.5 L.
3. V(CO₂) = (8/2) × 5.00 = 20.0 L.
4. No V_m is needed because the volume ratio is used directly (water is liquid, so its volume is not asked).

### chem-c02-q07 | points 5 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Aluminium and chlorine react: 2 Al + 3 Cl₂ → 2 AlCl₃. A chemist lets 5.40 g of aluminium react with 20.5 g of chlorine. Show which reactant is in deficiency, and calculate the mass of aluminium chloride that forms.
#### Answer
Cl₂ is in deficiency; 25.7 g AlCl₃
#### Solution
1. n(Al) = 5.40 / 26.98 = 0.200 mol; n(Cl₂) = 20.5 / 70.90 = 0.289 mol.
2. For 0.200 mol Al, the equation needs (3/2) × 0.200 = 0.300 mol Cl₂. Only 0.289 mol is available, so Cl₂ is in deficiency and decides the product amount.
3. Mole ratio Cl₂ : AlCl₃ = 3 : 2, so n(AlCl₃) = (2/3) × 0.289 = 0.193 mol.
4. M(AlCl₃) = 26.98 + 3 × 35.45 = 133.33 g mol⁻¹; m = 0.193 × 133.33 = 25.7 g (three significant figures).

### chem-c02-q08 | points 4 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
A 25.00 mL sample of a sodium hydroxide solution is titrated with 0.0985 M hydrochloric acid. The equivalence point occurs at 22.65 mL of acid. Calculate the molarity of the sodium hydroxide solution.
#### Answer
0.0892 mol L⁻¹
#### Solution
1. Reaction: OH⁻ + H₃O⁺ → 2 H₂O, so at the equivalence point n(OH⁻) = n(H₃O⁺).
2. n(HCl) = 0.0985 mol L⁻¹ × 22.65·10⁻³ L = 2.231·10⁻³ mol.
3. c(NaOH) = 2.231·10⁻³ / 25.00·10⁻³ = 8.92·10⁻² = 0.0892 mol L⁻¹ (three significant figures, as in 0.0985 and 22.65).

### chem-c02-q09 | points 6 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty hard
#### Question
An antacid tablet contains calcium carbonate. A tablet weighing 0.652 g is dissolved in 50.00 mL of 0.200 M hydrochloric acid (an excess). The excess acid is then titrated with 0.100 M sodium hydroxide, which takes 18.30 mL. The reaction in the tablet is CaCO₃ + 2 H₃O⁺ → Ca²⁺ + 3 H₂O + CO₂. Calculate the mass percentage of CaCO₃ in the tablet.
#### Answer
62.7%
#### Solution
1. Added acid: n(H₃O⁺) = 0.200 × 50.00·10⁻³ = 1.00·10⁻² mol.
2. Excess acid back-titrated: n(NaOH) = 0.100 × 18.30·10⁻³ = 1.830·10⁻³ mol, so n(H₃O⁺) left over = 1.830·10⁻³ mol.
3. Reacted with CaCO₃: 1.00·10⁻² − 1.830·10⁻³ = 8.17·10⁻³ mol H₃O⁺.
4. Mole ratio CaCO₃ : H₃O⁺ = 1 : 2, so n(CaCO₃) = 4.09·10⁻³ mol.
5. M(CaCO₃) = 40.08 + 12.01 + 3 × 16.00 = 100.09 g mol⁻¹; m = 4.09·10⁻³ × 100.09 = 0.409 g.
6. Mass percentage: 0.409 / 0.652 × 100% = 62.7% (three significant figures).

### chem-c02-q10 | points 4 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) is made from salicylic acid and ethanoic anhydride: C₇H₆O₃ + C₄H₆O₃ → C₉H₈O₄ + C₂H₄O₂. A student starts with 4.10 g of salicylic acid and an excess of ethanoic anhydride, and isolates 4.61 g of aspirin after purification. Calculate the percentage yield.
#### Answer
86.2%
#### Solution
1. M(C₇H₆O₃) = 7 × 12.01 + 6 × 1.008 + 3 × 16.00 = 138.12 g mol⁻¹; M(C₉H₈O₄) = 180.15 g mol⁻¹.
2. n(salicylic acid) = 4.10 / 138.12 = 2.97·10⁻² mol; the ratio is 1 : 1, so the theoretical amount of aspirin is the same.
3. Theoretical mass: 2.97·10⁻² × 180.15 = 5.35 g.
4. Yield = 4.61 / 5.35 × 100% = 86.2% (three significant figures).

### chem-c02-q11 | points 4 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
The guideline value for lead in drinking water is 10 μg per litre. Analysis of a 250.0 mL sample of tap water finds 3.5 μg of lead; the density of the sample is 1.00 kg L⁻¹. Express the measured lead concentration in mass-ppb (μg kg⁻¹) and in mass-ppm (mg kg⁻¹), and state whether the sample exceeds the guideline.
#### Answer
14 mass-ppb = 1.4·10⁻² mass-ppm; yes, it exceeds the guideline of 10 mass-ppb.
#### Solution
1. 250.0 mL of water has a mass of 0.2500 kg (density 1.00 kg L⁻¹).
2. Concentration = 3.5 μg / 0.2500 kg = 14 μg kg⁻¹ = 14 mass-ppb.
3. In mg kg⁻¹: 14 μg kg⁻¹ = 1.4·10⁻² mg kg⁻¹ = 1.4·10⁻² mass-ppm.
4. 10 μg L⁻¹ corresponds to 10 μg kg⁻¹ = 10 mass-ppb, so 14 mass-ppb exceeds the guideline (two significant figures, as in 3.5 μg).

### chem-c02-q12 | points 4 | outcome C2.1 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Concentrated hydrochloric acid contains 37 mass% HCl and has a density of 1.19 g mL⁻¹. Calculate the molarity of concentrated hydrochloric acid.
#### Answer
12 mol L⁻¹
#### Solution
1. Take 1.00 L = 1000 mL of the acid; its mass is 1000 × 1.19 = 1.19·10³ g.
2. Of this, 37% is HCl: m(HCl) = 0.37 × 1.19·10³ = 4.4·10² g.
3. M(HCl) = 36.46 g mol⁻¹, so n(HCl) = 4.4·10² / 36.46 = 12 mol.
4. This is per litre, so the molarity is 12 mol L⁻¹ (two significant figures, as in 37%).

### chem-c02-q13 | points 3 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Hydrogen peroxide decomposes: 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂. Calculate the volume of oxygen gas formed when 15.0 g of hydrogen peroxide decomposes completely, at 298 K and p = p₀.
#### Answer
5.40 L
#### Solution
1. M(H₂O₂) = 2 × 1.008 + 2 × 16.00 = 34.02 g mol⁻¹; n = 15.0 / 34.02 = 0.441 mol.
2. Mole ratio H₂O₂ : O₂ = 2 : 1, so n(O₂) = 0.441 / 2 = 0.220 mol.
3. V = n × V_m = 0.220 × 24.5 = 5.40 L (three significant figures).

### chem-c02-q14 | points 6 | outcome C2.2 | difficulty hard
#### Question
A 1.000 g mixture of sodium chloride and potassium chloride is dissolved in water, and an excess of silver nitrate solution is added. All chloride precipitates as AgCl(s). The washed and dried precipitate weighs 2.147 g. Calculate the mass percentage of NaCl in the mixture.
#### Answer
42.4%
#### Solution
1. M(AgCl) = 107.87 + 35.45 = 143.32 g mol⁻¹, so n(AgCl) = 2.147 / 143.32 = 1.498·10⁻² mol.
2. Let x = mass of NaCl in g; then the mass of KCl is (1.000 − x) g. Each mole of NaCl or KCl gives one mole of AgCl.
3. n(AgCl) = x / 58.44 + (1.000 − x) / 74.55 = 1.498·10⁻² mol (M(NaCl) = 58.44, M(KCl) = 74.55 g mol⁻¹).
4. Solving this linear equation: x × (1/58.44 − 1/74.55) = 1.498·10⁻² − 1.000/74.55, so x = (1.498·10⁻² − 1.341·10⁻²) / (1.711·10⁻² − 1.341·10⁻²) = 0.424 g.
5. Mass percentage: 0.424 / 1.000 × 100% = 42.4% (three significant figures).
