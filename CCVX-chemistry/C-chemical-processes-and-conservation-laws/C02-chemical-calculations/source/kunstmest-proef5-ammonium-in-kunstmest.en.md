**Experiment 5: Determining the mass percentage of ammonium in fertiliser**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Theory:**

To determine the amount of ammonium ions in fertiliser, you make the ammonium ions in a precisely weighed amount of fertiliser react with a precisely known excess of sodium hydroxide solution. Excess means that you add more than enough to react with all the ammonium from the fertiliser.

The fertiliser is first dissolved in water. Then, when the excess of sodium hydroxide solution is added, the following reaction takes place:

NH₄⁺ (aq) + OH⁻ (aq) → NH₃(g) + H₂O (l)

Because an excess of hydroxide ions is added, hydroxide ions are left over. You will determine how many hydroxide ions are left over by a reaction with hydrogen chloride solution.

During the reaction the remaining hydroxide ions react with the hydrogen chloride solution. The indicator bromothymol blue is also added.\
It turns blue when hydroxide ions are present and green when all the hydroxide ions have reacted with the hydrogen chloride solution.\
The reaction goes as follows:

H⁺ (aq) + Cl⁻ (aq) + OH⁻ (aq) → H₂O (l) + Cl⁻ (aq)

By knowing exactly how much hydrogen chloride solution you added, you can determine how many hydroxide ions reacted in the second reaction. Because you know how many grams of OH- you added at the start, you can calculate how much then reacted in the first reaction. You then also know how many grams of NH4 were present.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Materials:**

- Fertiliser (solid Pokon)
- Three 250 mL Erlenmeyer flasks
- Two 100 mL measuring cylinders
- Two 250 mL beakers
- Distilled water
- 1.70 g/L lye *[a hydroxide solution]*
- Burner
- Tripod
- Gauze
- Red litmus paper
- 3.65 g/L hydrochloric acid
- Bromothymol blue
- Three 10.0 mL syringes
- One 20.0 mL syringe
- Two 100 mL beakers
- Stirring rod

------------------------------------------------------------------------

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Method:**

1.  Weigh out about 0.25 gram of fertiliser. (Do write down the exact amount.)
2.  Put the fertiliser in a 250 mL beaker and add 100 mL of distilled water from the measuring cylinder. Dissolve the fertiliser by stirring with the stirring rod.
3.  Use a syringe to put 25.0 mL of the fertiliser solution into a 250 mL Erlenmeyer flask.
4.  Then use a syringe to add 25.0 mL of 1.70 g/L hydroxide ions (excess).
5.  Boil the ammonia gas, formed in the reaction between the fertiliser and the hydroxide solution, out of the solution. When it boils, hold a moist piece of red litmus paper in the vapour. If the litmus paper turns blue, ammonia gas is still escaping and you must keep heating. After a minute, try with a new piece of red litmus paper whether it changes colour. Repeat until the red litmus stays red. Then you can stop heating.
6.  Let the solution cool.
7.  Add three drops of bromothymol blue to the Erlenmeyer flask with the boiled liquid. The solution will now turn blue.
8.  Read the starting value of your syringe.
9.  Then put a white sheet of paper under your Erlenmeyer flask and carefully drip hydrochloric acid into the flask with a 10.0 mL syringe. Keep doing this until the colour of the solution turns green. Then read the final value of the syringe. If the colour turns yellow, you have added too much hydrochloric acid.
10. Now do a duplicate determination (a second determination to check whether the first one was done correctly. If there is only a small difference between the results of the two determinations, the experiment was done correctly). Do this from the point where you put 25.0 mL of fertiliser solution into a 250 mL Erlenmeyer flask.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Questions**

a\. Using the volume of hydrogen chloride solution you added, calculate how many mg of hydrogen chloride you used.

b\. Calculate how many mg of OH⁻ reacted with the number of mg of hydrogen chloride from question A.

c\. Calculate how many mg of OH⁻ reacted with NH₄.

d\. Calculate how many mg of NH₄ was present in 25 mL of the diluted solution.

e\. Calculate how many mg of NH₄ was then present in 100 mL of diluted solution.

f\. Calculate the mass percentage of NH₄ in the fertiliser tested.
