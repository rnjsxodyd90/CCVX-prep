# C5 — Chemical equilibrium: practice

Twelve original questions on the C5 learning outcomes, in CCVX style. Assume T = 298 K unless a question says otherwise. Use Binas for acid and base constants and for K_w.

### chem-c05-q01 | points 2 | outcome C5.1 | difficulty easy
#### Question
For each of the following reactions, state whether it is best described as irreversible or reversible, and give your reason.
1. The combustion of propane in an open flame.
2. The reaction of ethanoic acid with ethanol, giving an ester and water.
#### Answer
1. Irreversible: it runs to completion (the products do not react back). 2. Reversible: esterification is an equilibrium reaction (the ester also hydrolyses back).
#### Solution
1. Combustion reactions go in one direction only; CO₂ and H₂O do not spontaneously reform propane and oxygen, and in an open system the gases escape anyway. A single arrow is used.
2. An ester and water react back to the acid and the alcohol (hydrolysis), so in a closed mixture both reactions keep running: a reversible reaction, written with ⇌.

### chem-c05-q02 | points 4 | outcome C5.2 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Give the condition of equilibrium (the expression for the equilibrium constant K) for each of the following equilibria.
1. 2 SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2 SO₃(g)
2. NH₄Cl(s) ⇌ NH₃(g) + HCl(g)
3. HF(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ F⁻(aq) + H₃O⁺(aq)
#### Answer
1. K = [SO₃]² / ([SO₂]²·[O₂]); 2. K = [NH₃]·[HCl]; 3. K = [F⁻]·[H₃O⁺] / [HF]
#### Solution
1. Products in the numerator, reactants in the denominator, each concentration raised to the power of its coefficient: K = [SO₃]² / ([SO₂]² × [O₂]).
2. NH₄Cl is a solid, so it is left out of the expression: K = [NH₃] × [HCl].
3. Water is the solvent here, so it is left out: K = [F⁻][H₃O⁺] / [HF].

### chem-c05-q03 | points 4 | outcome C5.2 | difficulty medium
#### Question
A 4.00 L vessel is filled with 1.000 mol of N₂ and 3.000 mol of H₂ and kept at a fixed temperature. At equilibrium, 0.400 mol of NH₃ is present. The equilibrium is N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) ⇌ 2 NH₃(g). Calculate the equilibrium constant K (with its unit) under these conditions.
#### Answer
K = 0.231 L² mol⁻²
#### Solution
1. Changes follow the coefficients: 0.400 mol NH₃ formed means 0.200 mol N₂ and 0.600 mol H₂ reacted.
2. Equilibrium amounts: n(N₂) = 0.800 mol, n(H₂) = 2.400 mol, n(NH₃) = 0.400 mol.
3. Concentrations (divide by 4.00 L): [N₂] = 0.200, [H₂] = 0.600, [NH₃] = 0.100 mol L⁻¹.
4. K = [NH₃]² / ([N₂][H₂]³) = (0.100)² / (0.200 × (0.600)³) = 1.00·10⁻² / 4.32·10⁻² = 0.231; the unit is (mol L⁻¹)²/(mol L⁻¹)⁴ = L² mol⁻² (three significant figures).

### chem-c05-q04 | points 4 | outcome C5.3 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Ethanoic acid and ethanol react in a closed flask: CH₃COOH(l) + C₂H₅OH(l) ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅(l) + H₂O(l). At a certain temperature K = 4.0. A technician mixes the substances and analyses the mixture: [CH₃COOH] = 0.20, [C₂H₅OH] = 0.30, [CH₃COOC₂H₅] = 0.50 and [H₂O] = 0.40 mol L⁻¹. Show by calculating the reaction quotient whether this mixture is at equilibrium, and if not, state in which direction it will react.
#### Answer
Q = 3.3 < K = 4.0: not at equilibrium; the reaction goes to the right (more ester and water form).
#### Solution
1. Water is not the solvent in this liquid mixture of comparable amounts, so it is part of the expression: Q = [CH₃COOC₂H₅]·[H₂O] / ([CH₃COOH]·[C₂H₅OH]).
2. Q = (0.50 × 0.40) / (0.20 × 0.30) = 0.20 / 0.060 = 3.3.
3. Q (3.3) < K (4.0): not at equilibrium.
4. To raise the quotient to the value of K, the numerator must grow and the denominator shrink: the reaction proceeds to the right, making ester and water.

### chem-c05-q05 | points 4 | outcome C5.3 | difficulty medium
#### Question
A test tube holds the equilibrium [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺(aq) + 4 Cl⁻(aq) ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻(aq) + 6 H₂O(l). The pink solution turns blue when the equilibrium is on the right ([CoCl₄]²⁻) and pink when it is on the left.
1. Explain, using the reaction quotient Q, what colour appears when solid sodium chloride is dissolved in the tube.
2. Explain, in the same way, what happens when the solution is diluted with a large amount of water.
#### Answer
1. Blue: Q falls below K, so the equilibrium shifts right. 2. Pink: Q rises above K, so it shifts left.
#### Solution
1. Q = [[CoCl₄]²⁻] / ([[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺]·[Cl⁻]⁴). Dissolving NaCl raises [Cl⁻], so Q becomes smaller than K. The system reacts in the direction that raises Q again: to the right, forming blue [CoCl₄]²⁻.
2. Dilution divides all dissolved concentrations by the same factor d < 1. Because the denominator of Q holds five particles and the numerator one, Q is multiplied by d / d⁵ = 1/d⁴ > 1, so Q becomes larger than K. The system reacts to the left, back to pink [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺. (Water is left out of Q as the solvent.)

### chem-c05-q06 | points 3 | outcome C5.4 | difficulty easy
#### Question
In the Haber process, nitrogen and hydrogen react over an iron catalyst: N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) ⇌ 2 NH₃(g). State the effect of the iron catalyst on:
1. the rate of the forward and the reverse reaction;
2. the time needed to reach equilibrium;
3. the position of the equilibrium.
#### Answer
1. Both reactions become faster. 2. Shorter. 3. No change.
#### Solution
1. A catalyst lowers the activation energy of the forward and the reverse reaction by the same amount, so both become faster.
2. Because both rates are higher, the state where forward and reverse rates are equal is reached sooner.
3. The catalyst neither changes K (which depends on temperature only) nor appears in Q, so the equilibrium composition is unchanged; it is only reached faster.

### chem-c05-q07 | points 4 | outcome C5.5 | difficulty easy
#### Question
Calculate, at 298 K:
1. the pH of a 0.030 M solution of hydrobromic acid (HBr, a strong acid);
2. the pH of a 5.0·10⁻³ M solution of barium hydroxide (Ba(OH)₂, a strong base).
#### Answer
1. pH = 1.52; 2. pH = 12.00
#### Solution
1. HBr is strong, so [H₃O⁺] = 0.030 mol L⁻¹. pH = −log (0.030) = 1.52 (two decimals, as 0.030 has two significant figures).
2. Ba(OH)₂ → Ba²⁺ + 2 OH⁻, so [OH⁻] = 2 × 5.0·10⁻³ = 1.0·10⁻² mol L⁻¹. pOH = −log (1.0·10⁻²) = 2.00, and pH = 14.00 − 2.00 = 12.00.

### chem-c05-q08 | points 5 | outcome C5.5 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Calculate the pH of a 0.10 M solution of propanoic acid (C₂H₅COOH) at 298 K; K_z = 1.3·10⁻⁵ (Binas, table of acid and base constants). Use the approximation, and show afterwards that it is allowed.
#### Answer
pH = 2.94
#### Solution
1. C₂H₅COOH + H₂O ⇌ C₂H₅COO⁻ + H₃O⁺. Let x = [H₃O⁺] = [C₂H₅COO⁻]; then [C₂H₅COOH] = 0.10 − x.
2. K_z = x² / (0.10 − x). Approximation: x ≪ 0.10, so x = √(K_z × c) = √(1.3·10⁻⁵ × 0.10) = 1.14·10⁻³ mol L⁻¹.
3. pH = −log (1.14·10⁻³) = 2.94.
4. Check: x / c = 1.14·10⁻³ / 0.10 ≈ 1%, well under 5%, so the approximation is allowed. (The exact quadratic route gives the same x.)
5. Two significant figures in the data give two decimals in the pH.

### chem-c05-q09 | points 5 | outcome C5.5 | difficulty medium
#### Question
Calculate the pH of a 0.030 M ammonia solution (NH₃(aq)) at 298 K; K_b = 1.8·10⁻⁵ (Binas, table of acid and base constants).
#### Answer
pH = 10.87
#### Solution
1. NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻. Let x = [OH⁻] = [NH₄⁺]; then [NH₃] = 0.030 − x.
2. K_b = x² / (0.030 − x). Approximation: x = √(1.8·10⁻⁵ × 0.030) = 7.35·10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹. Check: x / c = 7.35·10⁻⁴ / 0.030 ≈ 2.4%, so the approximation is allowed.
3. pOH = −log (7.35·10⁻⁴) = 3.13.
4. pH = 14.00 − pOH = 14.00 − 3.13 = 10.87.

### chem-c05-q10 | points 5 | outcome C5.5 | difficulty hard
#### Question
A solution of ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH) has a pH of 3.15 at 298 K; K_z = 1.8·10⁻⁵. Calculate the molarity of the ethanoic acid solution.
#### Answer
0.029 mol L⁻¹
#### Solution
1. x = [H₃O⁺] = 10^(−pH) = 10^(−3.15) = 7.08·10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹; also [CH₃COO⁻] = x.
2. K_z = x² / (c − x), so c = x + x² / K_z.
3. c = 7.08·10⁻⁴ + (7.08·10⁻⁴)² / 1.8·10⁻⁵ = 7.08·10⁻⁴ + 2.79·10⁻² = 2.9·10⁻² mol L⁻¹.
4. The concentration is much larger than [H₃O⁺], as expected for a weak acid (c = 10^(−pH) would only hold for a strong acid). Two significant figures: 0.029 mol L⁻¹.

### chem-c05-q11 | points 5 | outcome C5.2 | difficulty hard
#### Question
Phosphorus pentachloride decomposes: PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g). At a certain temperature K = 0.80 mol L⁻¹. A vessel is filled with PCl₅ vapour at a concentration of 0.100 mol L⁻¹ and brought to that temperature. Calculate the concentrations of all three substances at equilibrium.
#### Answer
[PCl₅] = 0.010 mol L⁻¹; [PCl₃] = [Cl₂] = 0.090 mol L⁻¹
#### Solution
1. Let x mol L⁻¹ of PCl₅ react: at equilibrium [PCl₅] = 0.100 − x and [PCl₃] = [Cl₂] = x.
2. K = [PCl₃][Cl₂] / [PCl₅] = x² / (0.100 − x) = 0.80, so x² + 0.80x − 0.080 = 0.
3. Quadratic formula: x = (−0.80 + √(0.80² + 4 × 0.080)) / 2 = (−0.80 + 0.980) / 2 = 0.090 mol L⁻¹. The other root is negative and has no chemical meaning.
4. So [PCl₅] = 0.100 − 0.090 = 0.010 mol L⁻¹ and [PCl₃] = [Cl₂] = 0.090 mol L⁻¹. Check: 0.090² / 0.010 = 0.81 ≈ 0.80 (the small difference comes from rounding x to two significant figures, like K).

### chem-c05-q12 | points 5 | outcome C5.2 | difficulty hard
#### Question
The solubility product of silver chloride at 298 K follows from the equilibrium AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq). The solubility of AgCl in pure water at this temperature is 1.3·10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹.
1. Calculate the solubility product K_s of silver chloride.
2. Calculate the solubility of AgCl in a 0.010 M sodium chloride solution, and compare it with the solubility in pure water.
#### Answer
1. K_s = 1.7·10⁻¹⁰ mol² L⁻²; 2. 1.7·10⁻⁸ mol L⁻¹, about 800 times lower than in pure water
#### Solution
1. In saturated pure water [Ag⁺] = [Cl⁻] = 1.3·10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹. The solid is left out of the expression: K_s = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻] = (1.3·10⁻⁵)² = 1.7·10⁻¹⁰ mol² L⁻².
2. In 0.010 M NaCl, [Cl⁻] ≈ 0.010 mol L⁻¹ (the extra Cl⁻ from dissolved AgCl is negligible). Let s be the solubility: s × 0.010 = 1.7·10⁻¹⁰ gives s = 1.7·10⁻⁸ mol L⁻¹.
3. The added Cl⁻ (a common ion, the same effect as changing the reaction quotient in C5.3) shifts the equilibrium to the left, so the solubility drops by a factor 1.3·10⁻⁵ / 1.7·10⁻⁸ ≈ 7.6·10², about 800 times lower.
