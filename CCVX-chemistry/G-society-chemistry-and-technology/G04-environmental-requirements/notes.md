## Green chemistry and natural resources

- Green chemistry (*groene chemie*) designs processes that prevent waste rather than clean it up afterwards: a high atom economy, catalysts instead of reagents that are used up, safer substances and solvents, renewable raw materials, and mild conditions that save energy. The calculations (atom economy, E-factor) are in [F02](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F02-green-chemistry/notes.md). *(SLO G4; CvTE T2.1, T2.2)*
- Natural resources (*natuurlijke hulpbronnen*) are what a process takes from nature: fossil fuels, ores and minerals, water, air, biomass and land. Finite resources such as oil, gas and ores run out; renewable ones such as wind, sunlight and biomass are replenished, biomass only if it isn't harvested faster than it grows back. *(SLO G4; CvTE T2.1, T6.4)*
- **Trap:** renewable isn't automatically sustainable: biomass grown with a lot of fertiliser, or on land cleared for it, can do more harm than good.

## Re-use and cradle to cradle

- Re-use (*hergebruik*): using a product again (a returnable bottle), recycling its material (glass, metals, plastics), or, inside a plant, returning unreacted starting material to the reactor (recirculation; see [F01](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F01-industrial-processes/notes.md)). Each saves raw materials, energy and waste. *(SLO G4; CvTE T1.1, T2.3)*
- Cradle to cradle (*cradle-to-cradle*): products are designed so that after use every material returns to a cycle without losing quality, either the biological cycle (materials that break down safely: waste is food) or the technical cycle (materials recovered at the same quality, such as metals). Cycles and the conservation laws behind them are in [C03](../../C-chemical-processes-and-conservation-laws/C03-conservation-laws-and-cycles/notes.md). *(SLO G4; CvTE T2.3)*
- **Trap:** recycling into a product of lower quality (downcycling, such as mixed plastics into park benches) isn't cradle to cradle.

## Energy management

- Energy management (*energiebeheer*): use as little energy as possible and reuse what's released. Examples: heat exchangers that use the heat of an exothermic step to warm incoming streams, combined heat and power ([G02](../G02-environmental-impact-assessment/notes.md)), catalysts that allow lower temperatures, insulation, and renewable electricity. Energy flows in block diagrams are in [F01](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F01-industrial-processes/notes.md). *(SLO G4; CvTE T1.1)*
- To judge a change, compare the energy per kg of product before and after, and where the energy comes from.
- **Trap:** an exothermic reaction doesn't make a process energy-neutral: heating to the starting temperature, compression and separation (distillation in particular) also cost energy, so judge the whole process.

## Water quality

- How it's guaranteed: legal standards with a limit value per substance for drinking water (the EU limit for nitrate is 50 mg L⁻¹), permits that limit what companies may discharge into surface water, and regular measurements by water companies and authorities. *(SLO G4; CvTE T4.1, T6.4)*
- Drinking water is made from groundwater or surface water with steps such as aeration, flocculation and settling, sand filtration, adsorption of organic substances on activated carbon, and disinfection with UV light or ozone. Aeration of groundwater oxidises dissolved iron(II), which then precipitates and is filtered off: 4 Fe²⁺ + O₂ + 10 H₂O → 4 Fe(OH)₃ + 8 H⁺. The separation methods are in [D01](../../D-development-of-chemical-knowledge/D01-chemical-techniques/notes.md). *(SLO G4; CvTE R&A2.1)*
- Wastewater treatment protects surface water: bacteria break down organic matter, which would otherwise use up the oxygen in rivers, and nitrogen and phosphate compounds are removed, phosphate for example by precipitation with iron salts.
- Worked example with invented numbers: a well contains 1.2·10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ nitrate, which is 1.2·10⁻³ × 62.01 = 7.4·10⁻² g L⁻¹ = 74 mg L⁻¹. That's above the limit, so the water company has to mix it with low-nitrate water or remove nitrate. Nitrate in groundwater mostly comes from fertiliser and manure. *(CvTE R&A1.2)*
- **Trap:** the nitrate limit is in mg of NO₃⁻ per litre, not mg of N; convert with the molar mass of the ion.

## Air quality

- Main pollutants: particulate matter (*fijnstof*; PM10 and PM2.5 are particles smaller than 10 and 2.5 µm), NO₂, ground-level ozone (O₃) and SO₂. They cause or worsen lung and heart disease; SO₂ and NOₓ also cause acidification. *(SLO G4; CvTE T5.2)*
- Ozone isn't emitted directly: it forms in sunlight from NOₓ and volatile organic compounds. Light splits NO₂, NO₂ → NO + O, and the O atoms form ozone: O + O₂ → O₃. That's why ozone levels peak on sunny summer days.
- How it's guaranteed: EU limit values for concentrations in outdoor air, emission limits for plants and vehicles, and a national measuring network run by the RIVM. Measures include flue-gas cleaning ([G03](../G03-energy-and-industry/notes.md)), low-sulfur fuels, dust filters and catalytic converters in cars: 2 CO + 2 NO → 2 CO₂ + N₂. *(SLO G4; CvTE T4.1)*
- **Trap:** high up, the ozone layer protects against UV light; at ground level the same substance is a pollutant that harms the lungs.

## Soil quality

- Pollutants: heavy metals such as lead, cadmium, mercury, zinc and copper, oil products and pesticides, from industry, old waste dumps, traffic and agriculture. Heavy metals don't break down and build up in plants and animals; in acidic soil their ions dissolve better, so they spread and are taken up more easily. *(SLO G4; CvTE T6.4)*
- How it's guaranteed: soil standards with values above which soil must be cleaned up, soil investigation before building, and permits.
- Remediation (*bodemsanering*): digging up the soil and cleaning or storing it, washing out the contaminants, letting bacteria break down oil (biological remediation), pumping up and treating groundwater, or isolating the contamination.
- **Trap:** bacteria can break down oil, but not heavy metals: those can only be removed or fixed in place.

## Food quality

- Additives (*levensmiddelenadditieven*) get an E-number (*E-nummer*) once they're approved in the EU, for example E300 ascorbic acid (antioxidant), E330 citric acid (acid), E211 sodium benzoate (preservative) and E322 lecithin (emulsifier). *(SLO G4)*
- The acceptable daily intake (ADI, *aanvaardbare dagelijkse inname*) is the amount per kg of body mass that a person can take in every day for a lifetime without a noticeable health risk. It's derived from the highest dose without effect in animal tests, usually divided by a safety factor of 100.
- Maximum residue limits set how much of a pesticide or veterinary medicine may remain in a food. In the Netherlands the NVWA (the food and consumer product safety authority) checks food against such limits.
- Worked example with invented numbers: suppose a preservative has an ADI of 5.0 mg per kg body mass per day, and a soft drink contains 0.15 g of it per litre. A 60 kg person may take in 5.0 × 60 = 3.0·10² mg per day, which is in 3.0·10² mg / 150 mg L⁻¹ = 2.0 L of the drink.
- Antioxidants slow down the reaction of oxygen with unsaturated fats ([G01](../G01-chemistry-of-life/notes.md)), which makes the fats rancid.
- **Trap:** an ADI is per kg of body mass, so a child reaches it with less food or drink than an adult.

## Judging a proposed modification

- Name the quality (water, air, soil or food) that the change affects, and compare before and after with the data: emissions per kg of product against the limits, raw materials, energy, waste and safety. *(SLO G4; CvTE T2.1)*
- Check whether the problem just moves elsewhere: removing SO₂ with limestone produces gypsum and CO₂; a new solvent can bring a new hazard ([F04](../../F-industrial-chemical-processes/F04-risks-and-safety/notes.md)); burning waste saves landfill but needs flue-gas cleaning. The consequences of a process and the m.e.r. procedure are in [G02](../G02-environmental-impact-assessment/notes.md).
- End with a reasoned conclusion: which effect weighs most, and why.
