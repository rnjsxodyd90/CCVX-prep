# Mock exam 2 — Marking scheme

Duration 180 minutes; total 80 points. Open questions marked with partial credit.

**General marking rules:**
- A step marked (•) scores even if carried over from a wrong earlier value (consequential-error rule).
- Correct alternative methods score full marks.
- Per question part: missing/wrong final unit costs 1 point (once per part).
- Numerical answers within ±1 in the last digit of the model answer are accepted (intermediate rounding); significant figures score only where asked.
- Equations: atoms and charge must balance for the equation point.

## Question 1 — Ammonia from a scrubber (12)

a (4p)
- 1p: NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ written.
- 1p: x = [OH⁻] = √(K_b × c) (or full quadratic).
- 1p: x = 9.5·10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹ and approximation check.
- 1p: pOH and pH = 10.98 (± 0.02).

b (4p)
- 1p: n(NH₃) = 25.00 × 0.0500 = 1.25 mmol.
- 1p: at the equivalence point n(H₃O⁺) = n(NH₃) (reaction 1:1).
- 1p: V = 1.25/0.0400 = 31.3 mL.
- 1p: unit and 3 significant figures.

c (4p)
- 1p: before: x = 9.5·10⁻⁴ → 1.9% (accept 1.9–2.0%).
- 1p: after: x = 3.0·10⁻⁴ → 6.0% (exact quadratic gives 5.8%; accept 5.8–6.1%).
- 1p: conclusion: dilution raises the degree of ionisation.
- 1p: explanation in terms of the equilibrium expression (quotient drops on dilution → further reaction until Q = K).

## Question 2 — Refining copper (13)

a (3p)
- 1p: + : Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2 e⁻.
- 1p: − : Cu²⁺ + 2 e⁻ → Cu.
- 1p: electron flow: anode → cathode through the external wire.

b (4p)
- 1p: Zn and Ni are stronger reductors than Cu (lower V₀).
- 1p: so they dissolve too (as Zn²⁺, Ni²⁺).
- 1p: Ag and Au are weaker reductors (higher V₀), so they do not dissolve.
- 1p: Ag/Au fall as anode sludge.

c (3p)
- 1p: at the negative electrode the strongest oxidator present is reduced.
- 1p: Cu²⁺ (+0.34 V) wins against Ni²⁺ (−0.26) and Zn²⁺ (−0.76).
- 1p: so pure Cu deposits while the bath accumulates Ni²⁺/Zn²⁺.

d (3p)
- 1p: Cu mass is conserved through the bath.
- 1p: 98 kg pure copper deposited.
- 1p: 2.0 kg split into dissolved ions (Zn²⁺, Ni²⁺) and sludge (Ag, Au).

## Question 7 — Shells in a souring ocean (8)

c (2p)
- 1p: gas solubility falls as T rises (Binas, gas solubility).
- 1p: so cold deep water stores more CO₂.
