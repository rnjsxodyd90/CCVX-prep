## Opgave A — green chemistry with dimethyl carbonate (Q1–Q8)

**Subdomains:** F02 (E-factor), C06 (formation heats), C04/C06 (energy diagrams, catalyst), C05 (K, Q, removing water), B03/B01 (boiling points, polarity, structure), F01 (block diagrams, recycling), C02 (methanol mass balance). **BINAS:** formation heats, the table of molar masses given in the question.

### Q1 (2p) — E-factor of the polycarbonate process at 92% yield
**Solution:** starting materials per mol of polymer link: bisphenol-A (228) + phosgene (98.9) + 2 NaOH (80.0) → total 406.9 g; product obtained at 92%: 0.92 × 254 = 233.7 g polymer links. Waste = 406.9 − 233.7 = 173.2 g; E = 173.2/233.7 = **0.74**.

### Q2 (3p) — formation heat of DMC
**Solution:** from figure 2's stoichiometry: 2 CH₃OH + CO₂ → DMC + H₂O. ΔE = +0.67·10⁵ = [E_DMC + (−2.42·10⁵)] − [2 × (−2.02·10⁵) + (−3.94·10⁵)]. Solve: E_DMC = +0.67·10⁵ − 2.42·10⁵ −(−7.98·10⁵)? do the algebra carefully: E_DMC = ΔE + E(reactants) − E(H₂O) = 0.67·10⁵ + (−7.98·10⁵) − (−2.42·10⁵) = **−4.89·10⁵ J mol⁻¹**. 

### Q3 (3p) — draw both energy diagrams with the labels (without and with catalyst)
**Solution:** reaction is endothermic (+0.67·10⁵), so products above reactants in both diagrams; the hump (activated state) high in diagram 1 (uncatalysed), lower in diagram 2 (catalysed), while the product level is the same in both. Mark: Ea vs Ea(catalyst), ΔE in one.

### Q4 (2p) — equilibrium condition of reaction 1
**Solution:** K = [DMC]·[H₂O]/([CH₃OH]²·[CO₂]), per the reaction 2 CH₃OH + CO₂ ⇌ DMC + H₂O.

### Q5 (2p) — why adding cyanopyridine raises the DMC yield
**Solution:** cyanopyridine binds water so no water leaves R1: water (a reaction product) is removed from the equilibrium → the equilibrium shifts right / the reaction runs to completion → more DMC.

### Q6 (4p) — draw cyanopyridine + explain picolinamide's higher boiling point
**Solution:** cyanopyridine: a pyridine ring with a nitrile group (~C≡N attached); picolinamide: the same ring with –CONH₂ instead (amide). Higher boiling point of picolinamide because the amide NH₂ makes hydrogen bridges between its own molecules plus strong dipole-dipole interaction per the amide bond (and it is a bigger molecule with more electrons → more Van der Waals): those win over the nitrile's interactions.

### Q7 (3p) — complete the block diagram with R4 and the three flows
**Solution:** picolinamide (2) goes from S1's bottom to R4; in R4 converted fully to water and cyanopyridine: out: water (3) to waste; cyanopyridine (1) loops back to R1. Water also enters R4 (not shown between R1/S1 as instructed).

### Q8 (3p) — methanol: closed loop or net consumed?
**Solution:** reacties 1 and 2 use and form methanol equally, so those two cancel out. In R1 also methanol reacts to side products (ammonia + substance Z) through the extra side reaction → so methanol is unavoidably lost to waste streams → the process consumes methanol **netto** (not a closed system: fresh methanol must be fed).

### Q10 (2p) — why crystalline regions form when natural rubber is stretched, and why less in synthetic poly-isoprene
**Solution:** stretching aligns the chains; natural rubber's regular cis-1,4 structure lets the chains pack tightly (crystalline regions form between aligned sections). Synthetic poly-isoprene has four different link modes mixed in, so the chains are too irregular to pack: fewer crystalline regions on stretching.

### Q11 (2p) — crystalline regions protect against deformation
**Solution:** in the crystalline regions the chains lie close together → greater contact area per chain → stronger Van der Waals bonding between chains than in the amorphous regions; they act as physical crosslinks, so the band resists deformation better.

### Q12 (3p) — number of butadiene units per styrene unit in SBR (mass ratio 25 : 75)
**Solution:** per 1.00 kg: n(styrene) = 250/104.1 = 2.40 mol; n(butadiene) = 750/54.09 = 13.9 mol; ratio = 13.9/2.40 = **5.8** butadiene units per styrene unit.

## Opgave C — niacine / vitamin B3 (Q14–Q20)

**Subdomains:** C01 (equations), D03/E02 (enzyme logic), C05 (weak acid Kz), G01/B01 (DNA codons, protein chains). **BINAS:** codon table (71G), molar masses (quinoline acid 167, niacine 123), K_z given.

### Q14 (4p) — equation forming picoline from propanal + prop-2-enal + ammonia
**Solution:** picoline = methylpyridine C₆H₇N. The reaction: CH₃–CH₂–CHO + CH₂=CH–CHO + NH₃ → picoline + 2 H₂O + H₂: balance: left: CH3CH2CHO (C3H6O) + CH2=CHCHO (C3H4O) + NH3: C6 H: 6+4+3=13, N1, O2. Right: picoline C6H7N + 2H2O + H2: H: 7+4+2 = 13 ✓; O: 2 ✓ → the third product is H₂.
**Common mistakes:** forgetting the H₂ (the ring closure needs the two‑carbon-losses-as-water logic of enamine-imine condensation; count hydrogens carefully).

### Q15 (2p) — the enzyme modification table
**Solution:** goal 1 (no other products → no stockpiled Y): **Q's concentration raised hard** (the Y-consuming enzyme). Goal 2 (max quinolinezuur per hour): **P raised** (more input flux) and... per the official marks: P: higher; Q: much higher; R: lower. R lower because it drains quinolinezuur further (its product is the target itself; lower R keeps quinolinezuur from being processed away/downstream).

### Q16 (3p) — yield quinolinezuur → niacine
**Solution:** max theoretical mass: (5.5/167) × 123 = 4.05 g; actual 3.8 g: yield = 3.8/4.05 = **94%**.

### Q17 (4p) — pH where 90% of niacine is the zuurrest-ion
**Solution:** K_z = [A⁻][H⁺]/[HA] = 1.3·10⁻⁵; 90% ratio: [A⁻]/[HA] = 9 → [H⁺] = 1.3·10⁻⁵/9 = 1.4·10⁻⁶ → **pH = 5.84** (2 decimals).

### Q18 (2p) — around the receptor, is the real pH higher or lower?
**Solution:** "more than 90%" ionised means ratio A⁻:HA > 9, so [H⁺] < 1.4·10⁻⁶, so pH **higher** than 5.84.

## Opgave D — the plaster that makes current (Q21–Q24)

**Subdomains:** D03/C04 (polymerisation mechanism), C01 (half-reactions in a cell), C04 (rate concept), C02-adjacent (charge and mass). **BINAS:** the mol-electron charge (9.65·10⁴ C, given in the question), molar masses.

### Q21 (2p) — complete the mechanism (lone pairs + arrows)
**Solution:** the step is an addition to an epoxide/double bond: lone pairs on O/N (2, 1 respectively); arrows: nucleophile's lone pair → to the δ+ ring carbon; ring bond pair → onto the ring O/N leaving; the N keeps a +1 charge after receiving a new bond (write the formal charges).

### Q22 (2p) — the two half-reactions in the electrodes
**Solution:** negative (LOx, oxidation): CH₃CH(OH)COO⁻ → CH₃COCOO⁻ + 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻ (lactate → pyruvate ion); positive: Ag₂O + 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻ → 2 Ag + H₂O.

### Q23 (1p) — why rate grows slower at higher lactate concentration
**Solution:** at high lactate, the enzyme's active sites become saturated (all LOx enzymes occupied), so extra lactate no longer raises the rate — enzyme saturation kinetics (also constant Ag₂O amount).

### Q24 (4p) — mass of lactate converted in 10 min at 1.2·10⁻³ C s⁻¹
**Solution:** charge: Q = 1.2·10⁻³ × 600 = 0.72 C; n(e⁻) = 0.72/9.65·10⁴ = 7.5·10⁻⁶ mol. Lactate → pyruvate loses 2 e⁻ per molecule: n(lactate) = 3.7·10⁻⁶ mol; mass = ×89.07? the lactate ion C₃H₅O₃⁻: 89.07 → wait M(lactaat) 89.1: 3.7·10⁻⁶ × 89.1 = **3.3·10⁻⁴ g** (2 s.f.).
