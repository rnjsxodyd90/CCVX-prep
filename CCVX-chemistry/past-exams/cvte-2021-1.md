## Opgave A — 'green' paint, supercritical CO₂ (Q1–Q5)

**Subdomains:** B01/B03 (dipoles, structure), D03 (polymers), D01 (separations), C04/C01 (substitution mechanism, competitive side reaction). **BINAS:** structures per figure.

### Q1 (2p) — CO₂ structure and why apolar
**Solution:** O=C=O is linear; the two identical polar bonds point exactly opposite, so the δ+ and δ− cancel: the molecule as a whole is apolar.

### Q2 (1p) — why swelling gives a stronger colour
**Solution:** swelled fibres open space between the polymer chains, so pigment molecules can enter deeper into every fibre — more pigment per fibre, hence a stronger colour.

### Q3 (2p) — how to separate the pigment from supercritical CO₂
**Solution:** let the pressure drop (relax) / lower the pressure: CO₂ becomes a gas again and leaves; the apolar pigment remains — separable by simple pressure release/decompression (or gentle evaporation), so both can be reused.

### Q4 (2p) — complete the substitution: cotton-OH + dichlorotriazine-pigment
**Solution:** the cotton's O (lone pair) attacks the C bearing Cl of the triazine ring: C–Cl bond breaks (Cl⁻ leaves), new C–O–cotton bond forms; structural: cotton–O–triazine–pigment + H⁺ + Cl⁻ (or – Cl⁻; count charges: the chloride ion leaves).

### Q5 (2p) — why methanol in the dye mix lowers the bound-pigment percentage
**Solution:** methanol also carries an OH group and competes with the cotton's OH groups for the same reactive pigment: part of the pigment is consumed reacting with methanol instead of cotton → lower bound percentage on the cotton.

## Opgave B — the chloroprene factory (Q6–Q11)

**Subdomains:** C04 (resonance, radical/cationic mechanisms), E02 (stereoisomers), C05 (equilibrium with conditions), F01/F02 (block diagram, recycle, stoichiometry with Vm given). **BINAS:** V_m = 24.5 L mol⁻¹ given as 2.45·10⁻² m³ mol⁻¹ in the question.

### Q6 (2p) — two resonance forms of structural isomer 2
**Solution:** the chloronium ring opens to an allylic carbocation: form A: Cl–CH₂–CH⁺–CH=CH₂ ↔ form B: Cl–CH₂–CH=CH–CH₂⁺: draw the Cl atom fully, the lone pairs (4 on Cl), the + formal charge on the carbocation carbon (the one lacking a full octet).

### Q7 (2p) — why mechanism 2 can't explain the observed cis/trans ratio (19% : 81%)
**Solution:** mechanism 2 passes through a cyclic chloronium ion fixed in a cis geometry, so it would give (predominantly) cis-1,4-DCB; the measured product is mostly trans. Alternatively: to get trans, the C=C double bond would have to break for free rotation — no step in mechanism 2 allows that. Either way, mechanism 2 fails as the explanation of the observed ratio.

### Q8 (2p) — equilibrium condition + why the conditions maximise the 3,4-DCB yield
**Solution:** the equilibrium 1,4-DCB ⇌ 3,4-DCB: K = [1,4-DCB]/[3,4-DCB], but take the gas-fraction: 3,4-DCB boils at 119 °C in R2's pressure, lower than the 125–150 °C T of R2 → 3,4-DCB leaves as gas (the 1,4 forms stay liquid as their boiling points exceed 152 °C): physically removed product → equilibrium shifts right continuously.

### Q9 (3p) — complete the mechanism of 3,4-DCB + NaOH → chloroprene
**Solution:** OH⁻ (nucleophile) attacks the C bearing Cl (C3 or C4?—actually the allylic shift: OH⁻ removes the allylic H forming the second double bond, and Cl⁻ leaves — an elimination): net: 3,4-DCB + OH⁻ → CH₂=C(Cl)–CH=CH₂ + Cl⁻ + H₂O. Lone pairs: O of OH⁻ (3) and arrows: lone pair O→C; C–Cl bond→Cl; C–H bond→C=C. Formal charge: OH⁻ (−1), Cl⁻ (−1).

### Q10 (4p) — complete the block diagram
**Solution:** N₂ (9), Cl₂ (2) and butadiene (1) into R1 (recycled from S1 back); from R1 the fluid mix to S1; S1: gases (9,2,1) recirculate to R1 with a bleed-off; liquid from S1 to R2; from R2: one stream all-3,4-DCB (4) → R3; other (1,4-DCB 5 + nevenproducten 8) → S2: 1,4-DCB (5) loops back to R2, 8 out as waste; in R3: 3,4-DCB + NaOH (7) in; out: chloroprene (3) as gas and NaCl solution (6).

### Q11 (5p) — m³ of chlorine needed for 1.0 ton chloroprene
**Solution:** n(chloroprene) = 1.0·10⁶ g/88.54 g mol⁻¹ = 1.13·10⁴ mol (C₄H₅Cl = 88.54). Overall yields: R1+R2: 70%, R3: 93% → combined: 0.651. Needed n(Cl₂) = 1.13·10⁴/0.651 = 1.73·10⁴ mol; V = 1.73·10⁴ × 2.45·10⁻² = 4.3·10² m³ (2 s.f.).

## Opgave C — hemochromatosis / iron overload (Q12–Q16)

**Subdomains:** G01/B01 (codons, mutation notation), B03/G01 (protein binding groups), C01 (condensations), C05/C02 (buffers, capacity). **BINAS:** codon table (71G), K_z of 'H₂CO₃' (4.5·10⁻⁷).

### Q12 (4p) — mutation notation
**Solution:** base 1066 of the gene is, in mRNA coordinates, base 1066 − 221 = 845 (start codon begins at 222); 845 / 3 = 281.67 → second base of codon 282. From the figure, healthy reads codon UGC (cysteine), the diseased UAC (tyrosine): mutation **C282Y**.

### Q13 (2p) — why Fe³⁺ detaches from transferrin at low pH
**Solution:** at low pH the Tyr and Asp side chains take up H⁺ (become neutral), so they lose their ion-dipole attraction for Fe³⁺; His' side chain even becomes positive, which repels Fe³⁺ → the iron is released.

### Q14 (3p) — draw the fragment ~B–A–E of desferrioxamine
**Solution:** E = ethanoic acid (acetyl), B = succinic acid, A given in the figure (a diamine-like unit). Join E's COOH to A's N (amide), then A's other end to B's COOH (amide), then A's N: per C–N bond, one H₂O: draw the alternating amide chain: ~B–A–E with every amide group –CO–NH– explicitly shown and the right connectivities per the figure's A.

### Q15 (5p) — pH of the buffer from 0.338 mol NaHCO₃ + 0.034 mol HCl per litre
**Solution:** H₃O⁺ from HCl reacts with HCO₃⁻ to 'H₂CO₃' completely: [H₂CO₃] = 0.034 mol L⁻¹, [HCO₃⁻] = 0.338 − 0.034 = 0.304 mol L⁻¹. Buffer: [H₃O⁺] = K_z × [acid]/[base] = 4.5·10⁻⁷ × 0.034/0.304 = 5.0·10⁻⁸; **pH = 7.30** (2 decimals).

### Q16 (3p) — free iron-binding capacity CF
**Solution:** n(transferrin) = 3.0/8.0·10⁴ = 3.75·10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹; sites per molecule: 2 → total 7.5·10⁻⁵ mol sites per litre; free = 70%: **CF = 5.3·10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹** of Fe³⁺ binding places.

## Opgave D — the sugar battery (Q17–Q19)

**Subdomains:** C06 (formation heats, % of combustion energy), C01 (half-reactions with Pi carriers). **BINAS:** formation heats table 56/57 (CO₂ −3.9e5, H₂O(l) −2.86e5, CH₃COOH −4.84e5, glucose −12.61e5).

### Q17 (4p) — % of max possible energy released
**Solution:** battery reaction: ΔE = [2×(−3.94) + 2×(−2.86) + 2×(−4.84)]·10⁵ − (−12.61·10⁵) = (−23.28 + 12.61)·10⁵ = −10.7·10⁵ J mol⁻¹. Full combustion: ΔE = −28.2·10⁵ J mol⁻¹. Fraction: 10.7/28.2 = **38%** (the key's answer: 37.9%).

### Q18 (3p) — half-reaction II
**Solution:** C₆H₁₁O₅–Pi + H₂O → C₅H₉O₄–Pi + CO₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ (oxidation: 6 C → 5 C + CO₂).

### Q19 (2p) — reaction III
**Solution:** 6 C₅H₉O₄–Pi + H₂O → 5 C₆H₁₁O₅–Pi + H–Pi: carbon count: 6×5 = 5×6 ✓; H: 6×9+2 = 56 vs 5×11+1 = 56 ✓; O: 6×4+1 = 25 = 5×5 ✓; Pi groups: 6 = 6 ✓.

## Opgave E — Marquis reagent and alkaloids (Q20–Q23)

**Subdomains:** C02 (mole ratio from density/percentage), G01/D03 (natural alkaloid structures), C1/other (basicity reasoning), B01/B03 (NO₂ lone pairs, formal/partial charges, H-bond). **BINAS:** given table (mass%, density), molar masses (H₂SO₄ 98.1, CH₂O 30.0).

### Q20 (4p) — mole ratio zwavelzuur : methanal
**Solution:** per 20.0 mL acid: m = 20.0×1.84 = 36.8 g; acid = 0.98×36.8 = 36.1 g → n = 36.1/98.1 = 0.368 mol. Per 1.0 mL methanal solution: 1.09 g; methanal = 0.37×1.09 = 0.403 g → n = 0.403/30.03 = 1.34·10⁻² mol. Ratio = 0.368/1.34·10⁻² = **27 : 1.0**.

### Q21 (2p) — encircle the tyrosine part in morphine
**Solution:** tyrosine = 4-hydroxyphenylalanine; in morphine the (hydroxy)-benzene ring plus the two-carbon tie to the N: circle the aromatic ring and the two carbons of the C₂-bridging chain that link it to the tertiary amine centre (the structure "tyrosine skeleton": Ar-ring with OH + the 2-C chain to N).

### Q22 (2p) — which of the three alkaloids is not basic
**Solution:** the alkaloid with the quaternary nitrogen (berberine in the figure): its N atom already has four bonds and carries the formal +1 charge of a quaternary ammonium ion, so it has no free lone pair to accept another H⁺ — it cannot react as a base. Morphine and heroin carry a tertiary amine N with one free lone pair and can accept an H⁺, so they do react as bases.

### Q23 (2p) — lone pairs on NO₂, charges, and the H-bridge in nitro-morphine
**Solution:** NO₂ group: draw N=O double bond + N–O single bond; the single-bonded O carries 3 lone pairs and charge −1, N carries +1 (four bonds); the double O has 2 lone pairs with partial δ−. Hydrogen bridge: dotted line from the nitro-O (the −1-carrying single-bonded O) to the neighbouring phenolic O–H of morphine — this intramolecular bridge is what heroin (lacking the free OH) cannot make and what colours morphine orange.
