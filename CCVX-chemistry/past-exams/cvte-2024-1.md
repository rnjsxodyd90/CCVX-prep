# Walkthrough — CvTE vwo scheikunde 2024, tijdvak 1

23 questions in four opgaves: DME from CO₂, the Battolyser (battery-electrolyser), micro-balloons (drug delivery, PLGA), creatine. 2024 is also the last year compatible with the old numbering of bigger exams. Get the free official paper + correctievoorschrift + uitwerkbijlage from examenblad.nl. Nothing of the official text, figures, tables or answer-key wording is copied here: every line is our own phrasing and every number is derived independently. Note: vwo exams give the charge value of 1 mol electrons (9.65·10⁴ C) in the question when needed — not in CCVX's curriculum, so for CCVX skip the coulomb-only reasoning in Q8.

## Opgave A — DME from carbon dioxide (Q1–Q5)

**Subdomains:** C06 (formation heats), C05 (K, Q, pressure), B03 (boiling point reasoning), F01 (block diagram), F02 (E-factor). **BINAS:** formation heats (CO₂ −3.935·10⁵, H₂O(g) −2.42·10⁵; DME given −1.84·10⁵ J mol⁻¹), boiling points (table 42/8.3).

### Q1 (4p) — total equation + heat of formation per mol DME
**Solution:** 2 CO₂(g) + 6 H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃–O–CH₃(g) + 3 H₂O(g) (reaction 1 ×2 + reaction 2). ΔE = [(−1.84) + 3×(−2.42) − 2×(−3.94)]·10⁵ = (−1.84 − 7.26 + 7.87)·10⁵ = **−1.22·10⁵ J mol⁻¹** (exothermic). 

### Q2 (3p) — equilibrium condition of reaction 1 + why high pressure in R1
**Solution:** K = [CH₃OH][H₂O]/([CO₂][H₂]³). Raising the pressure multiplies the denominator faster than the numerator (4 gas particles left versus 2 right), so Q < K and the equilibrium must shift right: higher conversion/yield.

### Q3 (2p) — why DME has the lowest boiling point
**Solution:** methanol and water molecules make hydrogen bridges with each other (strongest intermolecular bond), DME molecules cannot (no OH): only Van der Waals and dipole-dipole bonds hold them; so vaporising DME costs least energy — lowest boiling point.

### Q4 (3p) — complete the block diagram
**Solution:** in: H₂ and CO₂ → R1 → S1: out at the bottom: water + by-products; methanol top onwards to R2 → S2: distillation splits into DME (lowest boiling point: top), methanol (middle fraction → recycle to the R1/R2 area), water (bottom out). Draw the recycle arrow of methanol back to a reactor.

### Q5 (3p) — E-factor at 63% yield (reaction 3) and whether it is min or max
**Solution:** reaction 3: CO + 5 H₂ + CO₂ → CH₃–O–CH₃ + 2 H₂O. Mass of reactants per mol: 28.01 + 5×2.016 + 44.01 = 82.1 g. Product at 63% yield: 0.63 × 46.07 = 29.0 g. E = (82.1 − 29.0)/29.0 = **1.8**. Since E falls as the yield rises, and 63% was the highest observed, this E is the **minimum** of the study.

## Opgave B — the Battolyser (Q6–Q10)

**Subdomains:** C01 (half-reactions, electrolysis, ion motion in a cell), C2-adjacent (charge per mol e⁻: vwo-extra), C04 (activation energy vs rate), C02 (gas/stoichiometry). **BINAS:** standard electrode potentials reasoning, molar mass, V_m not needed.

### Q6 (3p) — charging half-reactions and total
**Solution:** left electrode (reduction): Fe(OH)₂ + 2 e⁻ → Fe + 2 OH⁻. Right electrode (oxidation, ×2): Ni(OH)₂ + OH⁻ → NiO(OH) + H₂O + e⁻. Total: Fe(OH)₂ + 2 Ni(OH)₂ → Fe + 2 NiO(OH) + 2 H₂O.

### Q7 (2p) — direction of OH⁻ through the membrane during charging
**Solution:** electrons flow through the wire from right to left; electroneutrality forces negative charge the other way through the membrane: OH⁻ moves from left to right. (Same conclusion via OH⁻: produced at the left electrode, consumed at the right.)

### Q8 (3p) — average transported charge per second
**Solution:** n(H₂O) = 1.4·10³ g/18.016 = 77.7 mol → n(e⁻) = 155 mol over 18 months ≈ 4.665·10⁷ s: 155 × 9.65·10⁴ / 4.665·10⁷ = **0.32 C s⁻¹** (0.32 A). For CCVX: just see the method, CCVX does not examine charge in coulombs.

### Q9 (2p) — mass% FeO(OH) with the highest rate
**Solution:** rate is highest where the activation energy is lowest: at about **25 mass% FeO(OH)**.

### Q10 (5p) — volume of formic acid for 6.33 kg H₂
**Solution:** n(H₂) = 6.33·10³/2.016 = 3.14·10³ mol; reaction 1: 1 mol acid per mol H₂, at 90.0%: n(acid) = 3.14·10³/0.900 = 3.49·10³ mol; m = ×46.03 = 1.61·10⁵ g = 1.61·10² kg; V = 1.61·10²/1.22 = **1.3·10² L** (two significant figures).

## Opgave C — micro-balloons for drug delivery (Q11–Q16)

**Subdomains:** D03 (polyesters, hydrolysis), D01/C01 (separation methods), B03/B04 (solubility, dipoles, hydrophilicity), E01 (drug design reasoning), B01/D04 (Lewis + mechanism). **BINAS:** none numerical.

### Q11 (3p) — full hydrolysis of PLGA
**Solution:** the polyester has two ester links per repeat unit: hydrolyse both with one H₂O per ester: the chain of n repeat units + 2n H₂O → n lactic acid (CH₃–CH(OH)–COOH) + n glycolic acid (HO–CH₂–COOH). Draw both structural formulas; the water count balances the elements.

### Q12 (2p) — the two separation steps
**Solution:** extraction (solvent moves into the water phase; the PLGA-drug forms the suspension, separated) and evaporation/distillation (the organic solvent evaporates and is recovered for reuse).

### Q13 (2p) — EE with a highly water-soluble drug
**Solution:** a highly water-soluble drug partitions largely into the water phase instead of being captured inside the hardening PLGA droplets → less drug inside the microballoons → **EE relatively low**.

### Q14 (3p) — dichloromethane vs tetrachloromethane: which dissolves in water better
**Solution:** both are tetrahedra at C; in CH₂Cl₂, the lopsided pasting of the charge midpoints makes the molecule a dipole; in CCl₄ all four identical poles cancel the dipole out — apolar whole. Water is dipolar too, so it attracts CH₂Cl₂ molecules better: **dichloromethane is the more water-soluble one**.

### Q15 (3p) — Lewis structure of particle X in the ammonolysis
**Solution:** the ammonia N with its lone pair attacks the carbonyl C of DCEM's ester: X = the charged tetrahedral intermediate: N now with four bonds (formal +1) connected to the carbonyl C, the former C=O oxygen now single-bonded with three lone pairs and formal −1. Count lone pairs: 1 per N (none left after bonding), 3 on the former carbonyl O, plus the rest of the molecule unchanged.
**Common mistakes:** leaving N's charge unwritten; giving the leaving O a double bond (it became single); adding arrows from the positive centres.

### Q16 (2p) — water solubility of the weak-base drug at lower pH
**Solution:** at lower pH the weak base takes up H⁺: drug-B + H₃O⁺ → drug-BH⁺ + H₂O, forming charged particles; charged particles form strong ion-dipole bonds with water → solubility **higher** at lower pH.

## Opgave D — creatine (Q17–Q23)

**Subdomains:** B01 (Lewis, structure), D03/G01 (enzymes, protein), C04 (mechanism arrows), C02 (daily-need stoichiometry), G01/B01 (DNA codons), D01 (chromatography, K_v). **BINAS:** amino acid/codon table (70E/71G), molar masses (creatine 131, glycine 75.1).

### Q17 (2p) — draw ornithine
**Solution:** glycocyamine is made from glycine's N docked onto the arginine's guanidinium side-chain C; splitting off the rest gives ornithine = the arginine chain minus the guanidino group, ending in –NH₂: structural formula HOOC–CH(NH₂)–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂–NH₂ (5 C total).

### Q18 (2p) — two interactions holding glycocyamine in the enzyme's active site
**Solution:** ion-dipole bonds (from the deprotonated carboxylate −COO⁻ of the substrate to a positive group of the enzyme) and hydrogen bridges (between peptide-chain N–H/C=O of the enzyme and O/N groups of the substrate; also dipole-dipole is acceptable where no ions are involved).

### Q19 (2p) — complete the methylation mechanism with lone pairs and arrows
**Solution:** the CH₃⁺-carrying sulfur of SAM is attacked by the nucleophilic N of glycocyamine: arrow from N's lone pair to the methyl C, and the S–CH₃ bond pair moves onto the S: arrow from bond onto S (sulfonium S goes +1 to neutral, N goes to +1 charged ammonium form — afterwards the proton leaves onto a nearby base). Draw lone pairs on the marking atoms (N: 1; S: 2; carboxylate O: 3 per O).

### Q20 (5p) — grams of glycine needed per day for steady creatine
**Solution:** stock: 70 kg × 1.7 g kg⁻¹ = 119 g creatine; excreted: 1.9% = 2.26 g per day; = 2.26/131 = 1.73·10⁻² mol. Since biosynthesis is 1 : 1 glycine→creatine, 100% need would be 1.73·10⁻² mol; the body self-makes only 80% (20% comes from food): ×0.80 = 1.38·10⁻² mol; ×75.1 g mol⁻¹ = **1.0 g glycine per day** (two significant figures).

### Q21 (3p) — the differing base pair and its number
**Solution:** healthy: GAMT has arginine at amino acid 44; triplets: amino 44 → base pairs 130–132; arginine codon C G N vs leucine: single change middle base G→T (e.g. CGA→CTA). On the coding strand: normal G, variant T (complementary: C vs A); the deviating base pair is number **131** (3×43 + 2).

### Q22 (3p) — which chromatogram peak is creatine
**Solution:** K_v(creatine) = 0.63 ≫ K_v(asparagine) = 1.3·10⁻⁴: the higher K_v means creatine is the more apolar substance (dissolves best in the octanol phase), so it adsorbs most strongly to the apolar stationary phase, hence the latest retention time: **peak 2**.

### Q23 (2p) — glycocyamine and creatine levels in AGAT- and GAMT-deficiency
**Solution:** AGAT deficient: glycocyamine cannot be made → its level is LOW, so downstream creatine is LOW too. GAMT deficient: the step after glycocyamine is blocked: glycocyamine accumulates (equal to or higher than normal), creatine LOW.
