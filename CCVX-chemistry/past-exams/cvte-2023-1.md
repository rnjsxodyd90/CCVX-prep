## Opgave A — biodiesel out of algae (Q1–Q5)

**Subdomains:** G01/photosynthesis, C01 (equations), B03/D03 (fats, esters, transesterification), F01 (block diagrams), C02 (yield). **BINAS:** V_m not needed in this set; molar masses given in the question.

### Q1 (2p) — glucose → pyrodruivenzuur
**Solution:** C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → 2 C₃H₄O₃ + 2 H₂O. Check: C 6=6, H 12=12, O 8=8.
**Common mistakes:** oxidising glucose all the way to CO₂: the question says the CO₂ from glucose is NOT used here (photosynthesis made the glucose first; the question's chain is glucose → pyrodruivenzuur).

### Q2 (4p) — max grams of pyrodruivenzuur per litre at 44.2 g m⁻³ O₂
**Solution:** the O₂ the measurements see is the NET output of the whole chain, not of the last step: write the combined equation. Photosynthesis: 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂; conversion: C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → 2 C₃H₄O₃ + 2 H₂O. Sum: 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → 2 C₃H₄O₃ + 2 H₂O + 5 O₂. Net: per 5 mol of measured O₂ comes 2 mol of pyrodruivenzuur. Per litre: n(O₂) = 44.2·10⁻³/32.0 = 1.381·10⁻³ mol → n(pyro) = ×2/5 = 5.53·10⁻⁴ mol → m = ×88.1 = **4.87·10⁻² g per litre**.
**Common mistakes:** counting the full 2 : 1 ratio from Q1 only (that overlooks the photosynthesis part of the chain — exactly the trap that makes the "44.2 g/m³ measured O₂" meaningful, and the walkthrough's main lesson); volume conversion m³ ↔ L wrong way.

### Q3 (3p) — draw DHA (22:6 ω-3) with stereochemistry
**Solution:** in the shorthand, ω-3 means: the FIRST double bond from the CH₃ end sits between C20 and C21 counting from the C-1 (COOH) side? No — from the omega end: the ω-3 bond lies between the 3rd and 4th carbon counted from the CH₃ end. So the chain reads: CH₃–CH₂–CH=CH–CH₂–(CH=CH–CH₂)₅–CH₂–CH₂–COOH with six C=C total, all cis. Draw the carboxyl at the far end, the 22-carbon chain, the six cis-(CH=CH–CH₂) repeats starting so that the last double bond ends three carbons from the CH₃ terminus.
**Common mistakes:** putting the first bond at the COOH end instead of the CH₃ end; drawing trans double bonds (natural unsaturated fatty acids are cis).

### Q4 (5p) — yield of the biodiesel production
**Solution:** n(algae oil) = 500/884 = 0.5656 mol; theoretical esters: n = 3 × 0.5656 = 1.697 mol; theoretical mass = 1.697 × 296 = 502 g; theoretical volume = 502/874 g L⁻¹? careful: density 874 g L⁻¹ → V = 502/874 = 0.575 L. Actual: 0.392 L. Yield = 0.392/0.575 × 100% = **68.2%** (3 s.f.). (Alternatively in moles: actual = 0.392 L × 874 g L⁻¹ / 296 = 1.157 mol → 1.157/1.697 = 0.682.)

### Q5 (3p) — complete the block diagram (10 reactors + separators)
**Solution:** R9 out → into "R10 + S10" block with methanol added (4) at R10; out of S10: top: biodiesel (2), bottom: the rest (methanol 4 + unreacted/glycerol 3 if present); the loop of R2..R9 receives the output of S1 (after glycerol split: glycerol 3 down, and the oil/methanol stream towards R2..R9); fresh inputs: algenolie (1) and methanol (4) into R1.

## Opgave B — Gerolsteiner mineral water (Q6–Q11)

**Subdomains:** C01 (acid-carbonate), 0-03 (isotopes), D01 (analysis), B01/B04 (Lewis, dipoles), C05 (acid constants, fractions). **BINAS:** isotope table (25A/1.11), 'H₂CO₃' K_z, molar masses.

### Q6 (3p) — dolomite + dissolved CO₂
**Solution:** CaₓMg₍₁₋ₓ₎CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → x Ca²⁺ + (1−x) Mg²⁺ + 2 HCO₃⁻. (Equivalently with H₂CO₃ as the reactant.)

### Q7 (2p) — the other particle in ²³⁸U decay to ⁴He
**Solution:** ²³⁸U → ⁴He + ²³⁴Th. Symbol: **Th**, neutrons: 234 − 90 = **144**.

### Q8 (1p) — analysis technique for the ³He/⁴He ratio
**Solution:** mass spectrometry (MS).

### Q9 (2p) — do the found isotope concentrations rule out a purely rain-derived origin?
**Solution:** in the water: ³He/⁴He = 3·10⁻¹¹/4·10⁻⁶ = 7.5·10⁻⁶. Natural atmospheric ratio from Binas isotope data: abundances 0.00014% (³He) vs ~100% (⁴He) → ratio ≈ 1.4·10⁻⁶. 7.5·10⁻⁶ > natural: so part of the helium (and thus of the dissolved gases) comes from the deeper earth layers. Not exclusively rain.

### Q10 (3p) — Lewis structures CO₂ + H₂O with angles, partial charges and one interaction
**Solution:** CO₂: O=C=O linear (180°), each C=O polar (δ− on O, δ+ on C). H₂O: bent, ≈109.5°, O δ−, H δ+. Interaction: dotted line from the lone pair of the water O to the δ+ carbon of CO₂ (dipole-dipole attraction).

### Q11 (3p) — explain from the table why Gerolsteiner Sprudel has a higher pH than SPA Intense
**Solution:** write the constant: K_z = [HCO₃⁻][H₃O⁺]/[H₂CO₃]. Gerolsteiner's [HCO₃⁻] is much higher (1816 vs 18 mg L⁻¹) while [H₂CO₃] barely differs. For K to keep its value, a larger numerator [HCO₃⁻] must be matched by a smaller [H₃O⁺] — hence a higher pH for Gerolsteiner. (The loose reasoning "equilibrium lies left" earns only 2 of 3.)

## Opgave C — glyphosate (Q12–Q17)

**Subdomains:** C05 (K_z, protolysed fractions), C01 (redox classification), G01/B01 (DNA codon mutations), C02 (hydration/yield stoichiometry). **BINAS:** codon table (71G), molar masses (DSIDA ≈ 177).

### Q12 (4p) — % glyphosate as HGlyf⁻ at pH 7.00
**Solution:** [H₃O⁺] = 1.0·10⁻⁷. With K_z = [Glyf²⁻][H₃O⁺]/[HGlyf⁻]: ratio [Glyf²⁻]/[HGlyf⁻] = 2.5·10⁻⁶/1.0·10⁻⁷ = 25. So per 26 parts, 1 is HGlyf⁻: **3.8%** present as HGlyf⁻ (the rest Glyf²⁻).

### Q13 (1p) — formula of particle X in the EPSPS reaction
**Solution:** the enolpyruvyl group moves from PEP onto S3P; the rest of PEP leaves as the phosphate: X = HPO₄²⁻.

### Q14 (2p) — a possible structural formula of PEP-H⁺
**Solution:** H⁺ adds to the C=C of PEP: protonate the terminal CH₂ carbon so the positive charge sits at the other double-bond carbon (carbocation ~[CH₂]–C⁺–...). Draw the full structure with the + on the secondary carbon and one C without a complete octet explicitly visible, all other atoms as in PEP.

### Q15 (2p) — the differing base pair for CP4 vs EPSPS
**Solution:** alanine ↔ glycine codons: GCN vs GGN: one point mutation at the middle base: coding strand CP4: C; EPSPS: G (complementary: matrijs G vs C). (If the sheet also asks the number: amino acid 100 → base pair 3×99+2 = 299.)

### Q16 (2p) — DEA → DSIDA: acid-base or redox?
**Solution:** H₂ gas is formed from OH⁻-containing medium — a change in oxidation numbers (hydrogen reduced from H(+I) to H(0), the carbon carries the missing electrons): so it is a **redox reaction**, not acid-base (no proton is transferred: OH⁻ does not end up as H₂O).
**Common mistakes:** calling any reaction with OH⁻ acid-base (a reagent class isn't the answer class).

### Q17 (4p) — kg of NaOH per batch for 8.3·10³ kg DSIDA at 86.4% yield
**Solution:** target DSIDA (as produced) = 8.3·10³ kg → n = 8.3·10⁶ g/177 g mol⁻¹ = 4.69·10⁴ mol. Per figure 2, per DSIDA : NaOH 1 : 2 → 9.38·10⁴ mol. Mass: ×40.0 g mol⁻¹ = 3.75·10³ kg. With the 86.4% yield (more input needed): ÷ 0.864 = **4.3·10³ kg NaOH** per batch.

## Opgave D — lithium-air battery (Q18–Q25)

**Subdomains:** C01 (cells, electrodes, V₀-ish reasoning), B03/B01 (charge density, resonance, radicals), G01 (protein binding), D03 (copolymers), C06/F03 (energy). **BINAS:** atomic masses (Li 6.94, Al 26.98), protein/amino acid knowledge, the given ΔH_f(Li₂O₂) = −6.43·10⁵ J mol⁻¹.

### Q18 (2p) — which of the two has the highest charge density: Al-air or Li-air?
**Solution:** lithium: 1 e⁻ per 6.94 g = 0.144 mol e⁻ g⁻¹; aluminium: 3 e⁻ per 26.98 g = 0.111 mol e⁻ g⁻¹ → **lithium** has the higher charge density per mass of reductor.

### Q19 (2p) — is the lithium electrode positive or negative during discharge?
**Solution:** half-reaction 1: Li → Li⁺ + e⁻, an oxidation (lithium is the reductor; electrons are produced there): **negative electrode.**

### Q20 (2p) — resonance structure of O₂⁻ + why so reactive
**Solution:** draw ·O–O⁻: one O with a single bond, 3 lone pairs, charge −1, the other O with one lone pair + one unpaired electron (radical: an odd number of electrons). Reactive because of the unpaired electron — radical character: it attacks solvent and membrane.

### Q21 (3p) — which chain binds more Mn²⁺ at higher pH, A: ~…KAAF…N~ or B: ~…EAAF…E~ (my paraphrase: A keeps a basic lysine K and neutral N; B swaps them for two acidic glutamic acids E)
**Solution:** at higher pH the acidic side chains (E, and D) donate their H⁺: they become negatively charged carboxylates. Peptide chain B carries more of these acidic side chains, hence more negative charge to attract the positive Mn²⁺ (ion bonds): **B binds more**.

### Q22 (2p) — the charge of the shown ion (imidazolium-type)
**Solution:** the ring N with four single bonds (to three carbons + an ethyl) obeys the octet with four bonding pairs: formal charge of N = 5 − 0 − 4 = +1; no other atom carries formal charge: the ion's charge is **1+**.

### Q23 (3p) — draw part of a PVDF-HFP molecule (one unit of each monomer)
**Solution:** monomers: 1,1-difluoroethene CH₂=CF₂ and hexafluoropropene CF₂=CF–CF₃. Addition product across the double bonds: –(CH₂–CF₂)–(CF₂–CF(CF₃))– drawn as four backbone carbons: CH₂–CF₂–CF₂–C(F)(CF₃)– with continuation marks ~ on both ends.

### Q24 (2p) — can PVDF-HFP be shaped by melt + moulding?
**Solution:** it is an addition copolymer without crosslinks and without reactive side groups → a thermoplastic: it softens on heating: **yes**, melt + injection moulding works (as long as the heating stays below decomposition — no atomic-bond breaking needed).

### Q25 (4p) — maximum useful energy from the battery with 45 g lithium
**Solution:** n(Li) = 45/6.94 = 6.48 mol; n(Li₂O₂) = 6.48/2 = 3.24 mol; chemical energy = 3.24 × 6.43·10⁵ = 2.08·10⁶ J; useful = ×0.70 = **1.5·10⁶ J**.
