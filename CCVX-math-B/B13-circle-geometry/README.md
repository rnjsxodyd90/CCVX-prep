# B13 — Circle geometry

- **Dutch:** *meetkunde in cirkels*
- **CCVX syllabus:** programme points 13 and 14, and the section *meetkunde in cirkels* of the concept list.

## Learning outcomes (CCVX syllabus)

The candidate can:

1. Use the centre equation of a circle (*middelpuntvergelijking*) with centre M(a, b) and radius r: (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r², and the expanded form x² + y² + px + qy + s = 0; move from the first to the second by expanding brackets and back by completing the square (*kwadraat afsplitsen*).
2. Compute intersections of a circle and a line by substitution, which gives a quadratic equation with 0, 1 or 2 solutions.
3. Use tangents (*raaklijnen*) and tangent points (*raakpunten*): a tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius to the tangent point, so (slope of tangent) × (slope of radius) = −1.
4. Compute intersections of two circles: eliminating x² + y² from the two equations gives the equation of the line through the intersection points.
5. Use touching circles (*rakende cirkels*): the common tangent is perpendicular to the line through the centres.
6. Use tangents from a point P outside the circle: the tangent points are at equal distances from P.
7. Compute the distance between two circles via the line through the centres, and the distance between a line and a circle via the perpendicular from the centre to the line.
8. Use circles and triangles: the circumscribed circle (*omgeschreven cirkel*) passes through the three vertices, its centre being the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the sides; the inscribed circle (*ingeschreven cirkel*) touches the three sides, its centre being the intersection of the angle bisectors.
9. Apply the theorem of Thales (*stelling van Thales*): a triangle of which one side is a diameter of the circumscribed circle is right-angled; and the converse: in a right-angled triangle, the midpoint of the hypotenuse is the centre of the circumscribed circle.
