# B10 — Practice questions with solutions

Eight original practice questions, written for this prep. Nothing is copied from CCVX or CvTE exams. A full worked solution follows every question.

## Q1 — intercept form

A line passes through (5, 0) and (0, −3). Give its equation in the form y = ax + b.

**Solution.** Intercept form: x/5 + y/(−3) = 1 ⇔ 3x − 5y = 15 ⇔ y = (3/5)x − 3. Check both points: (5, 0): 3 − 3 = 0 ✓; (0, −3) ✓.

**Answer:** y = (3/5)x − 3

## Q2 — intersection of two lines

Compute the intersection of l: 2x + 3y = 12 and m: x − y = 1.

**Solution.** From m: y = x − 1. Substitute: 2x + 3(x − 1) = 12 ⇔ 5x = 15 ⇔ x = 3, y = 2. Check: 6 + 6 = 12 ✓.

**Answer:** (3, 2)

## Q3 — parallel or perpendicular?

For each pair, state whether the lines are parallel, perpendicular or neither:
- l₁: y = ⅔x + 1 and l₂: 2x − 3y = 5;
- l₁ and l₃: 6x + 4y = 1.

**Solution.** l₂: 3y = 2x − 5 → slope ⅔: equal to l₁'s slope, so **parallel** (distinct lines: at x = 0, l₂ gives y = −5/3 ≠ 1). l₃: 4y = −6x + 1 → slope −3/2; product with l₁: (2/3)(−3/2) = −1, so **perpendicular**.

**Answer:** l₁ ∥ l₂; l₁ ⟂ l₃

## Q4 — distance between two points

Compute |AB| for A(−2, 1) and B(4, 8), exactly and to 2 decimals.

**Solution.** Δx = 6, Δy = 7: |AB| = √(36 + 49) = √85 ≈ 9.22.

**Answer:** √85 ≈ 9.22

## Q5 — projection and minimal distance

Find the foot Q of the perpendicular from P(3, 4) to l: y = x − 1 (the projection), and the distance from P to l.

**Solution.** Perpendicular slope: −1; through P: y = −x + 7. Intersect: x − 1 = −x + 7 ⇔ x = 4, y = 3: Q(4, 3). Distance: √((4−3)² + (3−4)²) = √2 ≈ 1.41.

**Answer:** Q(4, 3); d = √2 ≈ 1.41

## Q6 — direction angles and the angle between lines

l: y = √3·x + 2 and m: y = (√3/3)x − 1.

a. Give both direction angles.

b. Compute the angle between l and m.

**Solution.** a. tan α = √3 → α = 60°; tan β = √3/3 → β = 30°. b. Difference: 60° − 30° = 30° < 90°, so the angle between the lines is 30°.

**Answer:** a. 60° and 30°; b. 30°

## Q7 — perpendicular bisector

Give the equation of the perpendicular bisector of A(0, 0) and B(6, 2).

**Solution.** Midpoint: (3, 1). Slope AB = 2/6 = 1/3, so the bisector has slope −3: y = −3(x − 3) + 1 = −3x + 10. Check any point on it, e.g. x = 2, y = 4: |PA|² = 4 + 16 = 20; |PB|² = 16 + 4 = 20 ✓.

**Answer:** y = −3x + 10

## Q8 — angle bisector pair

Compute the equations of both angle bisectors of l: x + y = 2 and m: x − y = 2.

**Solution.** First method, distances: P(x, y) is a bisector point when d(P, l) = d(P, m), i.e. |x + y − 2|/√2 = |x − y − 2|/√2 ⇔ x + y − 2 = ±(x − y − 2). Plus sign: 2y = 0 → y = 0. Minus sign: 2x − 4 = 0 → x = 2. Second method, direction vectors (check): l has direction (1, −1)/√2, m has (1, 1)/√2; they meet at (2, 0) (solve the system). Sum of unit directions: (2,0)∝(1,0) → the bisector y = 0; difference: (0,−2)∝(0,1) → the bisector x = 2. Both methods agree.

**Answer:** y = 0 and x = 2
