# A3 — Power and quadratic relations

- **Dutch:** *machtsverbanden en tweedegraadsverbanden*
- **CCVX syllabus:** programme points 4 and 5, and the section *machtsverbanden* of the concept list.

## Learning outcomes (CCVX syllabus)

The candidate can recognise and use the characteristics of quadratic functions and power functions in graph, table and formula. In detail:

1. Identify the power relation y = a·xⁿ in tables (x multiplied by k gives y multiplied by kⁿ) and set up the formula from data.
2. Describe the graph: for even n a vertex in (0,0) and line symmetry; for odd n the point (0,0) as centre of symmetry; describe end behaviour from n and the sign of a.
3. Apply the transformations to y = xⁿ: horizontal shift, vertical shift and vertical scaling, e.g. y = 3(x − 4)² + 5 from y = x², a *dalparabool* with vertex (4, 5); read the vertex (p, q) from y = a(x − p)² + q in both directions.
4. Analyse the quadratic function f(x) = ax² + bx + c: parabola opening upwards (*dalparabool*) for a > 0, downwards (*bergparabool*) for a < 0; axis of symmetry x = −b/(2a); vertex as minimum/maximum; intersections with the x-axis from the quadratic formula (formula sheet).
5. Solve quadratic equations exactly (factoring when possible, otherwise the abc-formula) and use them to find intersections between a parabola and a line or between two parabolas.
6. Solve quadratic inequalities by first computing the intersection points algebraically, then sketching.
7. Set up quadratic and power formulas from contexts (e.g. area-price, physics braking distance ∝ speed², cube scaling) and interpret outcomes in context.
