### matha-a03-q01 | points 2 | outcome o3 | difficulty easy

#### Question

Give the vertex, the opening direction and the range of y = −2(x + 1)² + 6.

#### Answer

Bergparabool with vertex (−1, 6); range y ≤ 6

#### Solution

Read off the form a(x − p)² + q: vertex (p, q) = (−1, 6). Since a = −2 < 0 it is a *bergparabool* (opens downward): the maximum value is 6, so the range is y ≤ 6.

### matha-a03-q02 | points 4 | outcome o3 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A parabola has vertex (1, 2) and passes through (3, 10). Give its formula, in vertex form and expanded.

#### Answer

y = 2(x − 1)² + 2 = 2x² − 4x + 4

#### Solution

y = a(x − 1)² + 2. Substitute (3, 10): 10 = 4a + 2 ⇔ a = 2. So y = 2(x − 1)² + 2; expanding: 2x² − 4x + 2 + 2 = 2x² − 4x + 4. Check: (3, 10): 18 − 12 + 4 = 10 ✓.

### matha-a03-q03 | points 3 | outcome o5 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Solve exactly: 3x² + 5x − 2 = 0.

#### Answer

x = 1/3 ∨ x = −2

#### Solution

Quadratic formula (formula sheet): D = 25 + 24 = 49, x = (−5 ± 7)/6, so x = 2/6 = 1/3 or x = −12/6 = −2. (Factor check: (3x − 1)(x + 2) = 0 gives the same.)

### matha-a03-q04 | points 4 | outcome o5 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Compute the intersections of the parabola y = x² − 2x − 3 and the line y = x + 1.

#### Answer

(−1, 0) and (4, 5)

#### Solution

x² − 2x − 3 = x + 1 ⇔ x² − 3x − 4 = 0 ⇔ (x − 4)(x + 1) = 0 ⇔ x = 4 or x = −1. Then y = x + 1 gives the points (−1, 0) and (4, 5).

### matha-a03-q05 | points 3 | outcome o6 | difficulty medium

#### Question

Solve: x² + 3x − 10 ≥ 0.

#### Answer

x ≤ −5 ∨ x ≥ 2

#### Solution

Zeros: (x + 5)(x − 2) = 0 ⇔ x = −5 or x = 2. The parabola is a *dalparabool*, so it lies at or above zero outside its zeros (boundaries included for ≥): x ≤ −5 or x ≥ 2.

### matha-a03-q06 | points 4 | outcome o1 | difficulty medium

#### Question

A cubic open tank has inner edge s (in metres). The inside (4 walls plus the floor) is painted; paint costs €1.50 per m².

a. Show that the cost satisfies C(s) = 7.5 s².

b. For which edge is the cost exactly €36? (Exact value.)

#### Answer

a. C(s) = 7.5 s²; b. s = ⅖√30 ≈ 2.19 m

#### Solution

a. Area painted: 5 faces of s², so 5s² m²; at €1.50 per m²: C = 1.5 · 5s² = 7.5 s². b. 7.5 s² = 36 ⇔ s² = 36/7.5 = 4.8 ⇔ s = √4.8 (positive root). Simplify: 4.8 = 24/5, so s = √(24/5) = (2√6)/√5 = 2√30/5 = ⅖√30 ≈ 2.19 m.

### matha-a03-q07 | points 3 | outcome o1 | difficulty easy

#### Question

A table of a power relation y = a·xⁿ gives: x = 1 → y = 2; x = 2 → y = 16; x = 3 → y = 54.

a. Give the formula.

b. Compute y at x = 5.

#### Answer

a. y = 2x³; b. 250

#### Solution

a. Test the power idea: y/x³ is 2, 2, 2 — constant, so n = 3 and a = 2: y = 2x³. (Ratio check on x doubling: ×2³ = ×8, and 2×8 = 16 ✓.) b. y = 2·125 = 250.

### matha-a03-q08 | points 4 | outcome o7 | difficulty hard

#### Question

A farmer fences a rectangular meadow against a straight wall, using 40 m of fence for the three open sides (two widths x and one depth d).

a. Express the area A as a function of the width x.

b. Compute the maximal area exactly.

#### Answer

a. A(x) = −2x² + 40x (0 < x < 20); b. 200 m²

#### Solution

a. Fence: 2x + d = 40 ⇔ d = 40 − 2x. Area: A = x·d = x(40 − 2x) = −2x² + 40x. b. *Bergparabool*: vertex at x = −40/(2·(−2)) = 10. Then d = 20 and A = 10·20 = 200 m² (vertices of parabolas give the maximum — check the meaningful domain 0 < x < 20 ✓).

### matha-a03-q09 | points 4 | outcome o5 | difficulty hard

#### Question

Compute exactly the intersections of the parabolas y = x² − 1 and y = 2 − x².

#### Answer

(½√6, ½) and (−½√6, ½)

#### Solution

x² − 1 = 2 − x² ⇔ 2x² = 3 ⇔ x² = 3/2 ⇔ x = ±√(3/2) = ±(√6/2) = ±½√6. Then y = x² − 1 = 3/2 − 1 = ½. Points: (½√6, ½) and (−½√6, ½).

### matha-a03-q10 | points 4 | outcome o4 | difficulty hard

#### Question

For which values of k does the parabola y = x² + kx + 9 touch the x-axis? Give the touching point for each k.

#### Answer

k = 6 at (−3, 0); k = −6 at (3, 0)

#### Solution

Touching means D = 0: D = k² − 36 = 0 ⇔ k = ±6. For k = 6: y = x² + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)², touching at (−3, 0). For k = −6: y = (x − 3)², touching at (3, 0).
